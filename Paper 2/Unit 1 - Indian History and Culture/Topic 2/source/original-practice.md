# Original Practice — NOT Previous-Year Questions

## MCQ 1
Which term most precisely describes a Sufi spiritual lineage?
A. Dargah  B. Silsila  C. Iqta  D. Sabha

**Answer: B.** A silsila denotes a lineage of spiritual transmission. A dargah is a shrine; iqta is an administrative/revenue assignment; sabha is an assembly term in a different context.

## MCQ 2
Which statement best reflects the diversity of Bhakti traditions?
A. All Bhakti groups rejected temples.
B. All Bhakti poetry was composed in Sanskrit.
C. Traditions varied in theology, language and social practices.
D. Bhakti began as a centrally administered medieval organization.

**Answer: C.** The other choices falsely impose uniformity on distinct regional traditions.

## Mains practice 1 — Original
**Question:** Examine the contribution and limitations of Bhakti and Sufi traditions to cultural interaction in medieval India. (Practice length: approximately 250 words.)

### Answer blueprint
- Define the traditions without treating either as uniform.
- Discuss historical setting and circulation through vernacular language, institutions and patronage.
- Compare ethical emphases and social outreach.
- Explain regional and doctrinal differences, limits of egalitarian practice and continued conflict.
- Conclude with qualified assessment of cultural exchange.

### Model answer
Bhakti and Sufi traditions shaped medieval Indian cultural life through varied devotional communities, literary expression and institutions. They were not single organizations: Bhakti encompassed distinct regional Shaiva, Vaishnava and other currents, while Sufi networks developed through different spiritual lineages.

Devotional poetry in regional languages widened the audiences for religious ideas. Some Bhakti poets criticized status hierarchy and empty ritual; Sufi centres often provided spaces for instruction, hospitality and interaction. Shared imagery of love and ethical cultivation sometimes facilitated conversation across communities. Pilgrimage, patronage and urban exchange also linked religious activity to wider social and economic networks.

Nevertheless, similarities should not obscure theological differences or institutional variation. Some traditions remained closely associated with established temples or political patrons; others emphasized renunciation or social critique. Criticism of caste did not automatically remove caste inequalities. Relations among religious groups could involve contestation as well as exchange.

Their historical importance therefore lies not in creating a uniform culture, but in enabling multiple, uneven pathways of communication and local adaptation. A balanced assessment recognizes both cultural creativity and the continuing limits imposed by social and political power.

# High-value syllabus revision and named evidence

## Battle–Treaty–Provision–Consequence: mandatory exam matrix
| War / political settlement | Treaty / date | Specific settlement and significance |
|---|---|---|
| First Anglo-Mysore War | **Treaty of Madras, 1769** | Mutual restitution of conquests and a defensive-assistance commitment; demonstrates Company vulnerability to Haidar Ali. |
| Second Anglo-Mysore War | **Treaty of Mangalore, 1784** | Broad restoration of conquests and prisoners after sustained Mysore resistance; not a British annexation settlement. |
| Third Anglo-Mysore War | **Treaty of Seringapatam, 1792** | Tipu Sultan surrendered about half his territory, paid indemnity and provided hostages as security; a major territorial and fiscal setback. |
| Fourth Anglo-Mysore War | **Fall of Seringapatam, 1799** | Tipu died; subsequent partition and Wodeyar restoration under British predominance. Do not invent a '1799 Treaty of Seringapatam' as the war-ending counterpart to 1792. |
| First Anglo-Maratha War | **Treaty of Salbai, 1782** | Broad restoration and recognition arrangements, settling the Company–Maratha conflict for a period. |
| Maratha political crisis | **Treaty of Bassein, 1802** | Peshwa Baji Rao II accepted subsidiary troops and constraints on foreign relations; provoked confederacy opposition and helped precipitate the Second Anglo-Maratha War. |
| First Anglo-Sikh War | **Treaty of Lahore, 9 March 1846** | Loss of Jalandhar Doab and indemnity; must be paired with the **Treaty of Amritsar (16 March 1846)** involving Gulab Singh and Kashmir, and the **Treaty of Bhairowal (December 1846)** strengthening British supervision of Lahore government. |
| Second Anglo-Sikh War | **Annexation of Punjab, 1849** | No interchangeable 'Treaty of Lahore 1849'; conflict ended in annexation under Dalhousie. |
| Bengal conquest | **Treaty of Allahabad, 1765** | Diwani of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa from Shah Alam II; transformed the Company's fiscal capacity. |

**Analytical instruction:** Each war answer must explain *why the treaty mattered*. The sequence is combat and negotiation → territory or indemnity → subsidiarity/paramountcy → fiscal resources → further coercive capacity. A simple list of battles misses the examination's causal core. Distinguish the historical documents from later national narratives and verify spellings/individual clauses from primary treaty compilations during final review.

# Quick Revision — British Colonial Expansion and Economic Transformation

## Military-political timeline

| Event | Date | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| First Carnatic War | 1746–48 | European rivalry becomes entangled with Indian politics |
| Battle of Plassey | 1757 | Company influence in Bengal through military action and elite defection |
| Battle of Wandiwash | 1760 | Decisive British victory over French forces in the Carnatic |
| Battle of Buxar | 1764 | Company defeats coalition of major regional powers |
| Bengal diwani | 1765 | Revenue rights create a fiscal-military base |
| Permanent Settlement | 1793 | Zamindari revenue demand fixed in Bengal |
| Fall of Tipu Sultan | 1799 | Fourth Anglo-Mysore War ends |
| Treaty of Bassein | 1802 | Peshwa accepts subsidiary alliance |
| Final Anglo-Maratha War | 1817–18 | Peshwa's political office abolished |
| Punjab annexation | 1849 | After Second Anglo-Sikh War |
| Bombay–Thane railway | 1853 | First passenger railway service |
| Awadh annexation | 1856 | Justified by British as misgovernment, not Doctrine of Lapse |

## Compare revenue systems

| System | Main assessment unit | Key issue |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent | Zamindar | Fixed state demand; tenants vulnerable to landlord exactions |
| Ryotwari | Individual cultivator | Direct assessment but periodic revision and high demands |
| Mahalwari | Village estate or mahal | Joint responsibility and periodic reassessment |

## Five analytical distinctions

1. **Plassey vs Buxar:** political leverage through elite conspiracy vs consolidated military authority against a coalition.
2. **Commercialization vs prosperity:** more cash-crop production need not raise real incomes.
3. **Craft decline vs all industry:** traditional sector contraction coexisted with later modern industrial growth.
4. **Railway capacity vs food security:** movement of grain does not guarantee poor people can afford it.
5. **Direct British India vs Hyderabad State:** do not apply provincial settlements mechanically to Telangana.

## Mains-ready causal chain

Company alliances and battles → fiscal rights → land and trade restructuring → uneven commercialization and industrial change → famines, resistance and economic nationalism.

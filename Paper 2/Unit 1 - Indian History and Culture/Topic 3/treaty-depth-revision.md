# Treaty-centred revision: British Colonial Expansion and Economic Transformation

## Why the settlements matter
Company expansion combined commercial finance, Indian political alliances, military force, fiscal access and diplomacy. A battle's historical consequence cannot be understood without studying the settlement that followed it.

## Mysore: four wars, distinct terms

### First Anglo-Mysore War — Treaty of Madras (1769)
Haidar Ali forced a negotiation after campaigning toward Madras. The agreement broadly restored pre-war positions and promised mutual defensive assistance. It did not make Mysore a British subsidiary state.

### Second Anglo-Mysore War — Treaty of Mangalore (1784)
After Haidar Ali died in 1782, Tipu Sultan continued resistance. The treaty provided for reciprocal restoration of captured possessions and release of prisoners, showing that Mysore still negotiated from a position of substantial sovereignty.

### Third Anglo-Mysore War — Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)
The coalition of Company, Hyderabad and Maratha forces forced Tipu to surrender approximately half his territory, pay **3 crore 30 lakh rupees** indemnity and furnish two sons as hostages pending performance. This inflicted territorial and fiscal losses while reducing options for a renewed coalition.

### Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799)
Tipu Sultan died defending Seringapatam. A reduced Wodeyar kingdom was restored under subsidiary dependency while territory was redistributed. **Do not confuse 1799 with the 1792 Treaty of Seringapatam.**

## Marathas: the subsidiary mechanism

The **Treaty of Salbai (1782)** ended the First Anglo-Maratha War. The **Treaty of Bassein (31 December 1802)** was far more transformative: Baji Rao II accepted a permanent British subsidiary force, territorial revenue to finance the garrison, restraints on employing rival European personnel and British influence over foreign dealings. The Company restored him in 1803, but the treaty deepened disagreement within the confederacy and helped precipitate the Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–05). The Peshwa's position was abolished after the Third War (1817–18).

## Punjab: three documents in 1846

**Treaty of Lahore (March):** Lahore ceded the Jalandhar Doab, faced a major indemnity, military restrictions and increasing British supervision. Treaty text demanded **1½ crore rupees**.

**Treaty of Amritsar (16 March):** a separate agreement with **Gulab Singh** concerning Kashmir territories in return for **75 lakh Nanakshahi rupees**, under British supremacy.

**Treaty of Bhairowal (December):** strengthened British administrative supervision of the Lahore Darbar during Dalip Singh's minority. **Punjab was annexed in 1849, not 1846.**

## Revenue and colonial economic consequences
- **Permanent Settlement (1793):** assessed liability through zamindars; cultivator security not guaranteed.
- **Ryotwari:** cultivator-based assessment; collection practices and debt conditioned outcomes.
- **Mahalwari:** village/estate-based assessment; collective liability and periodic revision in many locations.
- **Drain of wealth:** Naoroji and R.C. Dutt emphasised unrequited transfers and fiscal arrangements; quantitative historical estimates remain debated.
- **Railways:** commercial integration and public uses coexisted with extractive export patterns and easier military movement.

## Model-answer writing — mandatory subheadings

**Question:** Why did Bassein become a turning point in Maratha–Company relations?

### Background
Maratha authority was a confederation of the Peshwa and leading chiefs whose rivalries limited coordinated action.

### Immediate cause
After military defeat by Holkar in 1802, Baji Rao II sought British protection.

### Treaty provisions
Bassein imposed a subsidiary force, assigned revenues for its upkeep and limited the Peshwa's diplomatic and military independence.

### Consequences
British intervention restored the Peshwa but provoked hostility from other Maratha powers, contributing to the Second Anglo-Maratha War. This deepened Company political leverage; the Peshwa's position ended in 1818.

### Critical assessment
The agreement changed sovereignty through the language of protection: the Company secured strategic military access and influenced foreign relations even before subsequent battles.

### Conclusion
Bassein was not merely a peace treaty; it institutionalised dependency and destabilised the confederacy.

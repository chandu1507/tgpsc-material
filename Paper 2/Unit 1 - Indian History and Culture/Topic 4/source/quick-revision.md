# Quick revision — anti-British resistance and reform

- **1831–32:** Kol uprising in Chotanagpur; disputes over land, authority and outsiders.
- **1855–56:** Santhal Hul; Sidhu and Kanhu; resistance to exploitation and colonial institutions.
- **1857:** Meerut uprising on 10 May; Delhi became symbolic center; diverse regional leadership.
- **1858:** Company rule ended; authority transferred to Crown; Queen’s Proclamation.
- **1859–60:** Indigo revolt in Bengal; coercive contracts and peasant collective action.
- **1873:** Satyashodhak Samaj founded by Jyotirao Phule.
- **1875:** Arya Samaj founded; Deccan disturbances; MAO College founded at Aligarh.
- **1885:** Indian National Congress founded.
- **1899–1900:** Birsa Munda’s Ulgulan.
- **1908:** Chotanagpur Tenancy Act; part of longer tenure-rights history.
- **1916:** Justice Party political organization established; Annie Besant’s Home Rule activity.
- **1925:** Self-Respect Movement associated with Periyar.
- **1932:** Poona Pact: reserved seats within joint electorates.

## Distinguish in answers

**Trigger vs structural cause:** Enfield cartridge controversy vs annexation, military grievances and revenue politics in 1857.

**Peasant vs tribal movements:** Overlap in economic grievances; different claims to customary land, community authority and forest access.

**Social reform vs anti-caste transformation:** Changing specific practices does not always challenge the underlying caste hierarchy.

**Gandhi vs Ambedkar:** Both opposed untouchability, but differed sharply on caste, representation and political safeguards.

**Telangana caution:** Nizam-ruled Hyderabad State had distinct institutions; the Telangana armed peasant struggle is twentieth-century history.

# Visual learning and analytical diagrams

## Revolt-causation chain

Colonial revenue/property changes + customary rights erosion + debt/coercion + political exclusion → localized grievances → collective organization → protest or uprising → repression, negotiation and uneven legal change.

## 1857 multi-causal diagram

- **Political:** Annexations; displacement of rulers and taluqdars
- **Military:** Service conditions; allowances; cartridge controversy
- **Socio-religious:** Fears of interference and loss of customary practices
- **Economic:** Agrarian and elite grievances, differing by region
- **Outcome:** Crown rule (1858); army and administrative restructuring

## Comparison matrix

| Movement | Principal setting | Core issue | Key caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Santhal Hul | Damin-i-Koh | Debt, outsiders, official oppression | Not identical to 1857 |
| Indigo revolt | Bengal | Coercive crop contracts | Not primarily a sepoy revolt |
| Deccan disturbances | Western India | Debt and credit | Not a general anti-moneylender ethnicity claim |
| Ulgulan | Chotanagpur | Land rights and social renewal | Do not overstate direct legislative causation |
| Justice Party | Madras Presidency | Non-Brahmin representation | Different from Self-Respect movement |
| Self-Respect | Tamil region | Caste and gender hierarchy | Broader than electoral reform |

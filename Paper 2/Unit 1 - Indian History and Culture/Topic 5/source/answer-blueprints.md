# Answer-writing blueprints — Original mains practice

## Q1 (15 marks): Three phases

**Introduction:** Define the framework as a historiographical device, not successive self-contained movements.

**Body:** (1) 1885–1905 constitutional/economic critique; (2) 1905–1919 Swadeshi/assertive and Home Rule strategies; (3) 1919–47 Gandhian mass politics and wartime constitutional crisis; (4) continuing revolutionary, social and regional politics across each boundary; (5) changing participants and meanings of swaraj.

**Conclusion:** A useful sequence, but a plural movement, not linear evolution.

## Q2 (20 marks): Peasants, workers and Adivasis

**Introduction:** The movement was a federation of demands, not just elite Congress resolutions.

**Body:** (1) AIKS 1936 and land/rent/debt claims; (2) AITUC 1920, industrial labor and strikes; (3) forest rights and Rampa movement; (4) Telugu region: Hyderabad State and later Telangana struggle; (5) tensions with landlords, capitalists, Congress and competing left politics; (6) demonstrate local interpretations of national freedom.

**Conclusion:** Freedom meant redistribution of power as well as political sovereignty.

## Q3 (20 marks): Partition

**Introduction:** Independence and Partition were related but analytically distinct outcomes.

**Body:** (1) institutions of communal representation and political mobilization; (2) minority protections and divergent constitutional demands; (3) Lahore Resolution and League growth; (4) wartime conditions and 1946 Cabinet Mission failure; (5) communal violence and compressed 1947 timetable; (6) border uncertainty, refugee displacement and gendered violence; (7) reject a single-cause, inevitable-partition thesis.

**Conclusion:** Constitutional breakdown and escalating violence accompanied imperial withdrawal, with enduring human costs.

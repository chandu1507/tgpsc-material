# 30 Original Explained Practice MCQs — Paper II Unit I Topic 5

**Original practice, not official TGPSC PYQs.** Each explanation states the reasoning; distractors are intentionally plausible where possible.

### Q1. Why is the three-phase model of the freedom struggle a limited analytical tool?

A. It ignores all events after 1919

B. Constitutional and mass methods overlapped across periods

C. The Congress began only in 1905

D. Trade unions had no relation to nationalism

**Answer: B.** Political repertoires overlapped; the model helps revision but does not create sealed historical periods.

### Q2. Which demand best illustrates early nationalist scrutiny of colonial institutions?

A. Complete abolition of all trade

B. Indianisation of higher services and financial accountability

C. Immediate universal adult franchise in 1885

D. Dissolution of every provincial council

**Answer: B.** Early Congress leaders pressed administrative representation and budget scrutiny within limited constitutional openings.

### Q3. What was a major practical constraint on Swadeshi consumption?

A. All imported goods were illegal

B. Local supply and purchasing power varied

C. No traders participated

D. National schools were already universal

**Answer: B.** Boycott choices depended on household resources and access to substitutes.

### Q4. Which agreement combined Congress–League cooperation with communal representation?

A. Poona Pact 1932

B. Lucknow Pact 1916

C. Gandhi–Irwin Pact 1931

D. Cabinet Mission 1946

**Answer: B.** The Lucknow Pact exemplifies cooperation alongside the acceptance of separate political representation.

### Q5. Which date identifies the adopted AICC Quit India resolution?

A. 5 August 1942

B. 8 August 1942

C. 9 August 1945

D. 15 August 1947

**Answer: B.** The archive includes 5 August drafts, but the All India Congress Committee adopted the resolution on 8 August 1942.

### Q6. Where was the All India Kisan Sabha formed in 1936?

A. Karachi

B. Lucknow

C. Bombay

D. Hyderabad

**Answer: B.** The AIKS was constituted at Lucknow in April 1936.

### Q7. Who was the AIKS first president?

A. Lala Lajpat Rai

B. Swami Sahajanand Saraswati

C. M. N. Roy

D. Sarojini Naidu

**Answer: B.** Swami Sahajanand Saraswati led the initial national kisan organisation.

### Q8. Why did peasant organisers sometimes criticise nationalist landlords?

A. Nationalism abolished tenancy

B. Political independence alone would not remove rent and debt burdens

C. AIKS supported only industrial workers

D. Land revenue had ceased in the 1930s

**Answer: B.** Agrarian exploitation could continue under Indian intermediaries despite anti-colonial solidarity.

### Q9. Tebhaga is most closely associated with which demand?

A. Three-fifths of wages in Bombay mills

B. Two-thirds of crop share for Bengal cultivators

C. A separate Dominion for Hyderabad

D. Forest autonomy in the Agency areas

**Answer: B.** Tebhaga challenged produce-sharing arrangements for Bengal sharecroppers in 1946–47.

### Q10. Which distinction between Tebhaga and Telangana is historically sound?

A. Both occurred only in Hyderabad State

B. Tebhaga focused on Bengal sharecropping; Telangana also confronted vetti and Nizam-era structures

C. Both ended on 15 August 1947

D. Neither involved agrarian power

**Answer: B.** Their institutional settings and immediate grievances differed despite shared agrarian themes.

### Q11. What does vetti refer to in the Telangana agrarian context?

A. Free public education

B. Unpaid or coerced labour obligations

C. Voluntary cotton marketing

D. A constitutional assembly

**Answer: B.** Vetti denotes forms of unpaid compulsory labour opposed by village activists.

### Q12. Why is 1948 a turning point but not an endpoint in Telangana agrarian history?

A. The uprising began in 1952

B. Hyderabad integration occurred in September 1948, while the agrarian struggle continued

C. The British returned in 1948

D. All land disputes ended by decree

**Answer: B.** Political integration and the cessation of armed peasant struggle followed different timelines.

### Q13. Which labour organisation was founded in 1920?

A. AIWC

B. AITUC

C. AIKS

D. CSP

**Answer: B.** The All India Trade Union Congress created a national labour federation in 1920.

### Q14. Who was AITUC first president?

A. Lala Lajpat Rai

B. Jawaharlal Nehru

C. N. G. Ranga

D. Annie Besant

**Answer: A.** Lala Lajpat Rai presided over the founding AITUC conference.

### Q15. Why should labour strikes not automatically be equated with Congress campaigns?

A. Workers had no political views

B. Wage, recognition and workplace demands could differ from national constitutional priorities

C. All strikes were prohibited

D. Unions never collaborated with nationalists

**Answer: B.** Workplace bargaining and anti-colonial politics overlapped but were not identical.

### Q16. Which rebellion was led by Alluri Sitarama Raju in 1922–24?

A. Tebhaga

B. Rampa/Manyam

C. Ulgulan

D. Khilafat

**Answer: B.** The Rampa/Manyam movement arose in British-administered Agency areas amid forest and colonial grievances.

### Q17. What is a crucial jurisdictional distinction for Komaram Bheem?

A. He led the Bengal sharecroppers

B. His Gond-region struggle belongs to Hyderabad State, not the Rampa Agency

C. He drafted the 1947 Act

D. He founded the AIWC

**Answer: B.** Bheem is associated with the Hyderabad State's Adilabad/Gond context, unlike Alluri's Agency areas.

### Q18. Which movement preceded the three-phase Congress mass-politics period?

A. Birsa Munda Ulgulan, 1899–1900

B. Quit India, 1942

C. Tebhaga, 1946

D. Cabinet Mission, 1946

**Answer: A.** Birsa Munda’s resistance predates 1919 and shows why tribal histories cannot be fitted into a Congress-only chronology.

### Q19. Which organisation founded in 1927 pursued women's education and rights?

A. AIWC

B. AITUC

C. AIKS

D. Hindu College of 1817

**Answer: A.** The All India Women’s Conference developed its own reform and citizenship agenda.

### Q20. What is the key distinction between nationalist participation and gender emancipation?

A. They are always identical

B. Women could campaign for independence while still facing unequal rights

C. Women did not participate in picketing

D. Education reform ended in 1927

**Answer: B.** Nationalist activism did not automatically resolve property, education or representation inequalities.

### Q21. What was a central subject of the Child Marriage Restraint Act 1929?

A. Land tax remission

B. Age-related restrictions on child marriage

C. Trade union recognition

D. Separate electorates

**Answer: B.** The Sarda Act addressed child marriage; enforcement and social change remained separate questions.

### Q22. Which group formed in 1934 within the wider Congress arena?

A. Congress Socialist Party

B. All India Kisan Sabha

C. AITUC

D. Cabinet Mission

**Answer: A.** The CSP was founded in 1934 and connected anti-colonial struggle to socialist economic demands.

### Q23. Which pairing correctly captures two stages of Communist organisational history?

A. Tashkent 1920 and Kanpur 1925

B. Lucknow 1936 and Poona 1927

C. Bombay 1942 and Delhi 1947

D. Surat 1907 and Calcutta 1905

**Answer: A.** Historians distinguish early Tashkent networks from the Kanpur organisational consolidation.

### Q24. What changed the CPI wartime position after June 1941?

A. Formation of the AIWC

B. Germany’s invasion of the Soviet Union and the People’s War line

C. The Indian Independence Act

D. The 1905 Bengal partition

**Answer: B.** The German invasion of the USSR influenced the CPI’s anti-fascist wartime strategy and its non-endorsement of Quit India.

### Q25. Why is communalism not synonymous with ordinary religious practice?

A. All religious practice is political violence

B. Communalism organises political claims around supposedly uniform religious interests

C. Religious communities never entered politics

D. It began only after 1947

**Answer: B.** The concept concerns political representation and antagonistic group interests, not worship itself.

### Q26. Which reform introduced separate electorates for Muslims?

A. Indian Councils Act 1909

B. Indian Independence Act 1947

C. Government of India Act 1858

D. Regulating Act 1773

**Answer: A.** The Morley–Minto reforms of 1909 institutionalised separate Muslim electorates.

### Q27. What was the Cabinet Mission’s May 1946 constitutional objective?

A. To implement the final Radcliffe line

B. To propose a union/grouping framework that might avoid immediate Partition

C. To dissolve every princely state

D. To enact universal adult franchise overnight

**Answer: B.** The 16 May statement proposed a complex federal arrangement; disputes over grouping contributed to failure.

### Q28. What did section 1 of the Indian Independence Act 1947 establish?

A. Hyderabad’s accession

B. Two independent Dominions from 15 August 1947

C. The AIKS

D. A united Cabinet Mission federation

**Answer: B.** The enacted statute set the appointed day for India and Pakistan as 15 August 1947.

### Q29. Why is section 7 of the Indian Independence Act important for Hyderabad?

A. It automatically merged Hyderabad into India

B. It ended British paramountcy over princely states without automatically integrating them

C. It created the Singareni mines

D. It abolished all private land immediately

**Answer: B.** Lapse of paramountcy and subsequent political integration were legally distinct processes.

### Q30. Which is the best way to use disputed Partition casualty figures in an exam answer?

A. Present the highest number as certified

B. Distinguish uncertainty and avoid an unsupported exact total

C. Omit violence entirely

D. Attribute all deaths to one religious community

**Answer: B.** Historical estimates vary by method and source; careful analysis acknowledges uncertainty and avoids collective blame.

# High-value syllabus revision and named evidence

## Kakatiya ruler–inscription–architecture: high-scoring examples
**Rudradeva:** linked to early Kakatiya consolidation and the **Thousand Pillar Temple** at Hanamkonda, generally associated with the 12th century; temple patronage served political legitimation, Shaiva ritual and skilled labour. **Ganapati Deva:** consolidation of territory and commercial/agricultural networks, with local nayaka institutions; links to **Kakatiya Warangal urban development** should be made carefully rather than attributing every gateway to one ruler. **Rudrama Devi:** reigning sovereign who negotiated opposition from powerful chiefs; discussion must include **Warangal Fort**'s military and ritual landscape, **Kakatiya kala toranas** (ornamental gateways), and inscriptions and land-grant contexts, while distinguishing monuments built under the dynasty from those proven to be her personal commission. **Recherla Rudra**, a commander in the Ganapati Deva milieu, is identified with **Ramappa Temple** patronage; it is inaccurate to say Rudrama Devi personally built Ramappa. **Ramappa/Rudreshwara:** **Palampet**, construction began **1213 CE**, sandstone, carved dolerite figures, granite beams, lightweight porous 'floating' bricks and sandbox foundation; this structure records artisan innovation and performance iconography. **Jayapa Senani's Nrtta Ratnavali** provides a textual link to dance, though modern Perini revival is not straightforward evidence of its medieval performance continuity. **Prataparudra:** faced campaigns from Delhi, culminating in the 1323 fall of Warangal. **Velama centers:** **Rachakonda and Devarakonda** had their own distinct post-Kakatiya trajectories. **Sammakka–Sarakka** narratives arise from oral traditions and require clear separation of cultural memory from independently documented medieval chronology. **Sarvai Papanna** is much later (late 17th/early 18th century): do not merge his resistance with Kakatiya-era conflict.

**Verified 2026 conservation example:** UNESCO Decision **48 COM 7B.11** refers to Ramappa's historic canals and lake within an enlarged landscape-protection vision and calls for impact assessments before certain development projects. Source: https://whc.unesco.org/en/decisions/9068

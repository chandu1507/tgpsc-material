# Original practice - NOT official PYQs

## Practice mains 1: Evaluate the nature of British paramountcy over Hyderabad State. How did it affect the Nizam's sovereignty?

**Blueprint**

- Define paramountcy and distinguish it from direct rule.
- Explain subsidiary alliances, Resident and military dependence.
- Use the Hyderabad Contingent/Berar issue as fiscal-territorial evidence.
- Show continued internal administration.
- Conclude: layered sovereignty with shrinking strategic autonomy.

**Model answer**

British paramountcy over Hyderabad was an unequal relationship in which the Nizam retained extensive internal administration but lost much of the freedom normally associated with external sovereignty. It therefore differed from direct British rule in provinces such as the Madras Presidency. Treaties and the subsidiary alliance system placed British troops within the strategic structure of the state and restricted independent relations with other powers. The British Resident became an influential intermediary between the Nizam's court and imperial government. Military dependence also carried financial consequences. Disputes over the cost of forces maintained for Hyderabad's security contributed to the 1853 assignment of Berar and other territories to British management. Such arrangements show how imperial power could gain fiscal and territorial leverage without annexing the whole princely state. Nevertheless, Hyderabad maintained its own revenue departments, courts, public works, railways and educational institutions. The Nizam was therefore not merely a ceremonial district officer. The relationship is best understood as layered sovereignty: internal state-building continued, but diplomacy, defense and strategic finance operated under increasing British supervision. This distinction explains both the survival of Hyderabad as a princely state until 1948 and the limits within which Asaf Jahi rulers pursued modernization.

## Practice mains 2: Assess Salar Jung I's reforms as a process of modernization. Did they fundamentally transform Telangana society?

**Blueprint**

- Start with fiscal-administrative crisis in 1853.
- Explain district/revenue, judiciary, police, public works, education and communication changes.
- Identify bureaucratic centralization.
- Counterpoint: jagirdari/deshmukh power, vetti and rural inequality.
- Conclude: state-capacity modernization, incomplete social transformation.

**Model answer**

Salar Jung I's long tenure as Diwan from 1853 to 1883 marked a major phase of administrative modernization in Hyderabad State. His reforms were responses to fiscal weakness, fragmented authority and British pressure as much as expressions of abstract modernizing ideals. The government strengthened central departments, reorganized district and revenue administration, attempted more regular accounts, expanded judicial and police institutions, and supported public works, communications and education. These changes made offices increasingly part of a bureaucratic structure rather than solely extensions of individual nobles. Better roads, post and telegraph networks also increased the state's ability to communicate with distant districts. However, institutional modernization did not amount to social democratization. Jagirdari interests remained powerful, deshmukhs and other local elites could dominate village society, and coercive labour practices such as vetti persisted. Rural cultivators continued to face rent, debt and unequal access to authority. Political participation remained tightly limited. Salar Jung's achievement should therefore be evaluated as a strengthening and rationalization of the state, not as a complete transformation of Telangana society. His reforms created institutions later used by an expanding educated public, but many agrarian and social contradictions survived into the twentieth century.

## Practice mains 3: Discuss the socio-economic development of Hyderabad State under the sixth and seventh Nizams, with special reference to Telangana.

**Blueprint**

- Separate sixth and seventh reigns.
- Cover railways/communications, water, industries and education.
- Name Wadi-Secunderabad, Singareni, Osmania University and urban public works.
- Balance city modernization with rural inequality.
- Conclude with uneven development.

**Model answer**

The reigns of Mir Mahbub Ali Khan and Mir Osman Ali Khan witnessed significant expansion of the institutional and physical infrastructure of Hyderabad State. Under the sixth Nizam, railways, administrative departments, schools and urban services grew alongside an increasingly professional bureaucracy. The Wadi-Secunderabad line opened in 1874 and later railway expansion linked Hyderabad to wider markets. Coal production at Singareni became strategically important for transport and industry. Under the seventh Nizam, modernization accelerated through universities, hospitals, reservoirs, industries and monumental public buildings. Osmania University, established in 1918, created a distinctive Urdu-medium system of higher education and translation. Osman Sagar and Himayat Sagar strengthened Hyderabad's water supply and flood management after the 1908 Musi disaster. Industrial ventures in sugar, paper, textiles and mining broadened the non-agricultural economy. Yet these achievements were unevenly distributed. Hyderabad city benefited disproportionately, while much of rural Telangana continued to experience concentrated land power, jagirdari or deshmukh dominance, debt and vetti. Education remained unequal by region, caste, gender and class. The period should therefore be described as substantial but uneven modernization: state capacity and urban institutions expanded without removing the agrarian structures that later provoked mass political mobilization.

## Practice mains 4: Explain the historical significance of the 1857 Residency attack in Hyderabad and the role of Turre Baz Khan.

**Blueprint**

- Set Hyderabad within all-India 1857 context.
- Explain Nizam/Salar Jung support for British.
- Describe 17 July attack by Turre Baz Khan/Maulvi Alauddin.
- Assess scale carefully: significant resistance, not statewide takeover.
- Conclude with fragmented princely-state responses to 1857.

**Model answer**

Hyderabad's experience of 1857 demonstrates that the uprising cannot be understood only through territories under direct Company rule. The Nizam's government, led by Afzal-ud-Daula and supported administratively by Salar Jung I, chose to cooperate with the British. Strategic dependence under the subsidiary alliance system and concern about political instability shaped this policy. Yet the court's position did not eliminate anti-British resistance within Hyderabad. On 17 July 1857, an armed group led prominently by Turre Baz Khan and Maulvi Alauddin attacked the British Residency. The Residency's defenses held and the attempt did not develop into a successful state-wide overthrow. Turre Baz Khan was captured, later escaped, and was subsequently killed while being pursued. The episode is historically important because it reveals a divergence between princely-state policy and sections of popular or armed opposition. It also gives Telangana a specific connection to the wider 1857 revolt without requiring exaggerated claims about the scale of the action. Turre Baz Khan's later public memory reflects the symbolic importance of resistance to British authority. A balanced assessment therefore treats the attack as a significant local anti-colonial episode within a state whose ruling establishment remained aligned with the British.

# Practice MCQs - original questions, NOT official PYQs

### 1. The conventional political beginning of autonomous Asaf Jahi rule is associated with which event?

A. Battle of Shakar Kheda (1724)
B. Battle of Plassey (1757)
C. Annexation of Berar (1853)
D. Police Action (1948)

**Answer: A.** Asaf Jah I defeated Mubariz Khan at Shakar Kheda in 1724; the other events belong to different contexts.

### 2. Which statement best describes British paramountcy over Hyderabad?

A. Hyderabad became an ordinary British district
B. The Nizam retained internal administration but lost substantial external strategic autonomy
C. The Residency had no political influence
D. The Nizam controlled British foreign policy

**Answer: B.** Paramountcy constrained external and military sovereignty without converting the entire princely state into a directly ruled British province.

### 3. Salar Jung I is chiefly associated with which development?

A. Administrative and fiscal reorganization from the 1850s
B. Founding the Qutb Shahi dynasty
C. Leading the Residency attack of 1857
D. Abolishing the Nizamate in 1948

**Answer: A.** As Diwan from 1853 to 1883, Salar Jung I drove major administrative restructuring.

### 4. Why is vetti historically significant?

A. It was a railway tariff
B. It was a form of coercive/unpaid labour embedded in social hierarchy
C. It was the Nizam currency
D. It was a university department

**Answer: B.** Vetti involved unpaid compulsory service and became emblematic of oppressive agrarian relations.

### 5. Who were prominently associated with the 17 July 1857 attack on the British Residency in Hyderabad?

A. Turre Baz Khan and Maulvi Alauddin
B. Salar Jung I and the Nizam
C. Komaram Bheem and Ramji Gond
D. Rudrama Devi and Prataparudra

**Answer: A.** Turre Baz Khan and Maulvi Alauddin are associated with the Hyderabad Residency attack.

### 6. The Wadi-Secunderabad railway line is significant because it:

A. Opened in 1874 and connected Hyderabad State more closely with wider rail networks
B. Was the first railway in India in 1853
C. Connected only village tanks
D. Was constructed after 1948

**Answer: A.** The 1874 line formed an important link in the development of the Nizam's railway system.

### 7. Osmania University, established in 1918, was distinctive particularly for:

A. Using Urdu as a major medium of higher instruction
B. Banning scientific education
C. Operating only as a military academy
D. Being founded by the East India Company

**Answer: A.** Its Urdu-medium higher education and translation program made it a distinctive institutional experiment.

### 8. Which is the most defensible historical assessment of Asaf Jahi modernization?

A. Infrastructure growth automatically removed agrarian inequality
B. Institutional modernization coexisted with jagirdari power, vetti and limited political participation
C. There was no modernization at all
D. British paramountcy meant Hyderabad had no internal government

**Answer: B.** A balanced answer recognizes genuine institutional change alongside persistent social and political inequality.

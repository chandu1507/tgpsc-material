# Conceptual diagrams, cross-comparisons and exam visuals

## Structural map

```text
INDIAN SOCIETY
  ├── Differentiation: caste • class • tribe • gender • language • religion
  ├── Institutions: family • marriage • kinship • school • labour market
  ├── Integration: citizenship • trade • migration • cultural translation
  ├── Change: education • urbanisation • mobility • digital networks
  └── Tensions: exclusion • discrimination • unequal voice • displacement
```

## Difference versus inequality versus exclusion

| Concept | Test question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Difference | How are practices or identities unlike? | Telugu and Urdu language traditions |
| Inequality | Who has disproportionately more resources or status? | Unequal access to land or high-quality education |
| Exclusion | Who faces a barrier to participating? | A linguistic minority unable to access instruction |

## Three separate dimensions of social position

| Dimension | Core indicator | Thinker/concept |
|---|---|---|
| Caste status | Inherited jati ranking and endogamy | Ambedkar — graded inequality |
| Economic class | Land, income, occupation, assets | Béteille — caste, class and power |
| Political power | Ability to shape collective decisions | Srinivas — dominant caste |

## Exam contrast: family versus household

| Family | Household |
|---|---|
| Normative ties of kinship, care and descent | Shared residence and frequently consumption |
| Can span multiple places | Can contain unrelated members |
| Changes through marriage, inheritance, care | Changes through migration, housing and employment |

## Rural–urban continuum

Village production and care networks ⇆ migration / commuting / remittances ⇆ town-level trade and services ⇆ metropolitan labour and education.

**Limit:** This is a functional map, not a claim that all rural communities automatically urbanise in one direction.

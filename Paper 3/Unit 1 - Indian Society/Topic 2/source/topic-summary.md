# Topic summary — Social Exclusion and Vulnerable Groups

The central argument is that **social exclusion is structural, cumulative and institution-specific**. Poverty means lacking resources; exclusion means being denied fair access to opportunities, dignity, voice and institutions. The best answer traces a named chain of causes rather than announcing that vulnerable groups need welfare.

**SCs:** The constitutional status under Article 341 concerns communities historically affected by caste status and untouchability. Cite Ambedkar's *graded inequality*, Article 17, the Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955 and the SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989. The issue is not only reservation but safe schools, land, dignified work, enforcement and meaningful mobility.

**STs:** Article 342 recognition is not equivalent to an undifferentiated rural poverty label. Secure land/forest tenure, identity, cultural rights, access to services and self-government are central. Link Fifth Schedule/Article 244(1), PESA 1996 and FRA 2006. Distinguish community forest resource rights from individual cultivation/habitation rights. Telangana cases should differentiate Koya, Gond, Kolam and Chenchu contexts.

**BC/OBCs:** These categories concern social and educational backwardness, not just income poverty. Mandal Commission 1979, 1990 implementation debate and Indra Sawhney 1992 establish the background. NCBC gained constitutional status under the 102nd Amendment, 2018; 105th Amendment, 2021 is crucial for the relationship between state lists and the Central List.

**Minorities:** Article 29(1) protects sections with distinct language, script or culture; Article 30(1) concerns religious/linguistic minority educational institutions. Avoid equating religious identity with economic disadvantage. Language access, secure housing, fair policing and institutional trust matter. Hyderabad's multilingual context is a useful applied case.

**Women and children:** Highlight unequal unpaid care work, asset control, violence and mobility. Distinguish Domestic Violence Act, 2005 from POSH Act, 2013. For children differentiate RTE/Article 21A for 6–14, POCSO 2012, JJ Act 2015, child marriage law and child labour protections. Children's protection and women's autonomy are related but distinct questions.

**Senior citizens and persons with disabilities:** The Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 addresses maintenance and other legal protections. Disability policy uses a social/rights-based model; RPwD Act 2016 gives, subject to conditions, **4% government employment** reservation and **5% government/government-aided higher-education seats** for persons with benchmark disabilities. Physical and digital accessibility are key outcomes.

**Telangana current evidence:** The State's SEEEPC survey volumes appeared on the Directorate of Economics and Statistics site in April 2026. Check the definitions, survey coverage and publication version before inserting specific percentages. A May 2026 national disability-law schedule amendment notice is another current legal development, but its detailed impact must be checked in the gazette before reciting it.

**One-sentence conclusion:** Inclusion requires **enforceable equal rights + accessible public capabilities + representation and productive agency**, with implementation measured by real service use, dignity and opportunity rather than programme counts alone.

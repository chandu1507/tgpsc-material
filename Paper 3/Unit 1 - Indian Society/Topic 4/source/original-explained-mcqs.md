# 30 original explained practice MCQs
**Original practice questions; NOT authenticated TGPSC PYQs.**

## 1. Which constitutional provision directly prohibits begar and comparable forced labour?
A. Article 23
B. Article 356
C. Article 30
D. Article 19(1)(a)
**Answer: A.** Article 23 prohibits forced labour; Article 24 separately addresses child employment in factories, mines and hazardous employment.

## 2. What is the central feature distinguishing Vetti from merely low-paid work?
A. Payment by the day
B. Agricultural setting
C. Absence of meaningful freedom to refuse imposed service
D. Seasonal timing
**Answer: C.** Vetti refers to coerced service embedded in local social power; not all low wages meet the same test.

## 3. Which Telangana historical episode prominently challenged forced agrarian service?
A. Quit India movement of 1942 alone
B. The 1956 linguistic reorganisation alone
C. The 2014 state election alone
D. Telangana peasant mobilisation of 1946–51
**Answer: D.** The peasant struggle challenged forced service, landlord levies and tenancy coercion, while later legal reforms are distinct events.

## 4. Which statute provides a specific legal framework for abolishing bonded labour?
A. Companies Act, 2013
B. Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976
C. Indian Forest Act, 1927
D. Official Languages Act, 1963
**Answer: B.** The Act abolishes bonded labour (section 4) and expressly names Vetti in the explanation to section 2(b). Other options do not provide that framework; the statutory mention does not establish facts of any individual case.

## 5. Why is rehabilitation needed after release from coercive labour?
A. Dependence and threats can reproduce coercion
B. Release cancels all poverty automatically
C. Workers must return to recruiters
D. Rehabilitation is only a police duty
**Answer: A.** Safe livelihoods, wages and housing are needed to make legal freedom sustainable.

## 6. Which regional practice is explicitly included in the 1988 Devadasis prohibition statute?
A. PESA
B. Panchayat Samiti
C. Jogini
D. Gram Sabha
**Answer: C.** The official Telangana-adapted text lists Jogini among labels covered by the definition.

## 7. What is the legal status of dedication under section 3 of the 1988 Act?
A. Unlawful and void even when consent is alleged
B. Valid with parental permission
C. Only prohibited in cities
D. Valid after adulthood
**Answer: A.** Section 3 rejects dedication regardless of purported consent and protects the woman’s legal status.

## 8. Which approach best protects a survivor of ritual dedication?
A. Confidential protection, legal action and sustained livelihood support
B. Punishment of the dedicated woman
C. A ceremony without follow-up
D. Public identification at a village meeting
**Answer: A.** Survivor autonomy and safety are the outcomes; public exposure can create further harm.

## 9. Why should Devadasi history not be treated as one uniform institution?
A. Only one region used the term
B. All participants had identical legal status
C. Historical roles varied by place and period
D. No historical performance tradition existed
**Answer: C.** A rigorous account distinguishes historical performance traditions from contemporary coercive dedication.

## 10. Which official document establishes the adaptation of the 1988 Act for Telangana?
A. The 1973 Six-Point Formula
B. The 2009 December statement
C. G.O.Ms.No.14 dated 28 April 2016
D. G.O.610 of 1985
**Answer: C.** The Telangana-adapted India Code text identifies the 2016 adaptation under the Reorganisation Act.

## 11. Which statement best distinguishes fluorosis exposure from a clinical diagnosis?
A. One elevated fluoride reading proves every resident has skeletal fluorosis.
B. Prolonged excessive fluoride exposure can cause dental and skeletal fluorosis; individual diagnosis also needs clinical and exposure assessment.
C. Fluorosis is caused by iodine deficiency and is prevented solely by iodised salt.
D. Excess fluoride in groundwater necessarily proves bacterial contamination.
**Answer: B.** DGHS NPPCF describes dental, skeletal and non-skeletal manifestations of excessive fluoride exposure. A confuses a water-quality hazard with individual diagnosis; C confuses fluoride toxicity with iodine-deficiency disorders; D conflates a chemical contaminant with microbiological testing. Sources C182-1 (DGHS NPPCF) and C182-2 (Nalgonda District Fluoride Monitoring). This is an original practice item, not an authenticated TGPSC PYQ.

## 12. What can responsibly be concluded from Nalgonda district's official fluoride-monitoring account?
A. Every groundwater source in Nalgonda remains above the fluoride limit in November 2026.
B. The district monitoring centre regulates drinking-water standards throughout India.
C. The district has documented historical groundwater-fluoride risk and a coordination response; current household exposure requires contemporary source-level testing.
D. Any piped connection by itself establishes that fluorosis has been eliminated.
**Answer: C.** The district administration documents affected habitations and its monitoring response, but those historical figures do not measure every present-day source. A generalises beyond the evidence and time period; B mistakes a district coordination centre for a national standards authority; D confuses infrastructure provision with water quality and actual household use. Source C182-2 (Nalgonda district government). No 2026 district-wide prevalence claim is made.

## 13. What did Nalgonda district establish on 14 November 2013, according to its official account?
A. A national statute abolishing fluorosis.
B. The first national drinking-water quality standard.
C. The state of Telangana.
D. The District Fluoride Monitoring Centre for interdepartmental coordination.
**Answer: D.** The Nalgonda district government dates the District Fluoride Monitoring Centre (DFMC) to 14 November 2013 and describes its coordination function. A invents a statute; B mistakes a district administrative action for national standard-setting; C confuses the 2013 centre with Telangana state formation in 2014. Source C182-2 (Nalgonda district government). The date identifies the centre's reported establishment, not proven subsequent programme effectiveness.

## 14. Which comparison of drinking-water fluoride guidance is supported by the cited government sources?
A. NPPCF's desirable level is 1.0 mg/L; Jal Jeevan Mission's BIS-based table lists 1.5 mg/L as permissible in the absence of an alternate source.
B. NPPCF's desirable level is 1.5 mg/L and the permissible ceiling is 1.0 mg/L.
C. A reading at 1.5 mg/L guarantees that no resident can develop fluorosis regardless of exposure history.
D. The number of household taps is a substitute for fluoride concentration testing.
**Answer: A.** DGHS cites the 2014 NPPCF desirable level of 1.0 mg/L; the Department of Drinking Water and Sanitation's Jal Jeevan Mission document lists a BIS-based 1.0 mg/L acceptable level and 1.5 mg/L permissible level in the absence of an alternate source. B reverses the levels; C converts a water-quality threshold into an individual clinical guarantee; D mistakes access infrastructure for water-quality evidence. Sources C182-1 (DGHS NPPCF) and C182-3 (Jal Jeevan Mission concept note). The two limits have different labels and must not be conflated.

## 15. What is the strongest evidence that a piped-water scheme reduces fluorosis exposure?
A. Repeated testing of water actually consumed
B. Budget allocation alone
C. Ribbon-cutting photographs
D. Number of pipes installed alone
**Answer: A.** Functional safe delivery, household use and clinical surveillance test real impact.

## 16. A Child Marriage Prohibition Officer seeks a court order to prevent an arranged child marriage. Which provision specifically authorises a preventive injunction?
A. Section 5 of the Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009
B. Section 13 of the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006
C. Section 4 of the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976
D. Article 17 of the Constitution of India
**Answer: B.** Section 13 of the Prohibition of Child Marriage Act (PCMA) authorises a Judicial Magistrate of the first class or Metropolitan Magistrate, on the prescribed information and satisfaction, to prohibit an arranged or imminent child marriage by injunction. A concerns school transfers under the RTE Act, not marriage injunctions; C abolishes bonded labour, not child marriage; D is the constitutional abolition of untouchability, not the PCMA injunction power. This is a statutory power, not evidence that any specific injunction was granted. Source C183-1, India Code PCMA sections 13–14. Original practice question, NOT an official TGPSC PYQ.

## 17. Under the central Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006, which statement correctly distinguishes voidable and void marriages?
A. Every child marriage is automatically void from solemnisation solely because one party is below the statutory age.
B. No child marriage can be annulled unless a criminal conviction first occurs.
C. Section 3 generally makes child marriage voidable at the option of the party who was a child; section 12 specifies circumstances making a minor child's marriage void, and section 14 voids marriages solemnised in breach of an injunction.
D. Only the adult party can petition to annul a child marriage under section 3.
**Answer: C.** Section 3 gives the party who was a child the option to seek annulment in the competent district court, subject to statutory filing conditions; sections 12 and 14 create specified void categories. A wrongly converts the general voidability rule into automatic nullity; B invents a criminal-conviction prerequisite; D reverses who may petition. The India Code consolidation also displays a Karnataka-specific state amendment; do not silently apply that state rule to Telangana. Source C183-1, PCMA sections 2–3, 12–14. Original practice, not an authenticated PYQ.

## 18. Which metric best tests girls’ educational inclusion?
A. Sustained attendance, learning and safe access
B. Admission alone
C. Uniform distribution alone
D. School building count alone
**Answer: A.** Retention and meaningful access reveal burdens hidden by enrollment totals.

## 19. Under the Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986 as amended, which distinction is correct?
A. Children may work for any employer if the work occurs after school hours.
B. Every form of work by adolescents is prohibited, including non-hazardous work.
C. Children's employment is prohibited subject to narrow statutory exceptions; adolescents are prohibited from listed hazardous occupations and processes.
D. Family-enterprise work is exempt even when it is hazardous or interferes with schooling.
**Answer: C.** Section 3 states the child-employment prohibition and limited family/artist exceptions; section 3A prohibits adolescent employment in listed hazardous work. A wrongly generalises a narrow family/artist exception to any employer; B wrongly extends the adolescent prohibition to all work; D ignores the hazardous-work and school-time conditions. Source C181-1: India Code, Act 61 of 1986 as amended, sections 2, 3 and 3A. This describes statutory design, not proof of enforcement.

## 20. Which approach best prevents return to child labour after rescue?
A. Closing the case at first admission
B. Photographs of rescued children
C. A single raid
D. School reintegration with family livelihood support and follow-up
**Answer: D.** Rescue without educational and economic follow-through risks repeated exploitation.

## 21. What is a major risk in subcontracted labour supply chains?
A. Responsibility can be obscured across contractors
B. No worker can be recruited through agents
C. All subcontracting is illegal
D. All employers automatically keep complete records
**Answer: A.** Tracing recruitment, wage payment and the principal employer is necessary for accountability.

## 22. What is the correct analytical view of labour migration?
A. Mobility can be voluntary; exploitation arises from specific conditions
B. Every migrant is legally protected in practice
C. Every migrant is trafficked
D. Migration is a crime
**Answer: A.** Distinguish migration from coercive recruitment, wage theft and unsafe conditions.

## 23. Which statement correctly describes the central legal transition for inter-State migrant workers as of November 2026?
A. The 1979 Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act remains the sole operative central framework; the labour codes have not commenced.
B. The 2020 OSH Code protects only contractor-recruited migrants and categorically excludes self-migrated workers.
C. The 1979 Act is a historical framework replaced under the Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions Code, 2020, effective 21 November 2025; actual entitlements depend on statutory coverage and applicable rules.
D. Every migrant worker receives every statutory benefit automatically, regardless of the Code's definitions and coverage conditions.
**Answer: C.** The Ministry of Labour and Employment confirms 21 November 2025 commencement of the four codes. Section 143 of the OSH Code repeals the 1979 Act with savings, while the Code's inter-State migrant-worker provisions include defined categories of contractor-recruited and self-migrated workers. The applicable establishment threshold, Central or State rules, savings and facts of employment still matter. Sources: Ministry of Labour and Employment 30 December 2025 year-end review; Gazette G.S.R. 934(E), 30 December 2025 (a draft-rules notification recording the Code's commencement and repeal, not itself the final rules).

## 24. A 10-year-old child moves with a migrant-worker family from Telangana to another State. The previous school delays issuing the transfer certificate. Under section 5 of the RTE Act, 2009, which statement is correct?
A. Delay in producing the transfer certificate cannot by itself justify delaying or denying admission to an eligible receiving school for elementary education.
B. The receiving school may refuse admission until the family proves permanent residence in the destination State.
C. Section 5 allows transfer only between schools in the same State, not across State boundaries.
D. The receiving school must wait for a transfer certificate even when its issuance is delayed.
**Answer: A.** Section 5(2) covers transfers within a State or outside it, and the first proviso to section 5(3) prevents delayed transfer certificates from blocking admission. B adds a permanent-residence condition not in section 5; C contradicts the express interstate scope; D contradicts the proviso. This provision applies within the RTE Act's elementary-education framework and its specified school-category exclusions, not as a right to admission at any private school. Source C183-2, India Code RTE Act sections 2, 4–5 and 9(k). Original practice, not an official TGPSC PYQ.

## 25. Which region is strongly associated with the early-1990s mass anti-arrack literacy mobilisation?
A. Nellore in undivided Andhra Pradesh
B. Only Adilabad forests
C. Only Hyderabad Old City
D. Only Warangal city
**Answer: A.** The mass anti-arrack movement was prominently rooted in Nellore; northern Telangana had distinct earlier anti-liquor organising.

## 26. Why did women’s anti-arrack campaigns become a public-policy issue?
A. They opposed literacy
B. They excluded local government
C. They linked household harm and state excise decisions
D. They concerned only a single family
**Answer: C.** Collective mobilisation connected domestic burdens to licensing and state revenue policy.

## 27. A shop refuses service because of caste-based untouchability. Which constitutional Article directly abolishes the practice?
A. Article 17
B. Article 15(2)
C. Article 23
D. Article 24
**Answer: A.** Article 17 expressly abolishes untouchability and forbids its practice. B also protects against specified access discrimination, including shops, but does not itself contain the constitutional abolition clause; C prohibits trafficking, begar and forced labour; D addresses children's employment in factories, mines and hazardous work. Source C181-2: Constitution of India, Part III, Articles 15, 17, 23 and 24. The scenario is illustrative, not a finding in an actual case.

## 28. Which central law specifically defines atrocities against Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes and provides for Special Courts and victim/witness safeguards?
A. Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955
B. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989
C. Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996
D. Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976
**Answer: B.** The 1989 Act contains specified atrocity offences (section 3), Special Court provisions (section 14) and rights of victims and witnesses (section 15A). A addresses disabilities arising from untouchability but is not the named atrocities statute; C governs Panchayati Raj in Scheduled Areas; D abolishes bonded labour. Source C181-3: India Code, Act 33 of 1989, sections 3, 14 and 15A. Applicability to any alleged incident requires its facts and statutory elements.

## 29. In a Fifth Schedule Scheduled Area, which statute expressly requires the Gram Sabha to approve social and economic development plans before village-Panchayat implementation?
A. Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996 (PESA)
B. Forest Rights Act, 2006, by itself for every Panchayat development plan
C. The general 73rd Constitutional Amendment alone, without Scheduled-Area modifications
D. Sixth Schedule autonomous-district provisions as a blanket rule for Fifth Schedule villages
**Answer: A.** PESA section 4(e)(i) provides the specified Gram Sabha approval function; section 2 links its Scheduled Areas to Article 244(1). B concerns forest-rights recognition and related functions, not this general PESA approval clause; C omits PESA's tailored extension of Part IX; D conflates distinct Fifth- and Sixth-Schedule arrangements. Source C181-4: India Code, Act 40 of 1996, sections 2–4. Actual local implementation and State rules require separate checking.

## 30. What is the strongest test of a self-help group’s empowerment impact?
A. Number of meetings alone
B. Loan totals alone
C. Photographs of beneficiaries
D. Members’ agency, earnings control and inclusive decision-making
**Answer: D.** Credit disbursement may help, but substantive agency and reduced vulnerability are more meaningful outcomes.

# 30 original explained practice MCQs
**Original practice questions; NOT authenticated TGPSC PYQs.**

## 1. Which constitutional provision directly prohibits begar and comparable forced labour?
A. Article 23
B. Article 356
C. Article 30
D. Article 19(1)(a)
**Answer: A.** Article 23 prohibits forced labour; Article 24 separately addresses child employment in factories, mines and hazardous employment.

## 2. What is the central feature distinguishing Vetti from merely low-paid work?
A. Payment by the day
B. Agricultural setting
C. Absence of meaningful freedom to refuse imposed service
D. Seasonal timing
**Answer: C.** Vetti refers to coerced service embedded in local social power; not all low wages meet the same test.

## 3. Which Telangana historical episode prominently challenged forced agrarian service?
A. Quit India movement of 1942 alone
B. The 1956 linguistic reorganisation alone
C. The 2014 state election alone
D. Telangana peasant mobilisation of 1946–51
**Answer: D.** The peasant struggle challenged forced service, landlord levies and tenancy coercion, while later legal reforms are distinct events.

## 4. Which statute provides a specific legal framework for abolishing bonded labour?
A. Companies Act, 2013
B. Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976
C. Indian Forest Act, 1927
D. Official Languages Act, 1963
**Answer: B.** The 1976 Act addresses bonded labour obligations and associated debt, although the historical term Vetti is not an exact statutory synonym.

## 5. Why is rehabilitation needed after release from coercive labour?
A. Dependence and threats can reproduce coercion
B. Release cancels all poverty automatically
C. Workers must return to recruiters
D. Rehabilitation is only a police duty
**Answer: A.** Safe livelihoods, wages and housing are needed to make legal freedom sustainable.

## 6. Which regional practice is explicitly included in the 1988 Devadasis prohibition statute?
A. PESA
B. Panchayat Samiti
C. Jogini
D. Gram Sabha
**Answer: C.** The official Telangana-adapted text lists Jogini among labels covered by the definition.

## 7. What is the legal status of dedication under section 3 of the 1988 Act?
A. Unlawful and void even when consent is alleged
B. Valid with parental permission
C. Only prohibited in cities
D. Valid after adulthood
**Answer: A.** Section 3 rejects dedication regardless of purported consent and protects the woman’s legal status.

## 8. Which approach best protects a survivor of ritual dedication?
A. Confidential protection, legal action and sustained livelihood support
B. Punishment of the dedicated woman
C. A ceremony without follow-up
D. Public identification at a village meeting
**Answer: A.** Survivor autonomy and safety are the outcomes; public exposure can create further harm.

## 9. Why should Devadasi history not be treated as one uniform institution?
A. Only one region used the term
B. All participants had identical legal status
C. Historical roles varied by place and period
D. No historical performance tradition existed
**Answer: C.** A rigorous account distinguishes historical performance traditions from contemporary coercive dedication.

## 10. Which official document establishes the adaptation of the 1988 Act for Telangana?
A. The 1973 Six-Point Formula
B. The 2009 December statement
C. G.O.Ms.No.14 dated 28 April 2016
D. G.O.610 of 1985
**Answer: C.** The Telangana-adapted India Code text identifies the 2016 adaptation under the Reorganisation Act.

## 11. Which condition is most closely associated with prolonged excessive fluoride exposure?
A. Fluorosis
B. Malaria
C. Tuberculosis
D. Goitre from iodine deficiency
**Answer: A.** Fluorosis affects teeth and bones; laboratory water testing and exposure history are central.

## 12. Which Telangana district is a historically documented fluorosis case?
A. All districts uniformly
B. Nalgonda
C. Only Medchal
D. Only Hyderabad
**Answer: B.** Nalgonda district government documents groundwater fluoride and its monitoring centre; not every borewell is equally affected.

## 13. What was established in Nalgonda on 14 November 2013?
A. Hyderabad Metropolitan Development Authority
B. District Fluoride Monitoring Centre
C. National Green Tribunal
D. State Election Commission
**Answer: B.** The district government dates its coordination centre to 14 November 2013.

## 14. What does the DGHS NPPCF guidance identify as the desirable fluoride level?
A. 0.001 mg/L
B. 1.0 mg/L
C. 10 mg/L
D. 50 mg/L
**Answer: B.** DGHS describes 1.0 mg/L as desirable; distinguish it from the BIS permissible value under specified conditions.

## 15. What is the strongest evidence that a piped-water scheme reduces fluorosis exposure?
A. Repeated testing of water actually consumed
B. Budget allocation alone
C. Ribbon-cutting photographs
D. Number of pipes installed alone
**Answer: A.** Functional safe delivery, household use and clinical surveillance test real impact.

## 16. Which legislation addresses child marriage?
A. Right to Information Act, 2005
B. Prohibition of Child Marriage Act, 2006
C. Representation of the People Act, 1951
D. Banking Regulation Act, 1949
**Answer: B.** The PCMA provides prevention and legal remedies; distinguish voidable from automatically void marriages.

## 17. Which statement about child marriage is legally more accurate?
A. Always automatically valid
B. Always automatically void without exception
C. Generally voidable, with specified cases void
D. Not regulated by Indian law
**Answer: C.** The PCMA differentiates voidability and particular void circumstances.

## 18. Which metric best tests girls’ educational inclusion?
A. Sustained attendance, learning and safe access
B. Admission alone
C. Uniform distribution alone
D. School building count alone
**Answer: A.** Retention and meaningful access reveal burdens hidden by enrollment totals.

## 19. Which statute regulates child and adolescent labour?
A. Forest Conservation Act, 1980
B. Land Acquisition Act, 1894
C. Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986
D. Income-tax Act, 1961
**Answer: C.** The amended Act distinguishes children from adolescents and hazardous processes.

## 20. Which approach best prevents return to child labour after rescue?
A. Closing the case at first admission
B. Photographs of rescued children
C. A single raid
D. School reintegration with family livelihood support and follow-up
**Answer: D.** Rescue without educational and economic follow-through risks repeated exploitation.

## 21. What is a major risk in subcontracted labour supply chains?
A. Responsibility can be obscured across contractors
B. No worker can be recruited through agents
C. All subcontracting is illegal
D. All employers automatically keep complete records
**Answer: A.** Tracing recruitment, wage payment and the principal employer is necessary for accountability.

## 22. What is the correct analytical view of labour migration?
A. Mobility can be voluntary; exploitation arises from specific conditions
B. Every migrant is legally protected in practice
C. Every migrant is trafficked
D. Migration is a crime
**Answer: A.** Distinguish migration from coercive recruitment, wage theft and unsafe conditions.

## 23. Which historical Act specifically addresses inter-state recruited migrant workmen?
A. Municipalities Act alone
B. Indian Evidence Act, 1872
C. Inter-State Migrant Workmen Act, 1979
D. Official Secrets Act, 1923
**Answer: C.** The 1979 law specified contractor and employment duties; current transition to labour codes needs fresh verification.

## 24. What is an important migrant-child education measure?
A. Portable school records and bridge learning
B. Exclusion until permanent residence
C. No admission during seasonal moves
D. Only employer-run examinations
**Answer: A.** Continuity between source and destination schools reduces repeated learning disruption.

## 25. Which region is strongly associated with the early-1990s mass anti-arrack literacy mobilisation?
A. Nellore in undivided Andhra Pradesh
B. Only Adilabad forests
C. Only Hyderabad Old City
D. Only Warangal city
**Answer: A.** The mass anti-arrack movement was prominently rooted in Nellore; northern Telangana had distinct earlier anti-liquor organising.

## 26. Why did women’s anti-arrack campaigns become a public-policy issue?
A. They opposed literacy
B. They excluded local government
C. They linked household harm and state excise decisions
D. They concerned only a single family
**Answer: C.** Collective mobilisation connected domestic burdens to licensing and state revenue policy.

## 27. Which constitutional provision abolishes untouchability?
A. Article 17
B. Article 370
C. Article 280
D. Article 148
**Answer: A.** Article 17 is a direct equality guarantee; enforcement and social inclusion are separate tasks.

## 28. Which law provides protection against caste-based atrocities?
A. Companies Act, 1956
B. SC/ST (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989
C. Patent Act, 1970
D. Motor Vehicles Act alone
**Answer: B.** The 1989 law addresses specified atrocities; accessible complaints and victim protection are critical.

## 29. Which legal regime is especially relevant to self-governance in Scheduled Areas?
A. PESA and Fifth Schedule protections
B. The Official Secrets Act alone
C. A blanket application to every village
D. The Companies Act alone
**Answer: A.** PESA applies to Scheduled Areas; scope and implementing rules must be checked.

## 30. What is the strongest test of a self-help group’s empowerment impact?
A. Number of meetings alone
B. Loan totals alone
C. Photographs of beneficiaries
D. Members’ agency, earnings control and inclusive decision-making
**Answer: D.** Credit disbursement may help, but substantive agency and reduced vulnerability are more meaningful outcomes.

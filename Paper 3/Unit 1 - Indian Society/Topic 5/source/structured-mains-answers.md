# Six original structured mains model answers
**Original practice responses; not authenticated TGPSC PYQs.**

## Question 1: Assess the strengths and limitations of a rights-based approach to social welfare in India, with Telangana illustrations.
**Answer word count (including headings): 223**

**Introduction.** A rights-based approach treats welfare recipients as citizens entitled to fair treatment rather than passive beneficiaries. Its strength is to link constitutional equality with enforceable duties, transparent rules and remedies for exclusion. Yet legal recognition alone does not guarantee accessible services.

**Constitutional and legal foundation.** Articles 14, 15, 17 and 21A support equality and education, while Directive Principles guide social protection. The National Food Security Act creates specified nutritional entitlements; the Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act mandates non-discrimination and accessibility. Administrative schemes may remain discretionary unless backed by binding provisions.

**Telangana application.** Consider an elderly widow whose pension is blocked by biometric failure, or a student with a disability whose college has no accessible examination system. Both illustrate the gap between formal eligibility and actual receipt. The 2017 Telangana SC/ST Special Development Fund framework adds a statutory budgeting dimension, but allocation is not evidence of completed benefit.

**Limitations and counterview.** Fiscal capacity, weak frontline staffing, overlapping departmental responsibilities and outdated records constrain delivery. Narrow targeting can reduce expenditure yet create wrongful exclusion. Universal provision may improve access but requires sustainable financing and service capacity.

**Way forward and conclusion.** Publish operative eligibility rules, provide assisted applications and alternative authentication, strengthen independent appeals and audit outcomes rather than publicity. A rights-based welfare state succeeds when law, budget and everyday administrative experience converge.

## Question 2: Explain how women’s safety, economic empowerment and care services must be integrated in Telangana.
**Answer word count (including headings): 214**

**Background.** Women's welfare is multidimensional: freedom from violence, control over income, mobility and time for paid work are interdependent. A cash benefit may relieve immediate need without removing structural barriers to participation.

**Legal and programme mechanisms.** Mission Shakti separates Sambal safety measures, including One Stop Centres and helplines, from Samarthya empowerment components. The Domestic Violence Act, 2005 and workplace sexual-harassment legislation provide distinct remedies. Childcare and maternity support reduce the unpaid-care burden that otherwise restricts employment.

**Telangana illustration.** A self-help-group member in a rural mandal may receive credit but lack reliable transport, market access or control over proceeds. Mobility initiatives can reduce travel cost, while a functioning creche enables regular work. Neither automatically ensures safety on the final journey or financial independence. A district should link protection officers, healthcare, legal aid, livelihoods and accessible transport.

**Critical evaluation.** Loan counts, helpline calls and programme registrations are output indicators, not proof of empowerment. Women may fear reporting abuse; digital access and documentation may exclude migrants and women with disabilities. A cash-first perspective stresses choice, while a services-first perspective emphasises public infrastructure. Both contributions are necessary.

**Conclusion.** Measure safe case resolution, income controlled by women, employment retention, care hours and effective grievance response. Integrating protection, care and opportunity makes policy more than a collection of announcements.

## Question 3: Distinguish child nutrition, education and protection policies and propose a district convergence strategy.
**Answer word count (including headings): 218**

**Introduction.** Children need adequate nutrition, learning and protection throughout the lifecycle, but each need involves different laws, professionals and indicators. Treating all child welfare as a single scheme hides critical delivery gaps.

**Nutrition.** Mission Poshan 2.0 supports Anganwadi nutrition and early childhood services. The National Food Security Act provides specified maternal and child nutrition entitlements. Stunting, wasting and anaemia should be tracked separately because they reflect different conditions and interventions.

**Education.** Article 21A and the Right to Education Act cover free compulsory elementary education for ages six to fourteen. Samagra Shiksha and PM POSHAN support inclusive learning and school meals. Attendance alone cannot establish foundational literacy or educational quality.

**Protection.** Mission Vatsalya supports the Juvenile Justice child-care system, while POCSO and child-labour law address distinct risks. Child Welfare Committees and district protection officials require trained referral pathways.

**Telangana application.** When a construction-worker family migrates to Hyderabad, the child may lose continuity of Anganwadi, school and immunisation records. A district convergence protocol should enable assisted transfer without withholding immediate nutrition or schooling. Frontline workers must coordinate with health facilities and urban local bodies.

**Way forward.** Track uninterrupted service days, growth measurements, learning progression and safe protection-case closure, with privacy safeguards. Budget convergence is useful only when the child experiences continuity. Child-centred planning combines entitlements, quality and accountable referral.

## Question 4: Evaluate Scheduled Caste and Scheduled Tribe welfare through representation, resource rights and development funds.
**Answer word count (including headings): 220**

**Introduction.** SC and ST welfare requires more than poverty transfers because exclusion may involve discrimination, unequal representation, insecure land tenure and lack of control over common resources. The appropriate instruments therefore differ across contexts.

**SC framework.** Article 17 prohibits untouchability. The Protection of Civil Rights Act and SC/ST Prevention of Atrocities Act address specified harms. Reservation, scholarships, residential education and livelihood assistance aim to widen opportunity. Their effectiveness depends on quality and enforcement, not merely sanctioned seats.

**ST framework.** The Fifth Schedule, PESA and Forest Rights Act address territorial governance and qualifying individual or community forest rights. PESA concerns Scheduled Area self-government; FRA concerns recognition of forest rights. These cannot be replaced by an income grant. Culturally appropriate schooling and health access remain necessary.

**Telangana application.** The SC/ST Special Development Fund Act, 2017 provides a targeted budgeting framework. A hostel allocation is not a functioning hostel; a forest-rights application is not an approved title. Administrators must trace sanctioned funds, actual completion, Gram Sabha procedures and service outcomes.

**Critical perspectives.** Redistributive policy addresses immediate deprivation, while empowerment requires secure rights and institutional voice. Poor targeting and unequal reach within broad categories can reproduce disadvantage.

**Conclusion.** Combine anti-discrimination enforcement, transparent development budgeting, meaningful local participation and measurable educational and livelihood outcomes. Report group-specific evidence without treating all communities as identical.

## Question 5: Why must disability and old-age policies go beyond pensions and certificates?
**Answer word count (including headings): 216**

**Background.** Pensions and certificates are important administrative instruments, but inclusion depends on whether people can move, communicate, obtain care and exercise choices with dignity. Ageing and disability overlap without being the same condition.

**Disability rights.** The Rights of Persons with Disabilities Act, 2016 requires equality, accessibility and reasonable accommodation. Specified reservation provisions support representation, while accessible teaching, transport, public information and workplaces enable actual participation. A certificate alone cannot remove barriers.

**Older persons.** Article 41 guides assistance in old age, and the Maintenance and Welfare of Parents and Senior Citizens Act, 2007 provides a maintenance framework. Social pensions help income security, but chronic care, protection from abuse, affordable transport and community support remain essential.

**Telangana cases.** A student with a disability may receive a scholarship but face inaccessible examinations. An elderly widow may lose pension payments because biometric verification fails. Both need assisted service, lawful alternatives, written decisions and appeals. Urban isolation and rural travel distance create different policy demands.

**Limitations.** Counting ramps, certificates or approved pensioners overstates success when toilets, service counters, medicines or payments remain inaccessible. Strict fraud controls may inadvertently exclude eligible residents.

**Way forward.** Audit the complete service journey, restore interrupted payments, invest in community healthcare and consult affected groups. Measure participation, employment retention, continuous income and resolved grievances, not only paperwork.

## Question 6: Critically examine poverty-alleviation policy as a combination of employment, food, health and education.
**Answer word count (including headings): 222**

**Introduction.** Poverty is not only inadequate income. It also includes hunger, poor health, insecure employment, interrupted schooling and weak access to public services. Consequently, no single transfer or programme can reliably eliminate deprivation.

**Employment and food.** MGNREGA provides a demand-driven statutory rural employment guarantee within its legal conditions. The National Food Security Act establishes specified food and nutrition entitlements. Timely work, wage payment, food access and nutritional quality must be measured separately.

**Human capabilities.** Anganwadi nutrition, PM POSHAN, school education and primary healthcare reduce longer-term deprivation. Insurance for hospital treatment can limit eligible expenses but does not substitute for preventive care, regular medicines or safe drinking water.

**Telangana application.** A landless household in a drought-prone mandal may need rural work, reliable food support, childcare and school continuity simultaneously. In Hyderabad, a migrant household may face housing, documentation and service-portability barriers. A scheme's budget expenditure does not establish improved household capability.

**Debate.** Universal services can limit wrongful exclusion but require funding and capacity. Narrow targeting conserves resources yet may depend on inaccurate lists. Effective design combines accessible foundational services, transparent targeted support and a working appeal mechanism.

**Way forward and conclusion.** Track work demanded and supplied, delayed wages, nutrition, learning, out-of-pocket health costs and durable earnings. Coordinate programmes around households while respecting privacy. Poverty alleviation succeeds when immediate protection enables long-term autonomy.

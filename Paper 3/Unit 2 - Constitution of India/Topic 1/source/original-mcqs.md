# TGPSC P3 U2 T1 — Original Mcqs

## 6. Original practice MCQs — not official previous-year questions

**Q1.** Which enactment ended East India Company rule and transferred governance to the British Crown?  
A. Regulating Act 1773  B. Charter Act 1833  C. Government of India Act 1858  D. Indian Councils Act 1892  
**Answer: C.** The 1858 Act followed the 1857 revolt; 1773 and 1833 restructured Company-era government.

**Q2.** Dyarchy at the provincial level was introduced by which statute?  
A. Indian Councils Act 1909  B. Government of India Act 1919  C. Government of India Act 1935  D. Indian Independence Act 1947  
**Answer: B.** The 1919 Act divided provincial subjects into reserved and transferred fields.

**Q3.** Which statement about the Government of India Act 1935 is accurate?  
A. It introduced universal adult franchise  B. Its proposed all-India federation operated fully  C. It provided provincial autonomy  D. It abolished the office of Governor  
**Answer: C.** Provincial autonomy operated, but the envisaged federation did not operate as designed.

**Q4.** The Constituent Assembly first met on:  
A. 9 December 1946  B. 22 January 1947  C. 29 August 1947  D. 26 January 1950  
**Answer: A.** The Objectives Resolution was adopted in January 1947; the Drafting Committee was appointed in August 1947.

**Q5.** Who was Constitutional Adviser to the Constituent Assembly?  
A. B. R. Ambedkar  B. B. N. Rau  C. Rajendra Prasad  D. K. M. Munshi  
**Answer: B.** Rau prepared preparatory material and a draft; Ambedkar chaired the Drafting Committee.

**Q6.** The Drafting Committee was appointed on:  
A. 13 December 1946  B. 15 August 1947  C. 29 August 1947  D. 26 November 1949  
**Answer: C.** The Assembly appointed the seven-member Committee on 29 August 1947.

**Q7.** Which of the following was NOT one of the original seven Drafting Committee members?  
A. D. P. Khaitan  B. Syed Mohammad Saadulla  C. B. N. Rau  D. Alladi Krishnaswami Ayyar  
**Answer: C.** Rau was Constitutional Adviser, not a member of the seven-member Committee.

**Q8.** The Objectives Resolution was moved by:  
A. Vallabhbhai Patel  B. Jawaharlal Nehru  C. B. R. Ambedkar  D. Hansa Mehta  
**Answer: B.** Nehru moved it on 13 December 1946; it was adopted on 22 January 1947.

**Q9.** Which word was inserted in the Preamble by the 42nd Amendment?  
A. Republic  B. Liberty  C. Secular  D. Sovereign  
**Answer: C.** Socialist, secular and integrity were added in 1976; the other terms were already present.

**Q10.** Which proposition best states the legal role of the Preamble?  
A. It alone creates a legislative power  B. It overrides every operative Article  C. It guides interpretation and forms part of the Constitution  D. It is irrelevant to basic structure  
**Answer: C.** It is an interpretive guide, not a free-standing source of power.

**Q11.** Which Article states that India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States?  
A. Article 1  B. Article 3  C. Article 14  D. Article 368  
**Answer: A.** Article 3 concerns territorial reorganisation; Article 368 concerns amendments.

**Q12.** Under Article 3, the views of an affected State legislature are:  
A. a binding veto  B. constitutionally irrelevant  C. sought through the prescribed referral but not binding  D. required only after enactment  
**Answer: C.** Referral for views is required under the proviso, but Parliament is not bound to agree.

**Q13.** The law creating Telangana in 2014 was principally enacted under which constitutional mechanism?  
A. Article 368 alone  B. Articles 3 and 4  C. Article 32  D. Article 356  
**Answer: B.** Article 4 expressly distinguishes reorganisation-related changes from Article 368 amendments.

**Q14.** Which constitutional Part contains Directive Principles of State Policy?  
A. Part II  B. Part III  C. Part IV  D. Part IVA  
**Answer: C.** Part III contains Fundamental Rights and Part IVA contains Fundamental Duties.

**Q15.** Which statement about writ jurisdiction is correct?  
A. Article 226 is limited to Fundamental Rights alone  B. Article 32 applies to all legal disputes  C. Article 226 covers Fundamental Rights and other purposes  D. High Courts cannot issue writs  
**Answer: C.** High Court writ jurisdiction under Article 226 is broader in subject matter than Article 32.

**Q16.** Kesavananda Bharati (1973) is best known for holding that:  
A. no Fundamental Right can ever be amended  B. Parliament cannot amend any constitutional provision  C. Parliament may amend but cannot destroy basic structure  D. State legislatures can amend the Constitution unilaterally  
**Answer: C.** It rejected both absolute unamendability and unlimited amending power.

**Q17.** The 1973 Kesavananda decision was delivered by a bench of:  
A. 5 judges  B. 7 judges  C. 9 judges  D. 13 judges  
**Answer: D.** It was decided by a 13-judge bench, with a narrow 7–6 split on the key limitation.

**Q18.** Which case is particularly associated with the harmony between Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles?  
A. Minerva Mills (1980)  B. A. K. Gopalan (1950)  C. Keshav Singh (1965)  D. Berubari (1960)  
**Answer: A.** Minerva Mills rejected attempts to make amending power unlimited and stressed constitutional balance.

**Q19.** Which constitutional amendment created the GST constitutional framework?  
A. 42nd  B. 44th  C. 86th  D. 101st  
**Answer: D.** The 101st Amendment (2016) established key GST constitutional provisions.

**Q20.** Which statement about the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026 is correct as of 23 October 2026?  
A. It became the 131st Amendment Act  B. It failed to pass Lok Sabha in April 2026  C. It abolished Article 368  D. It created Telangana  
**Answer: B.** The proposal failed to secure the necessary majority on 17 April 2026; it must not be described as enacted law.

**Q21.** Which voting threshold does Article 368(2) prescribe in each House for a constitutional amendment Bill?  
A. A simple majority of members present and voting at a joint sitting  B. A majority of the total membership and at least two-thirds of members present and voting in each House  C. Two-thirds of the total membership in each House in every case  D. A simple majority in each House followed by ratification by all States  
**Answer: B.** Article 368 requires two simultaneous majorities in each House; there is no joint sitting for this purpose. Option A wrongly substitutes an ordinary-law voting route; C misstates the two-thirds denominator; D wrongly universalises State ratification. [S2]

**Q22.** Under the proviso to Article 368(2), which proposed change requires ratification by at least half of the State legislatures?  
A. Every amendment concerning Fundamental Rights  B. An amendment changing a List in the Seventh Schedule  C. Every parliamentary statute affecting State administration  D. Every amendment to the Preamble, irrespective of its subject  
**Answer: B.** The proviso expressly includes changes to the Seventh Schedule Lists. A and D are not automatic ratification categories; C concerns ordinary statutes rather than the enumerated constitutional amendment subjects. [S2]

**Q23.** Which statement correctly describes Article 4 in relation to State reorganisation?  
A. It makes every Article 3 law an Article 368 amendment  B. It gives an affected State legislature a binding veto  C. It permits necessary First and Fourth Schedule changes in an Article 3 law without treating that law as an Article 368 amendment  D. It requires a separate constitutional amendment before Parliament can alter State boundaries  
**Answer: C.** Article 4(1) authorises necessary Schedule and consequential provisions, and Article 4(2) expressly excludes such laws from the Article 368 amendment procedure. A and D contradict Article 4(2); B confuses the Article 3 consultation requirement with consent. [S2]

**Q24.** What distinguishes High Court writ jurisdiction under Article 226 from the Supreme Court's Article 32 jurisdiction?  
A. Article 226 can be used only to enforce Fundamental Rights  B. Article 226 permits writs for Fundamental Rights and for other legal purposes  C. Article 226 applies only after the Supreme Court has refused an Article 32 petition  D. Article 226 allows High Courts to amend constitutional text  
**Answer: B.** Article 226(1) explicitly extends beyond Part III rights; Article 32 specifically provides a remedy for enforcing Fundamental Rights. A narrows Article 226 incorrectly; C invents an exhaustion prerequisite; D confuses judicial remedies with constitutional amendment. [S2, S14]

**Q25.** Which statement most accurately describes the Supreme Court's Article 32 jurisdiction?  
A. It provides an original remedy for enforcement of Fundamental Rights, including specified writs  B. It authorises only advisory opinions requested by the President  C. It replaces Article 226 jurisdiction of High Courts  D. It authorises the Court to create a new State by writ  
**Answer: A.** Article 32 empowers the Supreme Court to enforce Fundamental Rights by appropriate directions, orders and writs. B confuses it with advisory jurisdiction under Article 143; C contradicts the coexistence of High Court writ powers; D confuses adjudication with the Article 3 legislative route. [S2, S14]

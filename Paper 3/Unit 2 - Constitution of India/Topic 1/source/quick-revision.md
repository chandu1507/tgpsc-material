# TGPSC P3 U2 T1 — Quick Revision

## 3. Standalone quick revision — 30 examination anchors

1. **1773 Regulating Act:** first important parliamentary intervention in East India Company governance; Governor-General of Bengal and a Supreme Court at Calcutta followed.
2. **1784 Pitt's India Act:** strengthened British governmental supervision of Company political affairs through a Board of Control.
3. **1833 Charter Act:** Governor-General of Bengal became Governor-General of India; centralised law-making, without creating Indian democracy.
4. **1858 Government of India Act:** Company rule ended; authority transferred to the British Crown.
5. **1861 Indian Councils Act:** limited legislative association; executive dominance continued.
6. **1892 Indian Councils Act:** enlarged councils and limited budget discussion; not responsible government.
7. **1909 Morley–Minto reforms:** expanded councils and introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
8. **1919 Government of India Act:** dyarchy in provinces; central bicameral legislature, restricted franchise.
9. **1935 Government of India Act:** provincial autonomy and three legislative lists; proposed federation did not operate as envisaged.
10. **1946 Cabinet Mission Plan:** scheme under which Constituent Assembly was formed, not a universal-adult-franchise election.
11. **9 December 1946:** Assembly first met; Sachchidananda Sinha temporary chairman.
12. **11 December 1946:** Rajendra Prasad elected Assembly President.
13. **13 December 1946 / 22 January 1947:** Objectives Resolution moved / adopted.
14. **29 August 1947:** seven-member Drafting Committee appointed; Ambedkar became chairman.
15. **B. N. Rau:** Constitutional Adviser, prepared an initial working draft; not a member of the seven-member Drafting Committee.
16. **26 November 1949:** Constitution adopted; **26 January 1950:** principal commencement date.
17. **Original Constitution:** 395 Articles, 22 Parts and 8 Schedules at commencement; do not present these as today's counts.
18. **Preamble:** sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic; justice, liberty, equality, fraternity.
19. **42nd Amendment (1976):** added socialist, secular and integrity to Preamble.
20. **Article 1:** India, that is Bharat, is a Union of States.
21. **Article 3:** Parliament may form new States or alter boundaries after constitutional procedure; State legislature's views are not a veto.
22. **Article 4:** Article 3-related schedule changes are not Article 368 amendments.
23. **Articles 12–35:** Fundamental Rights; **Articles 36–51:** Directive Principles; **Article 51A:** Fundamental Duties.
24. **Article 32:** Supreme Court remedies for Fundamental Rights; **Article 226:** broader High Court writ jurisdiction.
25. **Article 368:** constitutional amendment procedure; some matters additionally require ratification by at least half of States.
26. **Shankari Prasad (1951):** early broad view of Parliament's amending power.
27. **Golaknath (1967):** restricted amendments to Fundamental Rights; subsequently displaced by Kesavananda's approach.
28. **Kesavananda Bharati (1973):** 13-judge bench, 7–6 basic-structure limit on amending power.
29. **Minerva Mills (1980):** limited amending power and harmony of Fundamental Rights/DPSP are central to constitutional identity.
30. **2026 legislative distinction:** the proposed 131st Amendment Bill failed in Lok Sabha on 17 April 2026; a defeated Bill is not an enacted amendment.

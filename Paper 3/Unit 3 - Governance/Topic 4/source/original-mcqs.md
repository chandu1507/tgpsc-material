## 25 original MCQs with explanations — NOT official PYQs

**Provenance:** The following questions are newly authored for practice. They are **not** described as TGPSC/TSPSC official previous-year questions. No original official question-paper number has been established for this topic.

### Q1. Which observation most strongly demonstrates development beyond economic growth?
A. Increase in nominal state tax collections.  B. Increase in GDP without population adjustment.  C. Reduction in preventable mortality alongside improved access for disadvantaged groups.  D. Increase in registered construction contracts.

**Answer: C.** Mortality and equitable access directly reflect human capabilities; the other indicators may grow without a welfare improvement.

### Q2. A town has household water connections but supply lasts only two hours every third day. Which statement is most accurate?
A. Universal water service is achieved.  B. Connection coverage is an output; reliability is a service outcome.  C. The water system is a pure private good.  D. The connection count proves environmental sustainability.

**Answer: B.** Physical connection counts cannot establish quality, continuity or affordability.

### Q3. Which of the following is closest to a pure public good?
A. An individual dental filling.  B. A private tutoring class.  C. A household LPG cylinder.  D. A publicly available flood early-warning signal.

**Answer: D.** A warning signal is largely non-rival and difficult to exclude users from; healthcare and tutoring are usually merit/private services.

### Q4. What is the main problem with using per-capita income alone to judge development?
A. It cannot be expressed in currency.  B. It cannot be compared over time.  C. It can conceal distribution, capability and ecological outcomes.  D. It always underestimates urban income.

**Answer: C.** Averages can rise even when gains are concentrated or environmental harm increases.

### Q5. Which is an example of an externality justifying regulation?
A. A factory's untreated effluent damages downstream drinking-water quality.  B. A family purchases vegetables.  C. A teacher buys a textbook.  D. A bank publishes its opening hours.

**Answer: A.** Pollution imposes costs on third parties not fully reflected in the firm's private production cost.

### Q6. The Eleventh and Twelfth Schedules of the Constitution are best understood as:
A. Automatic transfer of all listed funds to local bodies.  B. Lists of possible functional subjects within the decentralisation framework.  C. Judicially enforceable tariffs for every municipality.  D. Mandatory staffing ratios for all Gram Panchayats.

**Answer: B.** Effective devolution also requires state legislation, finance and administrative capacity.

### Q7. Which process best reflects meaningful participation rather than tokenism?
A. Taking signatures after the contractor is selected.  B. Publishing the final plan only in English.  C. Inviting comments before choices are final and issuing reasoned responses.  D. Consulting only influential landowners.

**Answer: C.** Participation requires the possibility of influencing decisions and receiving accountability for rejected suggestions.

### Q8. The main difference between a CBO and a professional NGO is generally:
A. CBOs can never register legally.  B. NGOs cannot deliver services.  C. CBOs are typically rooted in member communities, while NGOs may be board/donor governed.  D. CBOs are always constitutionally created.

**Answer: C.** These are tendencies, not absolute legal categories.

### Q9. Which risk can undermine civil-society representation?
A. Public access to budgets.  B. Elite capture by a narrow local group.  C. Written grievance responses.  D. Rotation of office bearers.

**Answer: B.** An association may speak loudly while excluding poorer residents, tenants or women.

### Q10. Registration of a voluntary organisation as a society automatically grants which permission?
A. Foreign contribution under FCRA.  B. CSR implementing-agency status.  C. Income-tax exemption for all activities.  D. None of the above.

**Answer: D.** Legal registration, FCRA, tax status and CSR eligibility are distinct.

### Q11. Which pairing is correct for Telangana?
A. SERP—primarily rural SHG institutions; MEPMA—urban poverty/SHGs.  B. SERP—only international NGOs; MEPMA—Supreme Court tribunal.  C. SERP—municipal tax collection only; MEPMA—rural irrigation tribunal.  D. Both agencies regulate listed-company CSR accounts.

**Answer: A.** Their institutional mandates address different rural and urban development settings.

### Q12. The strongest evidence of SHG economic empowerment would be:
A. Number of new group passbooks printed.  B. Aggregate loans sanctioned only.  C. Sustained member net earnings, control over income and reduced debt distress.  D. Number of ceremonial launch events.

**Answer: C.** Financial inputs matter only insofar as they improve livelihoods and agency.

### Q13. Telangana's Indira Mahila Shakti Policy was announced in:
A. March 2025.  B. January 2010.  C. July 2014.  D. December 2026.

**Answer: A.** The government announced the policy on 8 March 2025 [S05].

### Q14. Which statement about the June 2026 SHG bus announcement is valid?
A. It independently proves every group is profitable.  B. It is a dated government programme announcement, not an independent profitability audit.  C. It means all SHGs own buses.  D. It repeals Section 135 of the Companies Act.

**Answer: B.** A reported handover or future target must be distinguished from verified financial outcomes [S08].

### Q15. Which arrangement is most clearly a PPP rather than ordinary procurement?
A. Buying office stationery.  B. Hiring a plumber for one day.  C. A long-term wastewater concession with defined construction, operating and performance risks.  D. Purchasing a desktop computer.

**Answer: C.** Long-term service responsibility and allocated risks distinguish the concession.

### Q16. In a PPP, demand risk should ideally be borne by:
A. The party best able to influence and manage it, consistent with the contract and public value.  B. Always the poorest users.  C. Always the contractor irrespective of context.  D. No party because it disappears after signature.

**Answer: A.** Mechanical risk transfer can create failure or renegotiation; incentives and control matter.

### Q17. Viability Gap Funding is primarily intended to:
A. Finance all corporate marketing.  B. Support economically justified PPP infrastructure projects that are commercially unviable under applicable rules.  C. Exempt all PPPs from competitive bidding.  D. Replace public oversight of tariffs.

**Answer: B.** DEA's VGF framework is conditional support, not an automatic subsidy [S11].

### Q18. Which is a common PPP fiscal danger?
A. Transparent reporting of guarantees.  B. Lifecycle costing.  C. Unrecorded contingent liabilities and termination payments.  D. Independent engineering review.

**Answer: C.** Off-budget promises can impose later costs on taxpayers.

### Q19. Under Section 135, a company may be covered if it meets which test in the preceding financial year?
A. All three thresholds simultaneously.  B. Any one of ₹500 crore net worth, ₹1,000 crore turnover or ₹5 crore net profit.  C. Only if it has 10,000 employees.  D. Only if it is listed on a stock exchange.

**Answer: B.** The three statutory criteria are alternative thresholds [S09].

### Q20. The ordinary CSR spending rule for eligible companies is based on:
A. Two percent of turnover.  B. Two percent of all capital expenditure.  C. Two percent of average net profits as prescribed.  D. Ten percent of GST paid.

**Answer: C.** Section 135 uses average net profits, generally of the preceding three financial years, with statutory calculation rules [S09].

### Q21. Which activity is generally **not** qualifying CSR merely because a company labels it social responsibility?
A. An eligible public-health project under Schedule VII.  B. An eligible community education project.  C. Spending required to meet the company's ordinary pollution-control legal duties.  D. An eligible disability-inclusion programme.

**Answer: C.** Compliance with an existing legal obligation is not converted into CSR by relabelling it.

### Q22. An NGO is shown on the National CSR Exchange Portal. What does that establish?
A. Independent government certification of all its outcomes.  B. The portal provides a matching platform; corporate due diligence remains necessary.  C. Automatic FCRA permission.  D. Guaranteed CSR funding.

**Answer: B.** The official portal expressly disclaims independent verification of agency claims [S09].

### Q23. The most defensible indicator for a training programme is:
A. Number of certificates printed alone.  B. Attendance photographs alone.  C. Sustained job placement and earnings, disaggregated by vulnerable groups.  D. Number of speeches delivered.

**Answer: C.** Employment and earnings are closer to the intended outcome; disaggregation tests equity.

### Q24. Why can a complaint count rise after an accountability reform?
A. Complaints always mean service collapse.  B. Improved access and trust may encourage reporting of previously hidden problems.  C. More complaints legally prove corruption.  D. It shows all complaints are false.

**Answer: B.** Complaint data require interpretation alongside resolution rates, severity and service quality.

### Q25. Which policy design best combines state, market and civil society roles?
A. Remove all legal remedies once a private contractor is hired.  B. Give all budget authority to the largest charity.  C. Specify public standards, competitively contract appropriate tasks, include affected communities and independently verify outcomes.  D. Evaluate only the number of contracts signed.

**Answer: C.** Clear allocation of authority, incentives, voice and verification protects public value.

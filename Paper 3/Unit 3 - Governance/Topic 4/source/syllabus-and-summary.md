**Central argument.** Development is not the amount of infrastructure constructed or money spent. It is the sustained expansion of people's substantive opportunities, with democratic voice, fair distribution, environmental viability and public accountability. State institutions, markets, local associations and firms have different comparative advantages. The practical governance question is not which actor should replace the others, but which combination produces accessible services, protects rights and makes failure correctable.

**Telangana thread.** Examine rural SHG federations under the Society for Elimination of Rural Poverty (SERP), urban SHGs under MEPMA, the 2025 Indira Mahila Shakti policy, local water and sanitation delivery, public transport opportunities for SHGs, Hyderabad's urban growth, and PPP/CSR decisions around peri-urban and tribal communities. Distinguish a government announcement or administrative target from independently demonstrated impact.

**Learning outcomes.** A candidate should be able to explain at least four rival theories of development; distinguish public provision from public financing and private delivery; design participatory accountability; differentiate NGOs, CBOs, SHGs and charities; identify CSR applicability thresholds; choose a defensible PPP model and its risk allocation; and write a balanced Telangana-specific 200–250-word answer.

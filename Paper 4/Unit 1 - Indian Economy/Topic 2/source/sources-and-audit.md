**Additional official/primary methodology references:** S17 UNDP, *Multidimensional poverty and its assessment found their place in the 2030 Agenda* (2015), https://hdr.undp.org/content/multidimensional-poverty-and-its-assessment-found-their-place-2030-agenda ; S18 UNDP, *Acute Multidimensional Poverty: A New Index for Developing Countries* (2010), https://hdr.undp.org/system/files/documents/hdrp201011.pdf ; S19 ILO, *Labour underutilization* (14 April 2016), https://www.ilo.org/resource/labour-underutilization ; S20 MoSPI, *Annual Report PLFS 2020–21*, methodology §2.20.3–2.20.4 (365 days, 30-day subsidiary threshold, 7-day CWS), https://mospi.gov.in/sites/default/files/publication_reports/Annual_Report_PLFS_2020%2021_0.pdf ; S21 ILO, *Measuring what matters: NEET vs youth unemployment* (12 August 2025), https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/measuring-what-matters-neet-vs-youth-unemployment . ### Option audit 1 — key D [conceptual]

**Correct proposition:** Minimum-resource versus society-relative thresholds; definition-level correspondence. **Why the other options fail:** A conflates absolute poverty with MPI and relative poverty with HPI; B wrongly confines absolute poverty to villages; C falsely guarantees that growth eliminates relative deprivation. **Evidence:** S05; S17.

### Option audit 2 — key C [mathematical]

**Correct proposition:** Headcount does not quantify remaining poor persons' normalized shortfalls. **Why the other options fail:** A inequality need not vanish; B a line need not be abolished; D some households remain below the line. **Evidence:** S05; FGT lab.

### Option audit 3 — key B [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** MPCE is monthly household consumption per person; free-item imputation conventions must be specified. **Why the other options fail:** A describes a person's salary; C confuses survey mean with official cutoff; D confuses household consumption with national product. **Evidence:** S04; S16.

### Option audit 4 — key A [secondary-pending-original-reports]

**Correct proposition:** Different normative baskets and methods can produce different 2011–12 estimates. **Why the other options fail:** B no duplicate census is needed; C both committees addressed poverty measurement; D legal tender is irrelevant. **Evidence:** historical committee comparison.

### Option audit 5 — key D [conceptual]

**Correct proposition:** Capability asks about substantive freedoms and achievable functionings. **Why the other options fail:** A highest-decile income is not freedom; B money supply is not capability; C GDP alone omits what people can achieve. **Evidence:** S17.

### Option audit 6 — key C [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** UNDP introduced MPI in 2010 to address overlapping deprivations beyond HPI's aggregate limitations. **Why the other options fail:** A CPI and B IIP are separate statistical series; D WPI is a price index, not HPI's successor. **Evidence:** S17.

### Option audit 7 — key B [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** MPI = H × A; A measures average weighted deprivation intensity among MPI-poor persons. **Why the other options fail:** A household count is not A; C workers' income is not A; D farm share is not A. **Evidence:** S18.

### Option audit 8 — key A [primary-source-bounded]

**Correct proposition:** National MPI uses twelve indicators across health, education and living standards; indicator definitions matter. **Why the other options fail:** B CPI is a price index; C a single income line is monetary poverty; D unemployment alone is not national MPI. **Evidence:** S06; S07.

### Option audit 9 — key D [primary-source-bounded]

**Correct proposition:** 3.76% is NITI's projected Telangana 2022–23 MPI headcount, not a fresh survey observation. **Why the other options fail:** A 2022–23 was not directly observed NFHS-6; B World Bank dollar line differs; C unemployment is a separate measure. **Evidence:** S07.

### Option audit 10 — key C [definition]

**Correct proposition:** UR uses unemployed divided by employed plus unemployed. **Why the other options fail:** A LFPR divides labour force by population; B is demographic growth; D WPR divides employed by population. **Evidence:** S03; S19.

### Option audit 11 — key B [reasoned-measurement]

**Correct proposition:** If discouraged workers leave measured unemployment and labour force, UR can decline with unchanged employment. **Why the other options fail:** A UR and LFPR use different numerators/denominators; C a rise is not necessary; D 100% is not implied. **Evidence:** S19.

### Option audit 12 — key A [primary-source-methodology]

**Correct proposition:** Usual status considers 365 days; subsidiary economic work generally needs at least 30 days. **Why the other options fail:** B is weekly status; C excludes informal and other economic activities; D insurance claims do not define PLFS status. **Evidence:** S20.

### Option audit 13 — key D [primary-source-methodology]

**Correct proposition:** CWS refers to activity during the seven days before interview. **Why the other options fail:** A is usual status; B five years is not CWS; C quarter is not CWS. **Evidence:** S20.

### Option audit 14 — key C [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** 2025 PLFS redesign and calendar-year reporting complicate naive year-to-year comparisons. **Why the other options fail:** A UR is not GDP growth; B formalisation was not universal; D PLFS continues. **Evidence:** S03.

### Option audit 15 — key B [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** 3.1% is 2025 all-India usual-status unemployment for persons aged 15+. **Why the other options fail:** A 59.3% is LFPR; C 9.9% is youth UR; D 14.96% belongs to an earlier MPI estimate. **Evidence:** S03.

### Option audit 16 — key A [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** 2025 usual-status youth UR 9.9% applies to ages 15–29. **Why the other options fail:** B older-than-60 is not youth; C poverty-line households are not an age group; D under-14 children are not the 15–29 group. **Evidence:** S03.

### Option audit 17 — key D [conceptual]

**Correct proposition:** Excess farm-family labour with negligible marginal product illustrates disguised unemployment. **Why the other options fail:** A active search without work is open unemployment; B voluntary job transition is not disguised unemployment; C layoff is different. **Evidence:** labour-economics definition.

### Option audit 18 — key C [definition-with-qualification]

**Correct proposition:** Low hours and skill mismatch are forms of underutilisation; strict ILO time-related underemployment also requires availability and a hours threshold. **Why the other options fail:** A employed persons need not have zero LFPR; B inflation is unrelated; D employed persons are not necessarily openly unemployed. **Evidence:** S19.

### Option audit 19 — key B [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** NEET includes people not employed, studying or training, whether or not seeking work. **Why the other options fail:** A poverty gap measures monetary shortfall; C UR excludes many non-seeking youth; D GDP deflator measures prices. **Evidence:** S21.

### Option audit 20 — key A [policy-inference]

**Correct proposition:** LFPR change must be evaluated alongside wages, hours, safety and job quality. **Why the other options fail:** B participation alone does not prove security; C joblessness need not disappear; D MPI change is not determined. **Evidence:** S19.

### Option audit 21 — key D [policy-inference]

**Correct proposition:** Childcare and safe transport target the stated binding barriers; this is a policy inference, not a measured impact claim. **Why the other options fail:** A courses alone do not remove childcare/transport constraints; B GDP rebasing is irrelevant; C crop subsidy is indirect. **Evidence:** ILO labour-barrier framing.

### Option audit 22 — key C [primary-law-bounded]

**Correct proposition:** Act 36 of 2025 provides 125 days of wage employment per eligible rural household, effective 1 July 2026. **Why the other options fail:** A 365 days exceeds statutory guarantee; B ignores express entitlement; D hours is the wrong unit. **Evidence:** S10; S11; S12.

### Option audit 23 — key B [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** ₹4,122 rural and ₹6,996 urban exclude specified free-welfare-item imputations; with-imputation values differ. **Why the other options fail:** A average MPCE is not poverty cutoff; C reverses with/without free-item imputations; D MPCE is not worker earnings. **Evidence:** S16.

### Option audit 24 — key A [primary-source]

**Correct proposition:** 2025 World Bank update changed PPP base, threshold and underlying survey series; direct cross-vintage comparison is invalid. **Why the other options fail:** B PPPs reflect price differences; C PPP conversion differs from market FX; D the poverty line is not unemployment. **Evidence:** S05.

### Option audit 25 — key B [independent-arithmetic]

**Correct proposition:** P1=[(100−50)/100+(100−80)/100]/4=17.5%; all options represent different valid quantities. **Why the other options fail:** A 2/4=50% is P0; C 0.70/2=35% is poor-only mean gap; D (0.25+0.04)/4=7.25% is P2. **Evidence:** FGT lab.

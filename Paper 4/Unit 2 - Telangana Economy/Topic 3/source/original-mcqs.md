# Original MCQs with Explanations — Not Official PYQs

**Provenance notice:** All 30 questions below are newly authored practice questions. No question is represented as a verified TGPSC previous-year question. Answers are based on the cited statutes and official institutions; they are not a substitute for the current bare Act.

**Q1. Which of the following is the most accurate definition of intermediary abolition?**
A. Automatic allotment of every former estate to landless labourers  
B. Removal of a privileged layer between the state and the cultivator  
C. Abolition of all agricultural leases  
D. Elimination of private ownership of farmland  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Intermediary abolition restructures revenue and estate rights. Ownership for tenants or distribution to landless persons requires separate legal steps.

**Q2. The Hyderabad (Abolition of Jagirs) Regulation is cited in which dating convention?**
A. Gregorian year 1973 only  
B. Hijri 1445 only  
C. 1358 Fasli  
D. 1956 Saka  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** The regulation is cited as 1358 Fasli (Regulation LXIX); it appears among the original entries in the Constitution's Ninth Schedule.

**Q3. Which statement best distinguishes a deshmukh from a jagirdar?**
A. Both are invariably identical statutory tenure classes  
B. Deshmukh was a historical office or influential local revenue status, while jagir refers to a defined revenue assignment  
C. Deshmukh always meant a protected tenant  
D. Jagirdar always meant a landless labourer  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The social and legal categories overlap in practice but are not interchangeable in statutory analysis.

**Q4. A protected tenant invokes Section 38-E while another claimant seeks an Occupancy Rights Certificate for former inam land. Which pairing is legally appropriate?**
A. Section 38-E—1973 ceiling statute; ORC—1977 assigned-land statute  
B. Section 38-E—Forest Rights Act, 2006; ORC—PESA, 1996  
C. Section 38-E—Bhu Bharati Act, 2025; ORC—Jagir abolition regulation  
D. Section 38-E—Telangana Tenancy and Agricultural Lands Act, 1950; ORC—Telangana Abolition of Inams Act, 1955  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** The 1950 tenancy statute provides the protected-tenant ownership route under Section 38-E; the 1955 Inams statute addresses eligible occupancy claims in former inam lands. The mechanisms and eligibility tests must not be conflated [S05, S06].

**Q5. Section 38-E is associated primarily with:**
A. GST registration  
B. Recognition or vesting of ownership in eligible protected tenants  
C. Gram Sabha forest management plans  
D. Nationalisation of banks  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The statutory conditions and proceedings matter; not every oral lessee automatically becomes an owner.

**Q6. Which pair is correctly matched?**
A. Telangana Abolition of Inams Act—1955  
B. Telangana Land Reforms Ceiling Act—1950  
C. Telangana Assigned Lands Transfer Act—1949  
D. Forest Rights Act—1970  
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** The inam abolition statute is Act VIII of 1955; ceiling legislation dates to 1973, assigned-land transfer restrictions to 1977, and FRA to 2006.

**Q7. Which instrument is most directly associated with registering eligible occupancy rights in former inam land, rather than resolving a tenancy or ceiling case?**
A. A Section 38-E protected-tenant ownership certificate under the 1950 tenancy law  
B. A surplus surrender order under the 1973 ceiling law  
C. An Occupancy Rights Certificate under the Telangana Abolition of Inams Act, 1955  
D. A restoration order concerning a prohibited transfer of assigned land  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** An ORC relates to qualifying former inam land under the Inams Act. A protected-tenancy ownership certificate and a ceiling-surplus order arise under different statutory mechanisms [S05, S06, S11].

**Q8. The Telangana Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act is from:**
A. 1947  
B. 1950  
C. 1973  
D. 2006  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** The Act is Telangana Act 1 of 1973, adapted from the Andhra Pradesh legislation after state bifurcation.

**Q9. A family holds both double-crop wetland and dryland. Why is one uniform acreage ceiling inadequate under the 1973 framework?**
A. Land classification affects only stamp duty, not ceilings  
B. The recorded tenant's name alone fixes the acreage ceiling  
C. All wetland is automatically exempt from agricultural land ceilings  
D. Section 5 uses land classes and standard-holding extents, while Section 4 also applies family-unit rules  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** The ceiling is not a universal number of acres: statutory land classification and family-unit provisions affect computation. Current amendments and exceptions require checking the applicable Act and rules [S11].

**Q10. Which sequence best measures actual ceiling redistribution?**
A. Political announcement → newspaper report  
B. Declaration → vesting → possession → allotment → beneficiary use  
C. Census enumeration → passport issue  
D. Registration → automatic immunity from all land laws  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Land identified as surplus is not necessarily physically available or productively allotted.

**Q11. Which statement about land ceilings is FALSE?**
A. Family-unit definitions influence calculation  
B. Tribunals may determine surplus holdings  
C. A declaration by itself proves the landless have received possession  
D. Land quality can affect a standard holding  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Implementation has distinct legal and physical stages; declaration alone is insufficient.

**Q12. The Telangana Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act was enacted in:**
A. 1955  
B. 1963  
C. 1977  
D. 2025  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Act 9 of 1977 protects specified government-assigned lands from prohibited transfers.

**Q13. The primary purpose of restrictions on transfers of assigned lands is to:**
A. Permit unrestricted speculation  
B. Preserve livelihood assets for intended landless beneficiaries  
C. Ban all private property in Telangana  
D. Replace tenancy records with voter lists  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Assignment often carries non-alienation conditions intended to prevent re-concentration and distress transfers.

**Q14. Which chronology correctly distinguishes the Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation and its Telangana application?**
A. 1970 original regulation → 1963 extension → 1959 amendment  
B. 1963 original regulation → 1970 extension → 1959 amendment  
C. 1959 original regulation → 1970 extension → 1963 amendment  
D. 1959 regulation → 1963 extension to Telangana Scheduled Areas → 1970 strengthening amendment  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** Regulation I of 1959, Regulation II of 1963 and Regulation I of 1970 have different legal functions. The territorial notification and amended text still require case-specific verification [S14, S15].

**Q15. What is the significance of Regulation I of 1970 ('1/70')?**
A. It abolished the RBI  
B. It tightened Scheduled Area land-transfer protections, including against circumvention through non-tribal transfers  
C. It introduced national income accounting  
D. It removed all tribal protections  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The 1970 amendment significantly strengthened restrictions on transfer to non-tribals in Scheduled Areas.

**Q16. A land parcel's location in a district containing Scheduled Areas means:**
A. Every village in the district is automatically notified  
B. Notification status of the particular village and parcel must be checked  
C. No land law applies  
D. All transfers are automatically valid  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Scheduled Area status depends on the relevant notification and territorial extent, not a broad district label.

**Q17. Which statute chiefly recognises individual and community forest rights?**
A. FRA, 2006  
B. Companies Act, 2013  
C. Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881  
D. GST Act, 2017  
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** The Forest Rights Act recognises eligible forest dwellers' cultivation, habitation and community resource rights.

**Q18. PESA, 1996 is most closely associated with:**
A. Monetary policy  
B. Gram Sabha and local self-governance in Scheduled Areas  
C. Banking deposit insurance  
D. Corporate income-tax rates  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** PESA adapts panchayat governance to Scheduled Areas; it is not a universal individual land-title statute.

**Q19. Which combination is correctly differentiated?**
A. PESA = forest title; FRA = GST  
B. 1/70 = Scheduled Area transfer restriction; FRA = forest rights; PESA = Scheduled Area self-governance  
C. All three are the same law  
D. 1/70 applies to every urban apartment  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** The three regimes have different territory, procedures, institutions and remedies.

**Q20. The Bhoodan movement is particularly associated with:**
A. Pochampally and Vinoba Bhave  
B. Plassey and Clive  
C. Dandi and salt duty alone  
D. Bretton Woods  
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** The voluntary land-gift movement began at Pochampally in 1951; later legislation institutionalised Bhoodan and Gramdan.

**Q21. Which law provided a statutory framework for Bhoodan and Gramdan in Telangana?**
A. Telangana Bhoodan and Gramdan Act, 1965  
B. RBI Act, 1934  
C. RTE Act, 2009  
D. Companies Act, 1956  
**Answer: A.** **Explanation:** Act 13 of 1965 addresses donation and disposal of Bhoodan land and associated institutions.

**Q22. Which is the best evidence that a Bhoodan promise improved livelihoods?**
A. Number of speeches praising donations  
B. Number of acres pledged, irrespective of title  
C. Land lawfully received, demarcated, possessed and cultivated by beneficiaries  
D. Number of newspaper headlines  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** Pledged land may be encumbered or unfit; effective distribution must be tracked through possession and use.

**Q23. A farmer asks whether a corrected Bhu Bharati record automatically cancels a statutory restriction on transfer of assigned land. Which answer is most defensible?**
A. Yes: any electronic mutation repeals older transfer restrictions  
B. Yes: displaying a private pattadar makes every transfer lawful  
C. Yes: the 2025 record-of-rights law replaced all tenancy, assignment and tribal-land laws  
D. No: record maintenance and mutation do not themselves override applicable substantive restrictions on land transfer  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** The Telangana Bhu Bharati Act, 2025 governs record-of-rights administration; legality of an assigned-land transfer must separately be tested under applicable substantive law [S07, S13, S19].

**Q24. Which statement about digital land records is MOST defensible?**
A. An online entry automatically overrides every court order  
B. A digital record can improve administration but does not itself resolve every title dispute  
C. All former jagir lands become private upon mutation  
D. Assigned land is freely transferable if shown online  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Record correction, ownership adjudication and statutory transfer restrictions are separate legal matters.

**Q25. Which document dated 14 April 2025 provides procedural rules under the Telangana Bhu Bharati (Record of Rights in Land) Act, 2025?**
A. The Telangana Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977  
B. The Telangana Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973  
C. G.O.Ms.No.39, Revenue (Land Administration-I) Department  
D. The Hyderabad (Abolition of Jagirs) Regulation, 1358 Fasli  
**Answer: C.** **Explanation:** The official Telangana Bhu Bharati documents repository lists G.O.Ms.No.39 dated 14 April 2025 as the Rules notification, separately from the Act and G.O.Ms.No.36 [S07, S19].

**Q26. Executive action deprives a person of land without authority of law. Which constitutional provision directly states the safeguard against such deprivation?**
A. Article 31A, protection of specified agrarian legislation  
B. Article 31B, Ninth Schedule provisions  
C. Article 39(b), a Directive Principle concerning material resources  
D. Article 300A, deprivation of property only by authority of law  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** Article 300A states the direct textual protection. Articles 31A, 31B and 39(b) have distinct constitutional roles and are not substitutes for that wording [S01].

**Q27. Article 31A is especially relevant to land reform because it:**
A. Creates a universal individual right to ten acres  
B. Protects specified agrarian-reform laws from certain constitutional challenges  
C. Creates the Election Commission  
D. Establishes the RBI  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Article 31A and the Ninth Schedule historically protected land-reform measures; they do not dispense with all constitutional limits.

**Q28. Agricultural Census 'operational holdings' should NOT be treated as identical to:**
A. Units of agricultural operation  
B. Ownership holdings established by title  
C. Land under operational control  
D. Size-class distribution of operated area  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** A tenant can operate land owned by another person; operational and ownership distributions differ.

**Q29. A land-reform evaluation counts only men recorded as pattadars. Which inference is LEAST defensible?**
A. The count may omit women who cultivate but lack recorded title  
B. The count may omit oral tenants without pattadar status  
C. The count may fail to describe outcomes for landless agricultural labourers  
D. The count accurately measures every adult woman's ownership and cultivation rights  
**Answer: D.** **Explanation:** Pattadar-only counts cannot establish women's legal and operational rights, informal tenancy or landless-worker outcomes. Ownership, operation and labour participation need separate measures.

**Q30. The strongest administrative response to a disputed Scheduled Area, former-inam parcel is to:**
A. Register any deed presented first  
B. Classify the parcel, check notified status and historical tenure, hear affected parties, apply the relevant statute and update records after reasoned decision  
C. Assume all digital records are conclusive  
D. Ignore tribal and occupancy protections  
**Answer: B.** **Explanation:** Multiple overlapping legal regimes require careful classification, evidence, notice, lawful remedies and appeal rather than an automatic portal-based decision.

### Audit Q4 — proposed answer D

**Primary-text scope:** 1973 Act s.13 cross-refers to 1950 tenancy s.38-E; 1955 Inams Act ss.4–10 regulate occupancy..

- **A:** The 1973 ceiling Act refers to protected-tenant ownership but is not the source of s.38-E; the 1977 assigned-land law is not the Inams occupancy statute.
- **B:** FRA 2006 addresses forest rights and PESA 1996 Scheduled Area governance; neither creates this tenancy/Inams pairing.
- **C:** Bhu Bharati 2025 administers land records, not s.38-E; jagir abolition is not the Inams occupancy law.
- **D:** The 1950 tenancy law contains s.38-E; the 1955 Inams Act provides occupant registration subject to eligibility and Collector enquiry.

### Audit Q6 — proposed answer A

**Primary-text scope:** 1955 Inams Act title/Act VIII; 1973 ceiling Act 1; 1977 assigned-land Act 9..

- **A:** Correct: the India Code text identifies the Telangana Abolition of Inams Act as Act VIII of 1955.
- **B:** Incorrect: the ceiling statute is Act 1 of 1973, not 1950.
- **C:** Incorrect: the assigned-land transfer statute is Act 9 of 1977, not 1949.
- **D:** Incorrect: the central Forest Rights Act dates to 2006, not 1970.

### Audit Q7 — proposed answer C

**Primary-text scope:** 1955 Inams Act ss.4–10; 1973 ceiling Act ss.10–14; 1950 tenancy s.38-E cross-ref..

- **A:** A tenancy ownership certificate under s.38-E is a separate statutory route; it does not itself register occupancy of former inam land.
- **B:** A ceiling surrender order concerns excess holdings and vesting, not an Inams occupancy claim.
- **C:** Correct: ss.4–8 identify eligible occupant categories and s.10 directs the Collector to examine claims; an ORC follows that legal process.
- **D:** Restoration after a prohibited assigned-land transfer belongs to a distinct protective regime, not the Inams occupant-registration mechanism.

### Audit Q8 — proposed answer C

**Primary-text scope:** India Code Telangana Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, Act 1 of 1973..

- **A:** 1947 is not the enactment year of the named ceiling Act.
- **B:** 1950 belongs to the tenancy statute, not this ceiling Act.
- **C:** Correct: the named statute is Act 1 of 1973; the Telangana text notes adaptation after 2014.
- **D:** 2006 belongs to the central Forest Rights Act, not this ceiling Act.

### Audit Q9 — proposed answer D

**Primary-text scope:** 1973 ceiling Act ss.3(d),(e),(f),4,5 and First Schedule..

- **A:** Incorrect: statutory wet/dry classes affect standard holdings, not merely stamp duty.
- **B:** Incorrect: s.4 aggregates holdings of a family unit; one recorded tenant name is not the sole ceiling determinant.
- **C:** Incorrect: wetland is expressly classified, not automatically excluded from the ceiling.
- **D:** Correct: s.4 provides family-unit ceilings and s.5 differentiates standard holdings by land class; applicable amendments/exemptions must be checked.

### Audit Q12 — proposed answer C

**Primary-text scope:** India Code Telangana Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, Act 9 of 1977, ss.1–5..

- **A:** 1955 identifies the Inams abolition statute, not the assigned-land transfer statute.
- **B:** 1963 is not the enactment year of the named assigned-land transfer Act.
- **C:** Correct: the named law is Act 9 of 1977; its subject is restricted transfers of certain lands assigned to landless poor persons.
- **D:** 2025 identifies the Bhu Bharati record-of-rights legislation, not the 1977 Act.

### Audit Q23 — proposed answer D

**Primary-text scope:** Bhu Bharati Rules GO Ms 39 (14 Apr 2025), rule 5(4)(b)–(c); 1977 Assigned Lands Act..

- **A:** Incorrect: an electronic mutation does not repeal statutory prohibitions; rule 5 expressly requires separate registration checks.
- **B:** Incorrect: a displayed pattadar entry does not remove prohibited-property and assigned-land checks under rule 5(4).
- **C:** Incorrect: the record-of-rights legislation does not replace the 1977 Assigned Lands Act or Scheduled Area transfer restrictions; the Rules cite both.
- **D:** Correct: rule 5(4)(c) explicitly requires checking 1959 Scheduled Area and 1977 Assigned Lands prohibitions.

### Audit Q24 — proposed answer B

**Primary-text scope:** Bhu Bharati Rules 2025 rules 4 and 5, including notice, evidence, enquiry and prohibited-property checks..

- **A:** Incorrect: rule 4 requires a reasoned process for contested entries; portal data cannot automatically defeat judicial decisions.
- **B:** Correct: digital records assist administration, but rule 4 anticipates disputes and rule 5 separately requires legal transfer checks.
- **C:** Incorrect: historical jagir status cannot be converted into automatic private title through mutation alone.
- **D:** Incorrect: rule 5(4)(c) requires scrutiny of assigned-land restrictions despite electronic records.

### Audit Q25 — proposed answer C

**Primary-text scope:** Government of Telangana GO Ms 39 dated 14 Apr 2025, notification under Bhu Bharati Act s.21(1)..

- **A:** The 1977 Assigned Lands Act is a separate primary statute, not the 2025 procedural rules notification.
- **B:** The 1973 ceiling Act is a separate statute, not the Bhu Bharati Rules notification.
- **C:** Correct: GO Ms 39 (Revenue, Land Administration-I) dated 14 April 2025 publishes the 2025 Rules under s.21(1).
- **D:** The 1358 Fasli Jagir regulation predates the 2025 Rules and is not their notification.

### Audit Q30 — proposed answer B

**Primary-text scope:** Bhu Bharati Rules 2025 rule 4(3)–(7) notice/evidence/reasoned order and rule 5(4) prohibited transfers..

- **A:** Incorrect: rule 5(4) requires checks before registration; accepting the first deed without classification is unsafe.
- **B:** Correct: notification/tenure classification, hearing and statutory checks reflect rules 4 and 5; the specific adjudicator depends on the claim.
- **C:** Incorrect: rule 4 provides rectification procedures and hearings, so electronic entries are not universally conclusive.
- **D:** Incorrect: rule 5(4)(c) expressly requires attention to Scheduled Area and assigned-land protections.

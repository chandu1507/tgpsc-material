# Original practice MCQs with explanations

**All 30 questions are original practice items, not verified TGPSC previous-year questions.**

### MCQ 1. Which distinction best explains why a forest plantation cannot automatically compensate for an old forest?
A. Old forests have no commercial value
B. Old forests may contain complex soils, species interactions and customary rights
C. Plantations are always legally prohibited
D. A plantation can never store carbon
**Answer: B.** Compensation in planted area cannot establish equivalent habitat, soil history, biodiversity or social rights.

### MCQ 2. A village grazing common has enforceable local access rules and monitoring. It is best described as:
A. Unregulated open access
B. A privately owned factory
C. Common property with institutions
D. A non-renewable mineral
**Answer: C.** Common property can be collectively governed; open access lacks effective controls.

### MCQ 3. FSI forest cover primarily differs from recorded forest area because forest cover is:
A. Always a title deed
B. A satellite-based canopy/land-cover assessment
C. Only national parks
D. Only private tree farms
**Answer: B.** Recorded forest area is an administrative/legal category, while forest cover is remotely assessed.

### MCQ 4. Which statement about Telangana ISFR 2023 data is correct?
A. Forest cover and tree cover are the same
B. Forest cover is 21,179.04 sq km and tree cover 3,517.66 sq km
C. Both figures are 2026 measurements
D. Tree cover proves all plantations are natural forest
**Answer: B.** The two distinct figures are from the ISFR 2023 series, released later.

### MCQ 5. Commercial forest value-chain equity is best improved by:
A. Only increasing logging quotas
B. Transparent collection rights, grading, storage and fair prices
C. Prohibiting all market sales
D. Removing all ecological monitoring
**Answer: B.** Market access without tenure and bargaining power can worsen exploitation.

### MCQ 6. The Indian Forest Act 1927 is principally associated with:
A. Recognition of CFR rights
B. Forest classification, produce, transit and offences
C. Creation of CGWB
D. Constitutional water tribunals
**Answer: B.** FRA and groundwater assessment arise under different institutions and laws.

### MCQ 7. The 2023 forest-conservation amendment took effect on:
A. 1 January 2020
B. 1 December 2023
C. 1 April 2026
D. 2 June 2014
**Answer: B.** MoEFCC annual reporting identifies commencement on 1 December 2023.

### MCQ 8. Which pair is correctly distinguished?
A. FRA = mining lease; MMDR = forest rights
B. FRA = specified forest rights; PESA = Scheduled Area self-governance
C. PESA = satellite forest mapping; FSI = water rights
D. FRA = pollution consent; PESA = carbon credits
**Answer: B.** FRA and PESA overlap geographically but have distinct statutory purposes.

### MCQ 9. Under the FRA claim pathway, final district-level title decisions are taken by:
A. Reserve Bank of India
B. District Level Committee
C. Central Water Commission
D. Forest Survey of India
**Answer: B.** The Gram Sabha/FRC initiates, SDLC examines, and DLC decides claims.

### MCQ 10. Community forest resource rights primarily concern:
A. Only household plots
B. Protecting, regenerating, conserving and managing customary community forest resources
C. Automatic mineral ownership
D. All urban parkland
**Answer: B.** CFR rights extend beyond mere access to collect produce.

### MCQ 11. The Ministry of Tribal Affairs Telangana FRA booklet data of 97,536 titles relates to:
A. June 2023
B. October 2026
C. Census 2011
D. ISFR 2023
**Answer: A.** The historical administrative snapshot is explicitly dated June 2023.

### MCQ 12. A mining lease granted under MMDR law:
A. Automatically satisfies EIA and forest requirements
B. Creates a specified mineral concession but does not waive other approvals
C. Replaces FRA titles
D. Eliminates all water-quality monitoring
**Answer: B.** Concession, environment, forest, land and pollution permissions address separate legal questions.

### MCQ 13. A river basin is best defined by:
A. District borders
B. Land draining to a common outlet
C. The location of state capital
D. A single irrigation canal
**Answer: B.** Hydrological boundaries can cross political jurisdictions.

### MCQ 14. Which is consumptive water use?
A. Withdrawal followed by full immediate return at unchanged quality
B. Water lost to evapotranspiration and not promptly returned
C. A reservoir level measurement
D. River sediment transport
**Answer: B.** Consumptive use differs from gross withdrawal and return flows.

### MCQ 15. Why may drip irrigation fail to reduce basin-scale water consumption?
A. Drip always increases evaporation
B. Farmers may expand irrigated acreage after efficiency gains
C. Aquifers cannot recharge
D. Only surface water is used
**Answer: B.** The rebound effect can offset field-level withdrawal savings.

### MCQ 16. Stage of groundwater extraction is:
A. Share of total underground water exhausted
B. Annual extraction divided by annual extractable resources, expressed as a percentage
C. Annual rainfall divided by forest cover
D. Borewell depth divided by river length
**Answer: B.** It is a flow-based assessment ratio, not a stock-depletion percentage.

### MCQ 17. Which statement about hard-rock aquifers is most accurate?
A. All deep borewells have equal yield
B. Fractures and weathered zones produce heterogeneous storage and transmissivity
C. Groundwater is an underground river everywhere
D. Recharge cannot occur through tanks
**Answer: B.** Telangana hard-rock aquifers vary sharply across locations and fractures.

### MCQ 18. Telangana May 2026 groundwater depth of 9.29 m-bgl is:
A. A guaranteed district minimum
B. A reported statewide dashboard average for that month
C. An annual volume in BCM
D. A groundwater-quality score
**Answer: B.** A monthly average does not describe every aquifer or establish causation.

### MCQ 19. A good allocation framework for surface water must include:
A. Only dam storage
B. Timing, quality, environmental flows and different users
C. Only irrigation yield
D. Only urban water tariffs
**Answer: B.** Annual volume alone ignores reliability and ecological needs.

### MCQ 20. Which four uses form the land-use “four Fs”?
A. Food, feed, fuel, fibre
B. Finance, forest, freight, fisheries
C. Farms, factories, forests, floods
D. Fuel, fertiliser, finance, fisheries
**Answer: A.** The four Fs frame competition among crops, livestock, energy and industrial fibres.

### MCQ 21. A policy encouraging biofuel crops on irrigated food land raises:
A. No opportunity cost
B. Food–fuel–water trade-offs
C. Automatic soil restoration
D. Only an urban transport issue
**Answer: B.** Bioenergy feedstock competes with other land and water uses depending on site and crop.

### MCQ 22. Which factor can make dryland crop diversification difficult even when it saves water?
A. Assured procurement of all alternative crops
B. Weak market access and price uncertainty
C. Complete absence of rainfall
D. No role for crop insurance
**Answer: B.** Farmers respond to markets, labour, risk and institutions, not water efficiency alone.

### MCQ 23. Which mining impact is most directly associated with poorly stabilised overburden?
A. Higher school literacy
B. Slope failure, erosion and sedimentation
C. Reduced truck traffic
D. Guaranteed aquifer recharge
**Answer: B.** Overburden design and drainage are core mine-environment safeguards.

### MCQ 24. What is the most defensible mine-closure indicator?
A. Saplings planted on opening day
B. Stable slopes, safe drainage, suitable land use and long-term monitoring
C. Number of mining permits alone
D. Only royalty collected
**Answer: B.** Closure is an ecological and safety outcome, not a one-time planting count.

### MCQ 25. District Mineral Foundation effectiveness should be judged by:
A. Only the size of collections
B. Transparent benefits and outcomes for mining-affected people
C. Coal production alone
D. Number of exploration licences
**Answer: B.** Funds must address affected areas and people through accountable planning and spending.

### MCQ 26. Environmental clearance under the EIA Notification 2006 is:
A. Identical to a mining lease
B. A separate prior environmental appraisal requirement for scheduled projects
C. A private insurance contract
D. A guarantee of zero future pollution
**Answer: B.** Project-specific EIA requirements coexist with mining and pollution regulation.

### MCQ 27. Precaution is most relevant when:
A. Harm is known and fully reversible
B. Potentially serious harm remains scientifically uncertain
C. No ecological effect is possible
D. Only financial returns matter
**Answer: B.** Precaution addresses uncertainty about serious harm; prevention addresses known risks.

### MCQ 28. Strong sustainability emphasises:
A. Unlimited substitution of forests by buildings
B. Protecting critical natural capital that is hard to replace
C. Mining every deposit immediately
D. No economic activity
**Answer: B.** Irreplaceable habitats and ecological functions warrant safeguards beyond monetary offsets.

### MCQ 29. Which pairing is institutionally correct?
A. FSI — forest-cover assessment; CGWB — groundwater assessment
B. FSI — mining leases; CGWB — wildlife clearances
C. MoTA — all air consents; RBI — FRA titles
D. NGT — village crop procurement
**Answer: A.** Different agencies have specialised mandates; one permission does not replace another.

### MCQ 30. The strongest Telangana natural-resource policy package would:
A. Rely on one statewide groundwater depth
B. Combine forest/rights maps, aquifer budgets, tank governance, mine monitoring and grievance systems
C. Assume plantation area equals biodiversity
D. Ignore Scheduled Area consultation
**Answer: B.** Integrated landscape planning must combine evidence, rights, enforcement and local accountability.

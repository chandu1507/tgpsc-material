# Original explained MCQs — not claimed as official PYQs

Each question is newly authored for practice. The correct option and causal explanation follow the alternatives.

### MCQ 1. Which statement correctly distinguishes ecology from ecosystem?
A. Ecology is a discipline; ecosystem is a functional biotic–abiotic system  
B. Both are synonyms for a forest  
C. Ecology is abiotic and ecosystem only biotic  
D. Ecosystem refers only to animals
**Answer: A.** Ecology studies interactions; an ecosystem is a unit containing living organisms, physical environment and exchanges.

### MCQ 2. A niche differs from a habitat mainly because a niche describes:
A. The place occupied  
B. Functional role and resource use  
C. Only altitude  
D. Only rainfall
**Answer: B.** Habitat is location; niche includes feeding, interactions, tolerances and resource utilisation.

### MCQ 3. Which expression gives net primary productivity?
A. GPP + respiration  
B. GPP − autotrophic respiration  
C. Consumer biomass − producer biomass  
D. Total carbon in soil
**Answer: B.** NPP subtracts producers’ own respiratory losses from gross fixation.

### MCQ 4. Why is an energy pyramid always upright?
A. Producers always outnumber consumers  
B. Useful energy is lost at each transfer  
C. All biomass pyramids are upright  
D. Detritivores do not respire
**Answer: B.** Thermodynamic losses reduce energy available at successively higher trophic levels.

### MCQ 5. A single tree supporting hundreds of insects illustrates which possible pattern?
A. Inverted pyramid of numbers  
B. Inverted energy pyramid  
C. No food chain  
D. No primary producer
**Answer: A.** One large producer can support many smaller consumers, so numbers need not form an upright pyramid.

### MCQ 6. An inverted aquatic biomass pyramid is possible mainly because phytoplankton:
A. Never photosynthesise  
B. Turn over rapidly despite low standing biomass  
C. Have zero respiration  
D. Are always larger than fish
**Answer: B.** A low producer stock with high turnover can sustain larger consumer standing biomass.

### MCQ 7. A grazing food chain begins with:
A. Dead litter  
B. Living primary producers  
C. Top carnivores  
D. Mineral nutrients alone
**Answer: B.** Grazing pathways start with living autotrophic biomass; detrital chains start with dead organic material.

### MCQ 8. In a food-web diagram arrows conventionally point:
A. From predator to prey  
B. From food to consumer  
C. From decomposer to sunlight  
D. From higher to lower biomass
**Answer: B.** Arrows show direction of energy transfer, from the organism eaten to the eater.

### MCQ 9. Biomagnification refers to:
A. Growth of body size  
B. Rising concentration of certain contaminants across trophic levels  
C. Plant photosynthesis  
D. Increase in water salinity alone
**Answer: B.** Persistent poorly eliminated contaminants may become more concentrated in higher-level consumers.

### MCQ 10. Which organism primarily mineralises complex organic matter?
A. Only herbivores  
B. Bacteria and fungi  
C. Only top predators  
D. Only birds
**Answer: B.** Microbial decomposers release inorganic nutrients; detritivores mainly fragment material.

### MCQ 11. Which process returns atmospheric nitrogen from nitrate under suitable conditions?
A. Photosynthesis  
B. Denitrification  
C. Transpiration  
D. Nitrification
**Answer: B.** Denitrifying microbes reduce nitrogen oxides to gaseous forms, closing part of the nitrogen cycle.

### MCQ 12. Which nutrient is commonly important in freshwater eutrophication and has no large biological gaseous reservoir?
A. Phosphorus  
B. Oxygen  
C. Hydrogen  
D. Helium
**Answer: A.** Phosphorus enters via runoff, fertiliser and sediments; it often limits freshwater primary production.

### MCQ 13. Which statement about carrying capacity K is most accurate?
A. K never changes  
B. K varies with resources, season and habitat  
C. K is the number of species  
D. K equals rainfall in millimetres
**Answer: B.** Carrying capacity is an ecological approximation dependent on habitat quality and environmental conditions.

### MCQ 14. Primary succession typically begins:
A. On developed soil after a harvest  
B. Where soil is absent or poorly formed  
C. Only in reservoirs  
D. Only after pesticide application
**Answer: B.** Primary succession starts on newly exposed substrate, whereas secondary succession follows disturbance with soil retained.

### MCQ 15. Which is a genetic-diversity example?
A. Multiple crop varieties of one species  
B. A river and a forest  
C. Number of bird species in a district  
D. Different habitats in one landscape
**Answer: A.** Varieties within a crop species represent genetic variation, not ecosystem or species diversity.

### MCQ 16. Alpha diversity is best described as:
A. Turnover between habitats  
B. Diversity within a local habitat  
C. Global extinction rate  
D. Total regional GDP
**Answer: B.** Alpha refers to local diversity; beta measures compositional change; gamma describes regional diversity.

### MCQ 17. Two sites have equal species richness but different dominance. Which is correct?
A. They must have equal evenness  
B. Their evenness and diversity indices may differ  
C. They must be the same ecosystem  
D. Richness counts biomass
**Answer: B.** Richness counts species but not the distribution of individuals across them.

### MCQ 18. An endemic species is one that:
A. Must be critically endangered  
B. Naturally occurs only in a particular geographic region  
C. Is always invasive  
D. Lives only in zoos
**Answer: B.** Endemism describes geographic restriction; it does not by itself establish threat status.

### MCQ 19. Which is an example of in situ conservation?
A. Seed bank  
B. Wildlife sanctuary protecting natural habitat  
C. Cryopreservation only  
D. Captive breeding enclosure
**Answer: B.** In situ measures conserve species in functioning habitats; ex situ maintains them outside natural settings.

### MCQ 20. Which statement about grassland afforestation is ecologically sound?
A. All grasslands are degraded forests  
B. Planting trees can harm native grassland biodiversity  
C. More trees always means more biodiversity  
D. Grasslands lack ecosystem services
**Answer: B.** Natural grasslands support specialist biota; unsuitable tree planting can change hydrology and displace species.

### MCQ 21. What does habitat fragmentation most directly threaten?
A. Only atmospheric oxygen  
B. Connectivity and population gene flow  
C. Only the colour of leaves  
D. Only nitrogen fixation
**Answer: B.** Fragmentation isolates populations and changes edges and movement, even if total vegetation area remains substantial.

### MCQ 22. Which is the best evidence of successful lake restoration?
A. Freshly painted promenade  
B. Improved water quality, native biota and hydrology over time  
C. Number of decorative lights  
D. One-day fish stocking
**Answer: B.** Ecological recovery requires functional, sustained indicators rather than cosmetic works.

### MCQ 23. The Telangana Biodiversity Board was constituted in which year according to its official site?
A. 1992  
B. 2002  
C. 2014  
D. 2025
**Answer: C.** The board states it was constituted in 2014 under section 22(1) of the Biological Diversity Act.

### MCQ 24. Which is NOT one of the CBD’s three broad objectives?
A. Conservation  
B. Sustainable use  
C. Fair benefit sharing  
D. Maximising fossil-fuel production
**Answer: D.** CBD objectives concern conservation, sustainable use and equitable benefit sharing.

### MCQ 25. Kunming–Montreal Target 3 principally concerns:
A. 30% effective area conservation by 2030  
B. 30% GDP growth  
C. 30% reduction in all taxes  
D. 30% increase in pesticide use
**Answer: A.** The target concerns effective, representative, connected and equitably governed conservation of areas.

### MCQ 26. Which best describes the 2019 IPBES one-million figure?
A. Species already extinct  
B. An estimate of species threatened with extinction  
C. Count of protected areas  
D. India’s endemic species total
**Answer: B.** It estimates extinction risk, with uncertainty; it is not a census of species already extinct.

### MCQ 27. What distinguishes resistance from resilience?
A. Resistance limits change; resilience enables recovery/reorganisation  
B. They are identical  
C. Resistance concerns only insects  
D. Resilience means no disturbance occurs
**Answer: A.** Resistance describes withstanding change; resilience describes capacity to absorb and recover while retaining functions.

### MCQ 28. A species detected by eDNA is necessarily:
A. A confirmed breeding population  
B. Evidence of genetic traces, requiring additional interpretation  
C. An invasive species  
D. A top predator
**Answer: B.** eDNA supports presence inference but not exact abundance or reproduction without corroboration.

### MCQ 29. Which is the best management response to persistent tank eutrophication?
A. Only paint the embankment  
B. Control catchment nutrients and consider sediment/food-web dynamics  
C. Introduce random exotic fish  
D. Drain once and declare permanent success
**Answer: B.** External nutrient inputs and internal sediment recycling both may sustain eutrophic conditions.

### MCQ 30. Which assessment best captures ecosystem restoration?
A. Number of saplings distributed  
B. Native regeneration, hydrology, habitat functions and social impacts  
C. Amount spent on publicity  
D. Number of concrete paths
**Answer: B.** Outcome-based monitoring covers ecological processes and affected communities, not activity totals alone.

### Review of existing MCQ 3 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT Chapter 12 §12.2 states NPP = GPP minus producer respiration; this is a rate of net organic production. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A adds rather than subtracts producer respiratory losses.
- **B:** B is the stated definition of net primary productivity.
- **C:** C compares consumer and producer standing stocks, not producer production rates.
- **D:** D describes a soil carbon stock, not net primary productivity.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 4 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT §12.5 states energy pyramids cannot be inverted because energy dissipates at each trophic transfer; this does not require numerical pyramids to be upright. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A confuses number pyramids with energy pyramids; a tree may support many insects.
- **B:** B captures dissipative energy transfer and is correct.
- **C:** C incorrectly generalises biomass pyramids, which can invert in aquatic systems.
- **D:** D is false: detritivores and decomposers respire.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 5 — keyed A

**Key proposition:** NCERT §12.5 explicitly uses the example of many insects feeding on one large tree as an exception to an upright pyramid of numbers. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A is a possible inverted number-pyramid pattern.
- **B:** B is ruled out by the always-upright energy pyramid.
- **C:** C is false because producer-to-insect feeding constitutes a food-chain link.
- **D:** D is false because the tree is the primary producer.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 6 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT Fig.12.4(c) depicts low phytoplankton standing biomass supporting higher zooplankton biomass; rapid producer turnover is the explanatory mechanism, not a rate measured by that figure. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A is false: phytoplankton are photosynthetic producers.
- **B:** B is the plausible high-turnover explanation for low standing stock.
- **C:** C is false: phytoplankton respire.
- **D:** D is false: phytoplankton are generally microscopic, not larger than fish.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 7 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT §12.4 distinguishes a grazing chain beginning with living producers from a detritus chain beginning with dead organic matter. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A is the starting material of a detrital chain, not a grazing chain.
- **B:** B correctly identifies living autotrophic production.
- **C:** C is a higher trophic consumer, not the starting trophic source.
- **D:** D supplies mineral nutrients but not the organic food initiating grazing.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 8 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT Fig.12.3 and §12.4 show energy passing from plants to herbivores to carnivores; food-web arrows indicate transfer to the consumer. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A reverses the direction of trophic transfer.
- **B:** B follows energy flow from eaten organism to eater.
- **C:** C is physically incoherent: decomposers do not supply solar radiation.
- **D:** D treats biomass ranking as arrow direction; food-web arrows indicate feeding/energy transfer.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 10 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** NCERT §12.3 identifies bacterial and fungal enzymes in detrital catabolism and microbial mineralisation. [NCERT-12]

- **A:** A herbivores consume living plant matter and do not alone mineralise complex detritus.
- **B:** B identifies principal microbial decomposers.
- **C:** C top predators are consumers, not the primary mineralising community.
- **D:** D birds are consumers; 'only birds' excludes the documented microbial agents.

**Primary source:** [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 11 — keyed B

**Key proposition:** USGS Nutrients in the Nation's Streams and Groundwater describes bacteria reducing nitrate to nitrogen gas under low-oxygen, organic-rich conditions. [USGS-2010]

- **A:** A photosynthesis fixes carbon; it is not nitrate-to-nitrogen-gas reduction.
- **B:** B is denitrification, the process described by USGS.
- **C:** C transpiration moves water through plants, not nitrate to atmospheric nitrogen.
- **D:** D nitrification oxidises reduced nitrogen toward nitrite/nitrate rather than reducing nitrate to N2.

**Primary source:** [USGS-2010](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2010/3078/).

### Review of existing MCQ 12 — keyed A

**Key proposition:** EPA phosphorus indicator links phosphorus loading to freshwater algal blooms and hypoxia; NCERT describes the phosphorus cycle as sedimentary with Earth's crust as reservoir. [EPA-P;NCERT-12]

- **A:** A is the nutrient and cycle described by both sources.
- **B:** B oxygen has a major atmospheric reservoir and is not the intended freshwater limiting nutrient.
- **C:** C hydrogen occurs extensively in water and is not the named phosphorus-like limiting nutrient.
- **D:** D helium is an inert noble gas, not an essential eutrophication nutrient.

**Primary source:** [EPA-P](https://www.epa.gov/national-aquatic-resource-surveys/indicators-phosphorus) and [NCERT-12](https://ncert.gov.in/textbook/pdf/lebo112.pdf).

### Review of existing MCQ 23 — keyed C

**Key proposition:** Telangana Biodiversity Board's official home page identifies G.O.Ms.No.21 dated 25 October 2014 and gazette notification 5 November 2014. [TGBB-2014]

- **A:** A 1992 predates the Biological Diversity Act and the state board.
- **B:** B 2002 is the parent Act's year, not Telangana Board constitution.
- **C:** C 2014 is the board's stated constitution year.
- **D:** D 2025 is not the stated constitution year; it must not be confused with later institutional events.

**Primary source:** [TGBB-2014](https://tgbiodiversity.org/).

## Q17 — UNFCCC common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC), key B
- **A incorrect:** The Convention explicitly recognises differences in historical contributions and capabilities; it does not prescribe identical historical responsibility.
- **B supported:** Article 3(1) provides for equity, CBDR-RC and developed-country leadership.
- **C incorrect:** Article 3 does not restrict climate action to developing countries; the framework applies to Parties, with differentiated obligations.
- **D incorrect:** Equity is stated in Article 3(1), so it cannot be excluded from climate policy.
- **Primary source:** UNFCCC (1992), Convention, Article 3(1), https://unfccc.int/resource/docs/convkp/conveng.pdf . **Boundary:** This confirms the Convention principle; it does not by itself determine a numerical contemporary finance obligation.

## Q18 — Paris Agreement temperature objective, key B
- **A incorrect:** Three degrees Celsius is not the Article 2 objective.
- **B supported:** Article 2(1)(a) seeks to hold global average warming well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts towards 1.5°C.
- **C incorrect:** The baseline is pre-industrial, not 2020.
- **D incorrect:** Article 2 describes a global long-term temperature objective, not a 1.5°C annual ceiling imposed separately on each country.
- **Primary source:** UNFCCC, Paris Agreement (2015), Article 2, https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement . **Boundary:** Do not infer that one annual global exceedance legally terminates the Agreement.

## Q19 — nationally determined contributions, key B
- **A incorrect:** An NDC is a Party's communicated plan; an IPCC assessment is scientific synthesis, not a national pledge.
- **B supported:** The Paris Agreement implementation system uses Parties' nationally determined contributions to communicate mitigation actions and adaptation information.
- **C incorrect:** NDCs are not restricted to forest cover; mitigation covers emissions and other measures, with adaptation information also communicated.
- **D incorrect:** NDCs are associated with the Paris Agreement, not solely the earlier Kyoto Protocol.
- **Primary source:** UNFCCC Paris Agreement explainer, NDC section, https://unfccc.int/process-and-meetings/the-paris-agreement . **Boundary:** National determination does not imply that every stated numerical target is legally enforceable as a guaranteed achieved outcome.

## Q22 — India's 2070 net-zero objective, key B
- **A incorrect:** 2070 is a future target year, not an achievement recorded in 2026.
- **B supported:** India's 2022 long-term low-carbon development strategy explicitly discusses the transition to net zero by 2070.
- **C incorrect:** The Paris Agreement was adopted in 2015 and entered into force in 2016, not 2070.
- **D incorrect:** The strategy is economy-wide and addresses energy, transport, industry, carbon removal and forests; it is not a forest-area-only target.
- **Primary sources:** Government of India (2022), LT-LEDS, section 2.7, https://unfccc.int/sites/default/files/resource/India_LTLEDS.pdf ; UNFCCC submission registry (14 November 2022), https://unfccc.int/documents/623511 . **Boundary:** This is a policy goal, not proof of delivery or an unconditional 2035 absolute-emissions reduction.

## Q23 — Sustainable Development Goal for climate action, key A
- **A supported:** SDG 13 expressly addresses climate action and impacts.
- **B incorrect:** SDG 4 concerns education.
- **C incorrect:** SDG 16 concerns peace, justice and institutions.
- **D incorrect:** SDG 9 concerns industry, innovation and infrastructure.
- **Primary source:** UN General Assembly, 2030 Agenda (2015), goals listing, https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda . **Boundary:** Climate change also intersects other SDGs; the stem asks for the *most directly named* goal.

## Q24 — SDG and target inventory, key B
- **A incorrect:** Eight goals and 21 targets do not match the adopted 2030 Agenda.
- **B supported:** The 2030 Agenda enumerates **17 goals and 169 targets**.
- **C incorrect:** Twenty goals and 100 targets are not the adopted inventory.
- **D incorrect:** Twelve goals and 150 targets are not the adopted inventory.
- **Primary source:** UN General Assembly, 2030 Agenda (2015), preamble, https://sdgs.un.org/2030agenda . **Boundary:** The number of indicators is a separate, revisable statistical framework; do not conflate it with 169 targets.

## Q25 — intergenerational equity, key A
- **A supported:** UNFCCC Article 3(1) explicitly requires protection of the climate system for the benefit of present and future generations, on the basis of equity.
- **B incorrect:** Opportunity cost is a decision-analysis concept, not the named ethical principle governing obligations to future generations.
- **C incorrect:** Exclusively short-run efficiency can disregard long-term ecological burdens.
- **D incorrect:** Non-intervention is not the stated principle of intergenerational climate responsibility.
- **Primary source:** UNFCCC Convention (1992), Article 3(1), https://unfccc.int/resource/docs/convkp/conveng.pdf . **Boundary:** The provision supports the principle; it does not specify a unique discount rate or legal remedy.

## Q27 — just transition, key B
- **A incorrect:** The ILO framework does not demand immediate termination of every fossil-sector job; it emphasises managed structural change.
- **B supported:** The ILO calls for decent work, social protection, skills development and dialogue among governments, employers and workers during transitions.
- **C incorrect:** Replacing public transport with private cars is neither a defining worker-protection measure nor a just-transition objective.
- **D incorrect:** Not every green investment is adaptation; adaptation and mitigation are analytically distinct.
- **Primary source:** ILO (2015 guidelines; 2016 publication), https://www.ilo.org/publications/guidelines-just-transition-towards-environmentally-sustainable-economies . **Boundary:** This supports the definition, not a claim that any specific Telangana programme has delivered a just transition.

## Q29 — methane and near-term climate action, key B
- **A incorrect:** Methane (CH4) is a greenhouse gas and an important short-lived climate forcer.
- **B supported:** IPCC explains that reducing methane contributes to limiting warming, alongside necessary sustained CO2 reductions.
- **C incorrect:** Methane is relatively short-lived compared with the cumulative long-term warming associated with CO2; it does not remain forever.
- **D incorrect:** IPCC pathways require deep CO2 cuts; methane mitigation does not make CO2 reduction unnecessary.
- **Primary source:** IPCC, *Global Warming of 1.5°C* (2018), chapter 4, section 4.3.6, https://www.ipcc.ch/sr15/chapter/chapter-4/ . **Boundary:** Avoid a fixed methane lifetime or warming multiplier unless its assessment time horizon is specified.

## Q30 — climate scenarios, key B
- **A incorrect:** A scenario is not one inevitable future.
- **B supported:** IPCC defines scenarios as plausible descriptions based on internally consistent assumptions, not forecasts or predictions.
- **C incorrect:** Scenario analysis does not eliminate local model, socioeconomic or natural variability uncertainty.
- **D incorrect:** Multi-decade scenarios are not interchangeable with daily operational weather forecasts.
- **Primary source:** IPCC AR6 WGII (2022), Annex I glossary, https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/annex-i/ . **Boundary:** Do not assign numerical likelihood to a scenario without a separately justified probabilistic framework.

## Thirty original explained MCQs (not official PYQs)

### MCQ 1. Which statement correctly distinguishes climate from weather?

A. Climate is a single day of atmospheric conditions
B. Climate describes long-term distributions of weather
C. Weather is always regional while climate is always global
D. Climate excludes variability

**Answer: B.** Climate describes statistical characteristics and variability over extended periods; a single hot or cold day is weather. Climate analysis uses defined baselines and spatial scales.

### MCQ 2. Which gas is best described as a feedback rather than the principal anthropogenic forcing?

A. Carbon dioxide
B. Methane
C. Water vapour
D. Nitrous oxide

**Answer: C.** Water vapour increases as air warms and amplifies warming; anthropogenic emissions of long-lived greenhouse gases drive the initial enhanced greenhouse effect.

### MCQ 3. What does a positive radiative forcing generally indicate?

A. A cooling tendency
B. An added warming influence on the energy balance
C. No change in ocean heat
D. A fall in greenhouse gases

**Answer: B.** Positive forcing increases net energy retained by the climate system, although the surface response is delayed by ocean heat uptake and other feedbacks.

### MCQ 4. What is the most defensible interpretation of a single year above 1.5°C?

A. The Paris Agreement has legally ceased
B. Long-term warming is automatically above 1.5°C
C. It is a warning signal but not alone the long-term Paris metric
D. Global warming has ended

**Answer: C.** Paris temperature goals refer to sustained long-term change. Annual variability can produce a single-year exceedance before a long-term threshold is assessed.

### MCQ 5. Which observation most directly captures accumulated planetary heat?

A. Daily minimum temperature in one city
B. Ocean heat content
C. One winter snowfall
D. Single cyclone landfall

**Answer: B.** The ocean stores most excess energy and ocean heat content integrates warming across years, making it a powerful long-term climate indicator.

### MCQ 6. Which mechanism is an example of positive climate feedback?

A. Melting ice exposes darker water that absorbs more sunlight
B. Cooling reduces snow cover
C. Volcanic aerosols increase solar input
D. Urban shade increases surface absorption

**Answer: A.** The ice–albedo feedback reinforces initial warming because dark water absorbs more solar energy than reflective ice.

### MCQ 7. Which claim about climate tipping points is scientifically careful?

A. Every tipping threshold is known to a calendar year
B. They are possible state shifts with uncertain thresholds
C. Any flood proves a tipping point
D. All tipping elements have already shifted

**Answer: B.** Some Earth-system components may change nonlinearly; thresholds and timings are uncertain, so policy combines precaution with ongoing observation.

### MCQ 8. Why can warming increase both drought and heavy rainfall risks?

A. Rainfall must rise equally everywhere
B. Circulation and moisture availability change unevenly
C. Evaporation stops in warm climates
D. All regions become uniformly wet

**Answer: B.** A warmer atmosphere can carry more moisture, but circulation, land conditions and seasonality determine where intense rain or prolonged dryness occurs.

### MCQ 9. Which is NOT a sound inference from a rise in renewable installed capacity?

A. The power system may need more flexibility
B. Grid integration matters
C. Renewable electricity generation share must equal capacity share
D. Storage can aid balancing

**Answer: C.** Capacity measures maximum rated output, not energy produced. Capacity factors, curtailment and dispatch determine actual generation.

### MCQ 10. An emissions-intensity target is measured as

A. Total emissions without denominator
B. Emissions divided by GDP
C. Installed solar capacity divided by population
D. Forest area divided by GDP

**Answer: B.** Emissions intensity expresses emissions per unit economic output; total emissions may still grow when GDP grows faster than intensity falls.

### MCQ 11. Which choice is an adaptation measure?

A. Reducing methane leakage from gas systems
B. Issuing heat-health alerts and opening cooling centres
C. Replacing coal with solar solely to cut CO2
D. Capturing cement process emissions

**Answer: B.** Heat-health alerts lower exposure to an existing climate hazard; the other choices principally target the emissions driving future warming.

### MCQ 12. Which is a classic maladaptation risk?

A. Repairing urban drains
B. Unlimited drought-time groundwater extraction without recharge planning
C. Monitoring hospital heat cases
D. Protecting natural wetlands

**Answer: B.** Unregulated extraction may buffer one season but deplete aquifers and increase future vulnerability, especially for households with shallow wells.

### MCQ 13. Which Indian state-level institution is identified as a Telangana climate nodal agency?

A. Reserve Bank of India
B. EPTRI
C. Election Commission of India
D. NHAI

**Answer: B.** Telangana government designated EPTRI as a climate-change nodal institution through a 2016 government order; implementation spans multiple departments.

### MCQ 14. What is a heat island?

A. Global greenhouse warming only
B. Local urban warming linked to built form and land cover
C. A tropical cyclone over a city
D. A seasonal ocean-current reversal

**Answer: B.** Urban materials, reduced vegetation and human heat release can make built-up areas hotter than surrounding areas; this interacts with global warming.

### MCQ 15. Which is a suitable indicator for heat adaptation?

A. Number of slogans printed
B. Measured heat illness and vulnerable-population coverage
C. Installed solar capacity alone
D. Number of saplings purchased

**Answer: B.** Outcome-oriented monitoring should assess exposure reduction, hospital cases and whether alerts and cooling measures reach high-risk groups.

### MCQ 16. Which statement about forest-carbon projects is correct?

A. Seedling count proves permanent removals
B. Additionality and permanence matter for credible carbon claims
C. All monocultures equal natural forests
D. Community rights are irrelevant

**Answer: B.** Carbon claims require baseline comparisons, survival and reversal risk, leakage checks and independent verification, alongside ecological and social safeguards.

### MCQ 17. Which statement best describes the UNFCCC principle CBDR-RC?

A. All countries have identical historical responsibility
B. Responsibilities and capacities differ across countries
C. Only developing countries must act
D. Climate policy excludes equity

**Answer: B.** CBDR-RC recognises unequal contributions and capabilities while supporting collective climate action; it informs debates on finance and fair burden-sharing.

### MCQ 18. What is the Paris Agreement temperature goal?

A. Exactly 3°C
B. Well below 2°C while pursuing 1.5°C
C. No warming relative to 2020
D. A 1.5°C annual cap in every country

**Answer: B.** Article 2 sets a global long-term temperature objective, not an annual country-specific temperature limit.

### MCQ 19. Which statement about NDCs is correct?

A. They are the same as IPCC predictions
B. They are nationally communicated climate commitments
C. They measure only forest cover
D. They apply only to the Kyoto Protocol

**Answer: B.** Nationally determined contributions describe countries’ climate plans under the Paris architecture and are subject to communication and transparency processes.

### MCQ 20. India’s 2035 GDP emissions-intensity target relative to 2005 is

A. 15% reduction
B. 47% reduction
C. 100% absolute reduction
D. 60% reduction

**Answer: B.** The April 2026 NDC specifies a 47% emissions-intensity reduction by 2035 from the 2005 baseline; it is not a 47% cut in absolute emissions.

### MCQ 21. India’s 2035 non-fossil target refers to

A. Total final energy consumption
B. Installed electric-power capacity
C. All vehicle sales
D. Forest area

**Answer: B.** The official NDC targets about 60% of cumulative installed electric-power capacity from non-fossil resources by 2035, subject to stated support conditions.

### MCQ 22. What does India’s 2070 commitment describe?

A. A completed achievement in 2026
B. A long-term net-zero goal
C. The date the Paris Agreement started
D. A target for forest area alone

**Answer: B.** Net zero by 2070 is a future economy-wide aspiration and should not be confused with 2035 NDC intensity or capacity commitments.

### MCQ 23. Which goal most directly addresses climate action?

A. SDG 13
B. SDG 4
C. SDG 16
D. SDG 9

**Answer: A.** SDG 13 is Climate Action; related goals address energy, water, cities, consumption, ecosystems and partnerships.

### MCQ 24. How many SDGs and targets are in the 2030 Agenda?

A. 8 and 21
B. 17 and 169
C. 20 and 100
D. 12 and 150

**Answer: B.** The UN 2030 Agenda adopted in 2015 establishes 17 integrated Sustainable Development Goals and 169 targets.

### MCQ 25. Which principle addresses obligations to future generations?

A. Intergenerational equity
B. Opportunity cost alone
C. Strictly short-run efficiency
D. Non-intervention

**Answer: A.** Intergenerational equity requires that present decisions not unfairly undermine the opportunities and environmental conditions of future people.

### MCQ 26. Which is a key concern with carbon offsets?

A. They always cut fossil emissions at source
B. Additionality, leakage and permanence
C. They have no baseline requirements
D. They are equivalent to all forms of adaptation

**Answer: B.** An offset credit needs a credible counterfactual, no double counting and durable benefit; poor-quality credits can exaggerate real climate progress.

### MCQ 27. What is a just transition?

A. Ending all employment in fossil sectors immediately
B. Supporting workers and communities during structural change
C. Replacing every bus with a private car
D. Counting all green investment as adaptation

**Answer: B.** A just transition combines decarbonisation with social dialogue, worker protection, retraining and regional economic alternatives.

### MCQ 28. Which approach best evaluates a flood-resilience project?

A. Count concrete poured only
B. Track losses, exposure, maintenance and downstream impacts
C. Ignore land-use change
D. Attribute every flood solely to global climate change

**Answer: B.** Flood resilience depends on hazard and exposure as well as drainage, wetlands and governance; outcome indicators and spillovers are essential.

### MCQ 29. Why is methane reduction useful alongside CO2 reduction?

A. Methane is not a greenhouse gas
B. Methane has important near-term warming effects
C. Methane stays forever in the atmosphere
D. CO2 reduction is unnecessary

**Answer: B.** Methane is a potent, shorter-lived greenhouse gas; reducing it can slow near-term warming, while sustained CO2 cuts are necessary for long-term stabilisation.

### MCQ 30. Which statement is most accurate about climate scenarios?

A. They predict a single inevitable future
B. They explore outcomes under alternative assumptions
C. They eliminate local uncertainty
D. They are interchangeable with daily weather forecasts

**Answer: B.** Scenarios condition projections on future emissions, socioeconomic choices and model assumptions; they are tools for risk planning, not deterministic forecasts.

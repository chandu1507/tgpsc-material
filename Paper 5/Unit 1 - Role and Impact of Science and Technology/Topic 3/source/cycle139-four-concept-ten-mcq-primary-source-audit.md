## Four concept-depth repairs

### 10. Navigation and timing: distinguish geometry, integrity and user outcomes
A GNSS receiver estimates *pseudoranges* from radio-signal travel times, then solves for three coordinates and receiver-clock bias. Four independent observations are normally necessary; satellite geometry, ionospheric delay, multipath and interference determine practical accuracy. A location pin is not proof of precise positioning. Integrity means confidence that a fix is reliable within a specified alert limit, not merely a small advertised error. ISRO defines NavIC as a regional position, navigation and timing system serving India and about 1,500 km beyond its boundary. Standard Positioning Service is distinct from encrypted Restricted Service; L5 and S signals are supplemented by civilian L1 capability. A Telangana ambulance programme needs suitable receivers, maps, dispatch integration and arrival-time evidence. Jamming, spoofing and indoor obstruction require fallback procedures and independent checks. **Source:** [N01].

### 11. Disaster management: observations are not compensation decisions
A geostationary weather image tracks clouds; a radar scene may estimate flood extent at its acquisition time. Neither directly establishes an individual household's loss, the structural safety of a bridge or a legally compensable claim. Flood mapping must account for acquisition delay, radar shadows, built surfaces, vegetation and validation. ISRO reported an NRSC–HYDRAA MoU signed 11 April 2025 for geospatial support to waterbody protection and disaster management, including a Bhuvan portal and field data. **The MoU establishes intended cooperation, not measured reductions in flooding or encroachment.** An auditable chain is observation → corrected map → hydrological analysis → uncertainty and alert threshold → district decision → accessible warning → evacuation and verification. Compare false positives, warning lead time, field-confirmed boundaries and grievance outcomes before claiming benefit. **Sources:** [N02], [N03].

### 12. Reform: separate policy, authorisation, business and public benefit
Indian Space Policy 2023 distinguishes public research and development, authorisation of non-government activities and commercial interfaces. ISRO describes IN-SPACe as the nodal body promoting, enabling, authorising and supervising non-government space activities. Registration as a startup is not launch permission, spectrum coordination or proof of a viable service. Evidence of industrial impact requires qualified suppliers, tested products, recurring contracts and reliable delivery rather than counting announcements. Earth-observation procurement should state accuracy, update frequency, rights of access, cybersecurity and data-retention terms. Linking high-resolution maps to private records can expose sensitive information; field review and appeals remain essential before government action. **Sources:** [N04], [N05], [N06].

### 16. Synthesis: correct SPADEX's October 2026 status
INSAT's services, IRS Earth observation, EDUSAT educational communication and Chandrayaan-1 lunar science require different success metrics. A successful launch does not establish a welfare improvement. **ISRO announced on 23 October 2026 that both SPADEX spacecraft re-entered Earth's atmosphere on 12 October 2026.** The pair demonstrated docking in January 2025, undocking in March and second docking/power transfer in April 2025; they must not be described as active orbiting spacecraft in late October 2026. ISRO's February 2026 NISAR report describes demonstrated 100-metre soil-moisture *product resolution*, with field calibration and validation continuing. It does not imply 100-metre penetration into soil or exact farm-level truth. A nominal 12-day repeat is not continuous observation. Telangana applications should connect sensor data to field checks, decisions and measured outcomes. **Sources:** [N07], [N08], [N09].

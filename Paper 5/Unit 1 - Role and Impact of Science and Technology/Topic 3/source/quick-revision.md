## Standalone quick revision: facts, comparisons and traps

### Mission and institution facts

- **1962:** INCOSPAR established; **1969:** ISRO established; **1972:** Department of Space constituted. These are milestones, not three names for one organisation.
- **1975:** Aryabhata launched; **1975–76:** SITE educational television experiment; **1988:** IRS-1A launch.
- **INSAT:** system for communication and meteorological services. INSAT-3DS strengthens weather imaging, sounding, data relay and search-and-rescue functions.
- **IRS:** Earth-observation programme. Optical instruments observe reflected light; radar uses active microwaves; sensor choice depends on question and weather.
- **EDUSAT / GSAT-3:** launched **20 September 2004**, dedicated educational communications; not a current operational spacecraft.
- **Chandrayaan-1:** launched **22 October 2008**, PSLV-C11, eleven instruments; lost contact **29 August 2009**; important evidence of lunar hydroxyl/water signatures.
- **Chandrayaan-3:** soft landing **23 August 2023**, in-situ measurements; do not confuse with Chandrayaan-1's orbital mapping.
- **NISAR:** launched **30 July 2025**; NASA L-band + ISRO S-band SAR; science-phase and S-band product updates documented in 2025–26.
- **SPADEX:** demonstrated docking/undocking and later power transfer; this does **not** mean an Indian space station is already operating.
- **Gaganyaan:** multiple safety and separation tests reported during 2026; a qualification test is not a completed crewed flight.
- **NRSC:** located in Hyderabad with Shadnagar data reception/processing facilities; supports Earth-observation applications, not legal land-title adjudication.

### Concept contrasts

| Confused pair | Correct distinction |
|---|---|
| GEO vs polar orbit | Continuous apparent regional view vs repeated detailed overpasses |
| Geosynchronous vs geostationary | Equal orbital period vs special circular equatorial case |
| INSAT vs IRS | Communication/weather system vs Earth-observation programme |
| Satellite image vs forecast | Measurement vs model-based prediction with other data |
| NDVI vs yield | Vegetation signal vs harvested production requiring calibration |
| Tank spread vs volume | Area of visible water vs area-depth relationship |
| Radar vs optical | Active microwave return vs reflected solar radiation |
| Launch vs operation | Reaching orbit vs commissioning and delivering validated service |
| Chandrayaan-1 vs Chandrayaan-3 | Orbital mapping vs successful landing and local rover experiments |
| IN-SPACe vs NSIL | Authorisation/facilitation vs commercial business functions |

### Five examiner-ready Telangana examples

1. **Dryland crops:** optical/radar stress mapping plus rainfall and field verification, followed by feasible extension advice.
2. **Tanks:** multi-date surface-water maps prioritise inspection; field depth surveys establish storage, and community checks assess access.
3. **Hyderabad flooding:** imagery supports exposure and flood-extent mapping; stormwater modelling and field observations govern immediate response.
4. **Industrial planning:** geospatial layers identify flood/heat/land-use constraints; statutory planning and community review remain essential.
5. **Remote education:** satellite links may carry content, but teachers, local-language support, interaction and learning assessment determine value.

### Frequent mistakes to avoid

Do not claim a satellite can identify a legal owner, a tenant, a confirmed crop yield or a household's compensation entitlement. Do not call EDUSAT a new 2026 satellite. Do not describe Chandrayaan-1 as a lunar lander. Do not treat the NISAR 100-metre soil-moisture demonstration as a universally proven irrigation service. Do not confuse satellite revisit interval with continuous imaging. Do not present an announced future mission as accomplished. Always pair the technological promise with validation and responsible institutions.

## Cycle 140: ten existing MCQs — source-correlated four-option audit (31 October 2026)

**Scope and provenance.** These are original practice questions, **not official TGPSC previous-year questions**. The following author-side checks evaluate the existing correct proposition and *each* of its three distractors; they do not constitute independent academic approval. Questions Q1, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q10, Q11, Q16, Q19, Q24 and Q28 were selected without changing their stems, choices or keys. Source dates refer to the issuing documents, not to a newly claimed observation on 31 October 2026. ### Q1 — Flood polygon to evacuation (key B)
- **A incorrect:** A classified SAR pixel is not a verified household-level flood observation. NRSC's 20 August 2025 Mancherial map explicitly calls its results preliminary and says no ground verification was performed.
- **B supported:** Combine acquisition timestamp, map uncertainty, river gauges and field reports before authoritative village-level action. NRSC describes disaster support as integrating satellite information with field observations and state agencies.
- **C incorrect:** Optical imaging can be delayed by monsoon clouds; waiting regardless of urgency can withhold timely information. NRSC explains why microwave observations are useful in cloudy flood conditions.
- **D incorrect:** NDVI is a vegetation greenness index, not a sole flood-extent or evacuation criterion. USGS defines its red/NIR inputs.
**Evidence:** [NRSC disaster support](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS.php); [NRSC Mancherial rapid map, 20 Aug 2025](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2025/TS/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs_map.pdf); [USGS NDVI](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-normalized-difference-vegetation-index). **Limit:** The sources support the workflow, not a specific evacuation order or the correctness of every pixel.

### Q2 — NDVI definition (key B)
- **A incorrect:** NDVI alone does not directly measure harvested tonnes; yield estimation requires crop type, area, agronomic calibration and validation.
- **B supported:** USGS specifies NDVI = (NIR − red)/(NIR + red), a spectral ratio indicating vegetation greenness.
- **C incorrect:** USGS notes soil-background sensitivity and saturation in dense vegetation; seasonal harvest also changes observed canopy.
- **D incorrect:** River depth is not inferred from the red/NIR vegetation index.
**Evidence:** [USGS Landsat NDVI formula](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-normalized-difference-vegetation-index); [USGS phenology and limitations, 27 Nov 2018](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/remote-sensing-phenology/science/ndvi-foundation-remote-sensing-phenology). **Limit:** Spectral greenness is not a direct crop-yield measurement.

### Q3 — Operational warning roles (key B)
- **A incorrect:** INCOIS runs tsunami early warning and ocean information, not statutory cyclone landfall orders.
- **B supported:** IMD's RSMC New Delhi cyclone standard operating procedures describe cyclone forecasts and multi-stage warning bulletins.
- **C incorrect:** NRSC supplies flood maps and decision-support inputs; it is not the statutory district evacuation authority.
- **D incorrect:** HYDRAA is a Telangana urban-governance body, not India's national tsunami advisory service. INCOIS identifies the Hyderabad-based ITEWC.
**Evidence:** [IMD RSMC cyclone SOP](https://rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in/images/pdf/sop.pdf); [NRSC DMS responsibilities](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS.php); [INCOIS ITEWC](https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/abouttsunamiready.jsp). **Limit:** Do not infer a specific legal evacuation delegation from an agency website alone.

### Q4 — SAR in monsoon flooding (key A)
- **A supported:** NRSC explicitly identifies microwave satellite data as especially useful during flood events because optical imagery is limited by clouds; active radar also operates without sunlight.
- **B incorrect:** Radar cannot always see through concrete: buildings, geometry and shadowing complicate urban interpretation.
- **C incorrect:** Radar backscatter does not directly measure disease incidence; health surveillance requires separate epidemiological evidence.
- **D incorrect:** SAR is active microwave sensing and does not require uninterrupted sunlight.
**Evidence:** [NRSC flood mapping and microwave advantage](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS.php); [ISRO EOS-04 C-band SAR product, 6 Feb 2024](https://www.isro.gov.in/High-Resolution_Operational_Soil_Moisture_Product.html). **Limit:** Cloud tolerance is not a guarantee of error-free water classification.

### Q10 — Tsunami mechanism and multiple waves (key C)
- **A incorrect:** Cyclone storm surge is driven by weather; tsunami waves are commonly generated by sudden ocean displacement associated with earthquakes or other disturbances.
- **B incorrect:** UNESCO-IOC warns that a tsunami is not a single wave; the first is not necessarily the largest.
- **C supported:** Seabed displacement can generate a sequence of waves. INCOIS integrates seismic, sea-level and numerical modelling evidence.
- **D incorrect:** The Indian Ocean experienced the 26 December 2004 tsunami, which prompted establishment of the Indian warning centre.
**Evidence:** [UNESCO-IOC tsunami protective guidance](https://tsunami.ioc.unesco.org/en/what-do); [INCOIS centre history and sensors](https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/abouttsunamiready.jsp). **Limit:** Not every tsunami is earthquake-generated; landslides and volcanic activity are other possible triggers.

### Q11 — Acquisition time versus dashboard time (key B)
- **A incorrect:** Replacing a map display timestamp does not improve spatial resolution or sensor capability.
- **B supported:** A previous-day acquisition presented as current risks a false real-time impression. NRSC flood-map products identify the image pass time and state scale, resolution, date and interpretation limitations.
- **C incorrect:** A timestamp does not turn a map into a legally binding order or validated cadastral record.
- **D incorrect:** Display metadata cannot change the soil-moisture state observed by a satellite.
**Evidence:** [NRSC Mancherial map with acquisition time and preliminary disclaimer](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2025/TS/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs_map.pdf); [NRSC NDEM decision support](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS.php). **Limit:** The scenario is hypothetical, not an accusation about a named operational dashboard.

### Q16 — 500-m soil-moisture grid (key B)
- **A incorrect:** A 500-m product pixel is not a direct in-situ measurement for every tiny agricultural plot.
- **B supported:** ISRO's 6 February 2024 EOS-04 SAR soil-moisture announcement specifies 500-m spatial granularity; smaller fields may vary within a cell, requiring local checks.
- **C incorrect:** The cited ISRO product explicitly uses C-band SAR; radar is therefore compatible with this measurement.
- **D incorrect:** A gridded environmental estimate is not an authoritative cadastral boundary.
**Evidence:** [ISRO high-resolution operational soil-moisture product, 6 Feb 2024](https://www.isro.gov.in/High-Resolution_Operational_Soil_Moisture_Product.html). **Limit:** ISRO's stated product-validation figure must not be interpreted as accuracy for each individual field.

### Q19 — Rapid NRSC flood map limitations (key B)
- **A incorrect:** NRSC's dated flood-inundation map is not a final property ownership or cadastral record.
- **B supported:** The actual 20 August 2025 Telangana map says it is preliminary rapid mapping and that no ground verification was done.
- **C incorrect:** Flood-inundation mapping is distinct from INCOIS tsunami advisories.
- **D incorrect:** A flood map does not automatically determine household compensation, which needs administrative assessment.
**Evidence:** [NRSC 20 Aug 2025 Mancherial flood map, map no. 2025/FL/TS/01/20082025](https://ndem.nrsc.gov.in/documents/Disaster_Document/2025/TS/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs/tsflood50dsc20082025_0600hrs_map.pdf); [NRSC disaster support](https://www.nrsc.gov.in/nrscnew/Apps_DMS.php). **Limit:** No assertion is made about a 2026 flood or compensation outcome.

### Q24 — Land-surface temperature versus air temperature (key B)
- **A incorrect:** USGS defines Landsat surface temperature as the temperature of Earth's surface; it is not identical to shaded near-surface air temperature.
- **B supported:** Residents' experienced heat depends on air temperature, humidity, wind, radiation and exposure, not only a satellite surface-temperature pixel.
- **C incorrect:** USGS specifically identifies urban heat-island monitoring as an application of Landsat surface temperature.
- **D incorrect:** A thermal image alone cannot prove the complete causal chain behind every heatwave.
**Evidence:** [USGS Landsat Surface Temperature](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-surface-temperature); [USGS surface-temperature science product](https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-collection-2-surface-temperature). **Limit:** This checks the distinction of measured variables; it does not validate a local heat-health model.

### Q28 — Natural tsunami warning and immediate action (key B)
- **A incorrect:** UNESCO-IOC states that tsunami waves may arrive before an official alert; waiting for a satellite photograph is unsafe.
- **B supported:** After strong or prolonged coastal shaking, move inland or to high ground immediately, and continue following official instructions.
- **C incorrect:** UNESCO-IOC explicitly warns against going to the shore to watch waves.
- **D incorrect:** Tsunamis can involve multiple dangerous waves for hours; the first wave does not mark an all-clear.
**Evidence:** [UNESCO-IOC, What to do](https://tsunami.ioc.unesco.org/en/what-do); [INCOIS national warning centre](https://tsunami.incois.gov.in/TEWS/abouttsunamiready.jsp). **Limit:** This is general coastal safety guidance, not an active warning for inland Telangana.

## Cycle 141 — bounded four-option audit of ten existing original practice MCQs (1 November 2026)

**Scope and method.** These are author-side technical checks, not official TGPSC PYQs, not independent academic certification and not a full R5 pass. The original stems, options and keyed letters are preserved. For each item, all four options are evaluated against the cited technical source or an explicitly shown calculation. An obviously implausible distractor is flagged rather than counted as a high-quality examination alternative. Dates below identify the review, not the original publication of an older reference.

**Q1 — B (power versus energy).** **A:** Reverses the units: MW is power, not annual energy; MWh is energy, not instantaneous load. **B:** Correct: MW is a rate and MWh integrates that rate over time; 2 MW sustained for 3 hours yields 6 MWh. **C:** False because MW and MWh are used for solar, hydro, nuclear and demand as well as fossil generation. **D:** False because MWh depends on duration; at fixed MW, doubling operating hours doubles MWh. **Evidence:** E01. **Quality flag:** distractors C/D are easy; a future revision should contrast peak MW, average MW and MWh with realistic numbers.

**Q2 — C (capacity factor).** **A:** 25% would correspond to 109,500 MWh for this 50-MW, 8,760-hour example. **B:** 40% corresponds to 175,200 MWh. **C:** Correct: 50 MW × 8,760 h = 438,000 MWh maximum; 219,000/438,000 = 0.50. **D:** 80% would require 350,400 MWh. **Evidence:** E01, E02 and reproducible arithmetic. **Qualification:** capacity factor is not identical to equipment availability; downtime, dispatch and resource affect output.

**Q6 — B (hydropower head/flow).** **A:** Reservoir surface area alone cannot specify power; the usable flow and net head are absent. **B:** Correct: hydraulic power is proportional to density × gravity × flow × effective head, reduced by conversion losses; efficiency matters. **C:** Water colour does not appear in the energy-balance equation. **D:** Rotor speed alone does not determine delivered power without torque, head, flow and efficiency. **Evidence:** E03. **Quality flag:** C is a non-discriminating distractor.

**Q7 — B (pumped hydro).** **A:** A closed-cycle pumped-storage plant is not a perpetual energy source; pumping requires input and round-trip losses occur. **B:** Correct: electricity pumps water to an upper reservoir and later turbine generation recovers part of the stored energy. **C:** Tidal stations draw on marine tidal flows, not the defining pumped-storage cycle. **D:** A fuel cell consumes chemical reactants, unlike elevation-based water storage. **Evidence:** E04. **Qualification:** natural inflows in an open-loop project can complicate the accounting; this question describes the storage cycle, not every hydrologic input.

**Q10 — A (solar inverter).** **A:** Correct: PV modules produce DC, and the inverter produces grid-compatible AC and can provide grid-support functions. **B:** A power-electronic converter does not chemically convert sunlight into coal. **C:** Inversion is conversion, not seasonal energy storage; batteries are separate equipment. **D:** An inverter cannot generate wind; wind energy requires a separate aerodynamic conversion system. **Evidence:** E05. **Quality flag:** B/D are implausible, so keyed correctness is stronger than distractor quality.

**Q12 — A (wind speed sensitivity).** **A:** Correct for available wind-stream kinetic power at fixed air density and swept area: ½ρAv³; real turbine output is bounded by cut-in, rated and cut-out regimes. **B:** False because resource power varies strongly with speed. **C:** False: at equal wind speed the kinetic power passing through the rotor grows with swept area. **D:** False because turbines do not produce rated output at all speeds. **Evidence:** E06. **Qualification:** the cubic law is not a universal annual-yield equation; use a site wind-speed distribution and turbine power curve.

**Q13 — A (Betz limit).** **A:** Correct: the ideal actuator-disk rotor maximum power coefficient is 16/27 ≈ 59.3%, before practical losses. **B:** Reservoir evaporation is a hydrology problem, not the Betz limit. **C:** Nuclear enrichment is a fuel-cycle process with no relation to wind momentum extraction. **D:** Ethanol blending targets are policy/fuel measures, not wind-turbine aerodynamics. **Evidence:** E06. **Quality flag:** B–D are too remote from the domain to discriminate advanced understanding.

**Q16 — A (anaerobic digestion).** **A:** Correct: oxygen-limited microbial decomposition of suitable organic matter produces biogas dominated by methane and carbon dioxide, plus trace impurities. **B:** Raw digester gas is not pure hydrogen; upgrading biogas also does not imply pure hydrogen. **C:** Digestion does not create coal; digestate is a distinct residual material. **D:** Nuclear steam is unrelated to biological digestion. **Evidence:** E07. **Qualification:** gas composition varies with feedstock and process; 'mainly' is essential to the stem.

**Q21 — A (fuel cell versus rechargeable battery).** **A:** Correct for conventional fuel cells: reactants are supplied during operation, while a rechargeable battery's active material participates in reversible internal electrochemical storage. **B:** False: continued fuel-cell electricity production requires fuel/oxidant supply. **C:** False: rechargeable batteries are not intrinsically nuclear devices. **D:** False: electrochemical conversion, auxiliaries and upstream hydrogen production all incur losses. **Evidence:** E08. **Qualification:** regenerative fuel cells blur simplistic one-way descriptions; this item addresses a conventional fuel cell.

**Q22 — A (hydrogen carrier).** **A:** Correct: usable hydrogen is generally produced by reforming, electrolysis or other processes that require feedstock and energy input. **B:** Hydrogen is not ordinarily mined as a naturally occurring liquid fuel. **C:** Hydrogen is an element, not a form of coal. **D:** False: hydrogen has chemical energy that can be converted to work or electricity. **Evidence:** E09. **Qualification:** carrier status does not by itself establish low lifecycle emissions; the production route determines those impacts.

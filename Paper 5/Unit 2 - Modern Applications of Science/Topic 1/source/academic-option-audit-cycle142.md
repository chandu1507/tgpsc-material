## Option-by-option checks

### Existing MCQ 2 — proposed key B · [F01]

**Question:** A landrace is best understood as

- **A. A genetically identical F1 hybrid supplied annually** — Incorrect. An annually supplied genetically uniform F1 hybrid is a defined crossing product, not the usual farmer-maintained local population.
- **B. A locally maintained crop population shaped by farmer selection** — Supported. Farmer selection and local adaptation characterise many landraces, while their genetic diversity varies by population.
- **C. A species that can grow only in forests** — Incorrect. A landrace is not restricted to forest growth; many are cultivated in farms across agro-ecological settings.
- **D. A seed lot that has failed germination tests** — Incorrect. Failed germination is a seed-quality outcome and does not define the breeding history or identity of a landrace.

**Editorial quality issue:** C and D are extremely implausible; rewrite them into credible competing seed categories in a later editorial revision.

### Existing MCQ 4 — proposed key B · [F02]

**Question:** Which pair is correctly matched?

- **A. Rice — C4 crop** — Incorrect. Rice is generally C3, not C4. Crop physiology should not be inferred from water demand alone.
- **B. Maize — C4 crop** — Supported. Maize uses the C4 pathway, which concentrates CO2 near Rubisco and can reduce photorespiration.
- **C. Sorghum — CAM crop** — Incorrect. Sorghum is C4 rather than CAM; drought tolerance does not automatically mean CAM metabolism.
- **D. Wheat — CAM crop** — Incorrect. Wheat is C3 rather than CAM; CAM is a separate temporal carbon-fixation adaptation.

**Editorial quality issue:** Distractors C/D test a legitimate pathway distinction but the two CAM alternatives make elimination easier.

### Existing MCQ 6 — proposed key B · [F03]

**Question:** Cropping intensity is calculated as

- **A. Net sown area divided by gross cropped area × 100** — Incorrect. This is the reciprocal of the official intensity ratio and can be below 100% even where multiple cropping occurs.
- **B. Gross cropped area divided by net sown area × 100** — Supported. Cropping intensity (%) = gross cropped area ÷ net sown area × 100, using the same agricultural year.
- **C. Total grain output divided by fertiliser** — Incorrect. Output per fertiliser input measures another efficiency concept; its dimensions cannot represent cropping intensity.
- **D. Irrigated area divided by forest cover** — Incorrect. Irrigated area relative to forest cover is not a land-use cropping-frequency measure.

**Editorial quality issue:** C/D are dimensionally absurd; replace with gross-irrigated/net-irrigated and other plausible ratios before final approval.

### Existing MCQ 7 — proposed key B · [F04]

**Question:** Which practice is a crop rotation rather than intercropping?

- **A. Redgram and sorghum grown in alternate rows simultaneously** — Incorrect. Redgram and sorghum grown simultaneously in alternate rows constitute intercropping, not rotation.
- **B. Rice followed by chickpea on the same field in successive seasons** — Supported. Rice followed by chickpea in successive seasons on the same field is a temporal crop sequence.
- **C. Cotton and pigeonpea grown together** — Incorrect. Cotton and pigeonpea together are a concurrent intercropping system.
- **D. Two maize cultivars grown in the same season** — Incorrect as written. Two maize cultivars grown together are a within-species mixture; they are not successive-season rotation.

**Editorial quality issue:** D is not clearly described as spatial mixture vs sequential cultivars; specify simultaneous sowing explicitly.

### Existing MCQ 8 — proposed key B · [F05]

**Question:** Which statement about legume nitrogen fixation is accurate?

- **A. Every pulse fixes unlimited nitrogen without microbes** — Incorrect. Nitrogen fixation is biological and conditional; not every pulse has unlimited fixation or independence from microbes.
- **B. Compatible rhizobia can fix atmospheric nitrogen under suitable conditions** — Supported. Compatible rhizobia form symbiotic nodules with suitable legumes and fix atmospheric nitrogen under enabling conditions.
- **C. Pulses require no phosphorus** — Incorrect. Legumes require phosphorus and other nutrients for metabolism and nodulation; fixation does not remove all inputs.
- **D. Nitrogen fixation occurs only in grain after harvest** — Incorrect. Nitrogen fixation takes place in living symbiotic tissues, not exclusively in harvested grain.

**Editorial quality issue:** A/D are obvious absolutes; use plausible biological distractors such as non-nodulating legumes or limiting soil conditions.

### Existing MCQ 11 — proposed key B · [F06]

**Question:** Under the PPV&FR framework, which is the most defensible principle?

- **A. Only companies can contribute to varietal improvement** — Incorrect. The Indian PPV&FR framework explicitly recognises farmer contributions, not only company breeding.
- **B. Farmers' conservation contributions and breeders' innovation both merit recognition** — Supported. The statute recognises both breeder innovation and farmers’ conservation, improvement and varietal rights.
- **C. All protected seed may be sold under the breeder's brand without restrictions** — Incorrect. Section 39 farmer seed freedoms are not permission to sell branded seed of protected varieties without restrictions.
- **D. Plant varieties cannot be registered** — Incorrect. Variety registration is a central function of the Authority, including eligible farmers’ varieties.

**Editorial quality issue:** Legal precision: cite the Act for legal claims; the Authority FAQ says it is explanatory, not a legal instrument.

### Existing MCQ 13 — proposed key B · [F07]

**Question:** A farm harvests 4,800 kg using 6,000 m³ of irrigation water. Its simple irrigation-water productivity is

- **A. 0.08 kg/m³** — Incorrect. 4,800/6,000 is 0.8, not 0.08 kg/m³; this is a tenfold decimal-place error.
- **B. 0.8 kg/m³** — Supported by direct arithmetic. 4,800 kg ÷ 6,000 m³ = 0.8 kg/m³ of irrigation water supplied.
- **C. 8 kg/m³** — Incorrect. 8 kg/m³ is ten times the calculated ratio; units alone cannot rescue the wrong magnitude.
- **D. 80 kg/m³** — Incorrect. 80 kg/m³ is one hundred times the correct ratio and is numerically inconsistent with the inputs.

**Editorial quality issue:** This is a mechanical ratio question; the numerator is total harvest, not incremental yield attributable to irrigation. Define denominator before cross-study comparison.

### Existing MCQ 17 — proposed key B · [F08]

**Question:** Which statement distinguishes a natural forest from a tree plantation?

- **A. Both necessarily have identical species composition** — Incorrect. Natural forests and plantations need not have identical species composition or age structure.
- **B. A plantation's tree cover does not automatically replicate a natural forest's biodiversity** — Supported with nuance. Tree cover from a plantation does not automatically recreate natural-forest species composition and ecological complexity.
- **C. All plantations are legally protected forests** — Incorrect. Plantation status does not itself confer legal protected-forest designation.
- **D. Natural forests contain no grasses or shrubs** — Incorrect. Natural forests can contain grasses, shrubs and diverse non-tree vegetation.

**Editorial quality issue:** Nuance: FAO explicitly distinguishes all planted forests from intensively managed plantation forests; not every planted forest is a monoculture.

### Existing MCQ 20 — proposed key B · [F09]

**Question:** Which claim about medicinal plants is scientifically defensible?

- **A. Natural extracts cannot be toxic** — Incorrect. Natural origin does not establish non-toxicity; plant constituents and dose may create adverse effects.
- **B. Correct identity, dose, processing and contaminants influence safety** — Supported as a safety principle. Botanical identity, preparation, dose and contaminants affect quality and risk.
- **C. All traditional claims have identical clinical evidence** — Incorrect. Traditional use does not imply that all efficacy claims have equal clinical support.
- **D. Wild root harvesting never affects population survival** — Incorrect. Wild root harvest can damage plants and regeneration; blanket no-impact language is indefensible.

**Editorial quality issue:** WHO catalogue confirms quality-control scope, but the full 122-page monograph was not directly inspected; do not claim detailed monograph verification.

### Existing MCQ 28 — proposed key B · [F10]

**Question:** Integrated pest management generally recommends pesticide use

- **A. On a fixed daily schedule regardless of pest presence** — Incorrect. Automatic daily spraying without pest monitoring contradicts ICAR need-based IPM.
- **B. When justified by monitoring, thresholds and compatible measures** — Supported. Monitoring, economic-loss considerations, prevention and compatible controls precede justified pesticide use.
- **C. Only after destroying all natural enemies** — Incorrect. Deliberately destroying natural enemies conflicts with biological control within IPM.
- **D. Without regard to registered dose or worker safety** — Incorrect. Ignoring approved doses and worker safety contradicts ICAR judicious-use guidance.

**Editorial quality issue:** A/C/D are overly extreme; a stronger exam item should distinguish economic thresholds from zero-tolerance eradication and routine prophylaxis.

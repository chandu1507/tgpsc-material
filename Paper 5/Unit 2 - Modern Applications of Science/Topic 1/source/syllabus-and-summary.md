## Executive summary

Crop science links plant biology to productive and sustainable agriculture. A plant's root system, photosynthetic pathway, reproduction, growth duration and stress responses help determine where and how it can be cultivated. India grows cereals, pulses, millets, oilseeds, fibre and commercial crops across different agro-climatic environments. Crop improvement must combine genetic diversity, breeding, reliable seed, field management, plant protection and market suitability. Telangana's dryland and irrigated regions need different strategies; neither universal rice expansion nor blanket crop substitution is scientifically sound. Forest species provide timber, non-timber products, habitat and ecological regulation; agroforestry can connect farms and trees but cannot replace natural forests. Medicinal and aromatic plants offer specialised value chains requiring botanical identity, traceability, safe extraction, quality testing and sustainable collection. Harmful plants include competitive weeds, invasive aliens, poisonous species and allergens; integrated management is more credible than indiscriminate eradication. Genetic conservation, farmers' rights and evidence-based extension preserve future options. Public programmes should be evaluated by measured adoption, income, nutrition, water use and biodiversity, not only announcements.

## Standalone quick revision: 36 high-value distinctions

1. Crop science covers genetics, physiology, agronomy, protection, quality and livelihoods.
2. Species, cultivar, F1 hybrid and farmer landrace are different biological and institutional categories.
3. Domestication may improve harvestability while narrowing genetic diversity.
4. Xylem mainly carries water/minerals; phloem transports sugars to sinks.
5. Stomata balance carbon dioxide uptake against water loss.
6. Rice and wheat are C3; maize and sorghum are C4; CAM is different.
7. Drought escape through early maturity differs from physiological drought tolerance.
8. Resistance limits pathogen establishment; tolerance limits damage despite infection.
9. Annuals complete a season; perennials persist for years.
10. Kharif, rabi and zaid are seasonal cultivation conventions, not rigid species labels.
11. Cropping intensity = gross cropped area / net sown area × 100.
12. Intercropping is simultaneous; rotation is sequential.
13. Legume nitrogen fixation requires compatible symbiosis and suitable conditions.
14. Seed genetic purity, physical purity, germination and vigour are different metrics.
15. Marker-assisted selection needs field validation.
16. Breeder, foundation and certified seed form a multiplication and quality chain.
17. Harvest index relates economic to biological yield, not net profit.
18. Water productivity requires a specified water denominator.
19. Biofortification occurs through the crop; fortification may occur after harvest.
20. Seed distribution does not prove nutritional outcomes.
21. Cotton breeding must consider lint quality as well as yield.
22. Oilseed value depends on recoverable oil and seed quality, not seed weight alone.
23. Forest cover, recorded forest area, tree cover and plantation area differ.
24. Non-timber forest products still require sustainable harvest rules.
25. Agroforestry may improve diversity but trees can compete with crops for water.
26. Medicinal use does not establish clinical efficacy or safety.
27. Botanical identity, harvest stage and contamination control matter in medicinal plants.
28. Essential oils are concentrated volatile mixtures; steam distillation is one extraction route.
29. Natural origin does not mean non-toxic.
30. Ecosystem services include provisioning, regulating and cultural benefits.
31. A weed is context-dependent; invasive alien status has a biogeographic component.
32. Parthenium can cause competition and allergies; seed-bank control needs repeated action.
33. Castor seeds contain ricin; oleander contains cardiac glycosides.
34. Disease triangle: susceptible host, pathogen and conducive environment.
35. IPM uses monitoring and economic thresholds, not automatic pesticide spraying.
36. PPV&FR rights, biodiversity access rules, seed certification and forest rights are separate legal regimes.

## Summary for mains preparation

A strong answer should move from plant mechanism to field practice, then to livelihood and policy consequences. Explain how traits shape suitability, why one intervention works only under certain conditions, and which institutions can implement it. Use Telangana examples with named research stations and dates where verified. Compare productivity with water, nutrition, biodiversity and equity; conclude with measurable recommendations. Do not mistake official policy intentions for observed outcomes, or a registered variety for universal farmer adoption.

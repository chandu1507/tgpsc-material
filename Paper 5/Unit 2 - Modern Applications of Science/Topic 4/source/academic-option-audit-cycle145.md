# Cycle 145 — Topic 5-2-4: ten original MCQs, four-option evidence review
**Date:** 1 November 2026. **Scope:** original practice questions Q1, Q3, Q5, Q9, Q10, Q13, Q17, Q23, Q24, Q29. **Status:** author-side, source-correlated; NOT independently certified. **Official TGPSC PYQ claims:** none.

The question stems and existing answer letters were compared with the unchanged `source/original-mcqs.md`. Every option below is assessed separately. A source supporting a correct answer does not, by itself, establish examination quality of distractors. This audit does not substitute for a named microbiologist/epidemiologist's review.

## Q1 — organism categories. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** *Candida* is a yeast/fungus, not a virus. CDC categorises candidiasis as fungal [C01].
- **B (correct):** *Plasmodium* is a protozoan malaria parasite; CDC's organism-classification register lists *P. falciparum* and *P. vivax* among protozoa [C02].
- **C (wrong):** HIV is a virus, not a bacterium [C03].
- **D (wrong):** *Vibrio cholerae* is a bacterium, not a fungus [C04].
**Quality:** Correct categorisation, but three category-switch distractors are very easy; retain provisionally, revise for higher-order classification later.

## Q3 — watery-diarrhoea cluster. Existing key D: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** A cluster of watery diarrhoea is not automatically laboratory-confirmed cholera; the WHO outbreak toolbox distinguishes suspected and confirmed classifications [C05].
- **B (wrong):** WHO explicitly calls for immediate public-health action for a suspected cholera outbreak without waiting for laboratory confirmation [C05].
- **C (wrong):** Universal antibiotics for all residents are not indicated; WHO emphasises rehydration, WASH and clinically selected treatment [C04].
- **D (correct):** Safe water, triage and investigation are immediate, proportionate actions before aetiology is confirmed [C05].
**Quality:** Good decision-order test; the scenario alone does not establish cholera. Do not invent local case counts as historical evidence.

## Q5 — oral rehydration. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** ORS treats dehydration; it does not create lasting immunity or prevent all future infection [C04].
- **B (correct):** Sodium–glucose cotransport through intestinal SGLT1 promotes sodium and water absorption, a physiologic basis for ORS [C06].
- **C (wrong):** ORS is not a water disinfectant; water safety is a separate WASH intervention [C04].
- **D (wrong):** ORS does not directly kill *V. cholerae*; it replaces fluid and electrolytes [C04].
**Quality:** Correct mechanism; three distractors are implausible and should be strengthened before R5 PASS.

## Q9 — pulmonary TB transmission. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** Chlorinated tap water is not the principal transmission route of pulmonary TB [C07].
- **B (correct):** Infectious pulmonary/laryngeal TB releases airborne particles that can be inhaled, especially in poorly ventilated indoor settings [C07].
- **C (wrong):** Mosquito bites are not a route of TB transmission [C07].
- **D (wrong):** Sharing cooked food is not the principal route; CDC specifically rejects food-sharing as TB transmission [C07].
**Quality:** Biologically correct but obvious distractors; a stronger item would contrast ventilation, masking and surface cleaning.

## Q10 — infection versus active TB. Existing key C: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** Inactive/latent TB infection is not infectious, so not every infected person is immediately infectious [C07].
- **B (wrong):** Active TB commonly affects lungs and may involve other organs; it is not confined to skin [C08].
- **C (correct):** TB infection can occur without active disease or symptoms; latent infection does not transmit TB [C07][C08].
- **D (wrong):** Positive sputum culture is not universal among people infected with TB, especially in latent infection [C07].
**Quality:** Valid distinction; avoid treating all active TB cases as equally infectious.

## Q13 — malaria relapse. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** *Shigella dysenteriae* is a bacterium and has no malaria hypnozoite liver stage [C02][C09].
- **B (correct):** *P. vivax* can persist as dormant hepatic hypnozoites and later relapse; *P. ovale* can also relapse, but is not an offered option [C09].
- **C (wrong):** HIV is a virus, not a *Plasmodium* parasite [C03].
- **D (wrong):** *V. cholerae* causes bacterial cholera, not malaria relapse [C04].
**Quality:** Correct but easy organism-category distractors; do not confuse relapse with recrudescence or reinfection.

## Q17 — HIV casual contact. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (not the answer):** Perinatal transmission is a recognised route, including during pregnancy or delivery [C03].
- **B (correct 'NOT transmitted'):** Ordinary shared meals and handshakes do not transmit HIV [C03].
- **C (not the answer):** Unsafe blood transfusion can transmit HIV [C03].
- **D (not the answer):** Unprotected sexual exposure is a recognised transmission route [C03].
**Quality:** Unambiguous, relevant anti-stigma item. The word 'NOT' should be emphasised in accessible HTML and print.

## Q23 — Indian IDSP streams. Existing key D: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** S/P/L do not denote energy-source categories; the NCDC defines surveillance forms [C10].
- **B (wrong):** They are not school/police/legislature reporting categories [C10].
- **C (wrong):** The three forms are not three laboratory specialties [C10].
- **D (correct):** S = suspected/syndromic, P = presumptive/clinician-reported, L = laboratory-confirmed; NCDC specifies the reporting roles [C10].
**Quality:** Correct but weak distractors. S/P/L counts should not be added as though they were necessarily distinct patients.

## Q24 — event attack proportion. Existing key A: SUPPORTED
- **A (correct):** 24/120 × 100 = 20% observed attack proportion among the specified exposed attendees [C11].
- **B (wrong):** 5% would correspond to 6 cases among 120, not 24 [C11].
- **C (wrong):** 24% incorrectly uses the numerator as a percentage [C11].
- **D (wrong):** 120% cannot be the observed proportion when 24 of 120 attendees became cases [C11].
**Quality:** Arithmetic verified. Assumes the 120 denominator represents all eligible exposed attendees and cases are uniquely counted.

## Q29 — surveillance ascertainment. Existing key B: SUPPORTED
- **A (wrong):** Increased case reporting does not imply a biological change from bacteria to fungi [C10].
- **B (correct):** New laboratories can increase case ascertainment; a genuine incidence change is also possible. Interpret with testing volume, case definitions and reporting completeness [C10].
- **C (wrong):** Higher reported counts alone do not guarantee worsening underlying incidence [C10].
- **D (wrong):** More diagnoses do not establish eradication [C10].
**Quality:** Useful epidemiological reasoning; correct answer is qualified rather than falsely definitive.

## Additional defect — Q27: NOT COUNTED AS A COMPLETED FOUR-OPTION REVIEW
Current stem asks, “Which result alone is insufficient to establish an outbreak source?” **All four options are insufficient alone**: an exposure history, time–place analysis, patient testing, and one negative post-disinfection sample each require triangulation. Thus the existing keyed D is *not uniquely defensible as written*. [C12] supports the intended negative-sample caveat but does not rescue the stem. **Proposed replacement stem for later approved edit:** “Which observation does NOT rule out a previously contaminated water supply as an outbreak source?” Under that stem D is uniquely appropriate. Preserve the inherited text pending controlled correction; mark Q27 INVALID/AMBIGUOUS for R5.

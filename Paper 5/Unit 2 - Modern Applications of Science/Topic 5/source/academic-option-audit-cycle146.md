# Topic 5-2-5 — Cycle146 primary-source four-option review

## Cycle 146 — bounded primary-source audit of 12 existing vaccine MCQs (1 November 2026)

**Scope and limits.** This is an author-side, question-by-question review of **existing original practice** questions Q11, Q12, Q13, Q18, Q19, Q20, Q21, Q22, Q23, Q25, Q27 and Q29. It examines 12 proposed keys and 36 distractors against the cited primary documents. It does **not** certify official TGPSC PYQ provenance, the other 18 questions/54 distractors, the six mains answers, the diagrams, or independent academic approval. All 30 original questions and their answer letters remain unchanged. Where a distractor is absurdly easy, it is recorded as a *quality defect*, not celebrated as an academic pass.

### Q11 — Modern human rabies vaccine (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** Human PEP does not deliberately administer infectious wild-type rabies virus; WHO describes modern vaccines derived from inactivated cell-culture preparations. **Quality:** an implausible distractor.
- **B — supported:** Inactivated rabies vaccine antigen stimulates the recipient's active immune response, which takes time; it is not equivalent to immediately supplying ready-made neutralising antibody.
- **C — incorrect:** Antibiotics do not neutralise rabies virus and are not the protective mechanism of rabies vaccination. **Quality:** trivially unrelated.
- **D — incorrect:** Mosquito control is irrelevant to rabies vaccine action; the virus is associated principally with exposure to infected mammals. **Quality:** trivially unrelated.

**Evidence:** WHO, *New rabies recommendations*, 15 January 2018 [C146-01]. **Disposition:** B defensible; rewrite C and D into biologically plausible misconceptions in a future controlled revision, without silently changing the inherited MCQ.

### Q12 — Suspected rabies exposure (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect and unsafe:** Waiting for symptoms forfeits the opportunity for timely preventive post-exposure management.
- **B — supported:** WHO calls for immediate thorough wound washing/flushing (approximately 15 minutes with soap/detergent and water) and prompt assessment for vaccine and, where indicated, rabies immunoglobulin.
- **C — incorrect:** Antibiotics alone cannot replace rabies-specific PEP; bacterial-wound management is a different clinical question.
- **D — incorrect:** Passive observation without assessment is not a sound general rule for a possible exposure.

**Evidence:** WHO rabies recommendations [C146-01]. **Caveat:** This is general educational guidance, not an individual treatment schedule; exposure category, previous vaccination, immune status and local clinical protocol matter. **Disposition:** B supported; A/C/D are too easily eliminated.

### Q13 — Rabies immunoglobulin (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** Rabies immunoglobulin is not a vaccine preservative.
- **B — supported with scope:** It provides immediate *passive* antibody protection while vaccine-induced active immunity develops; WHO recommends it for eligible category III exposures in previously unvaccinated persons, not every bite.
- **C — incorrect:** Immunoglobulin does not replace prompt wound washing; these are complementary interventions.
- **D — incorrect:** Passive antibody does not turn an inactivated vaccine into a live preparation.

**Evidence:** WHO rabies exposure categories and PEP [C146-01]. **Disposition:** B supported only with eligibility caveat; A/C/D are weak distractors.

### Q18 — Hepatitis B birth dose (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** Hepatitis B vaccination does not prevent every hepatitis virus (A, C, D, E); disease-specific antigen matters.
- **B — supported:** Timely hepatitis B birth dose (preferably within 24 hours), followed by the recommended infant series, reduces perinatal and early-childhood HBV transmission and its high chronic-infection risk.
- **C — incorrect:** A birth dose does not substitute for the subsequent primary series.
- **D — incorrect:** Vaccination prevents infection; it does not treat existing cirrhosis.

**Evidence:** WHO hepatitis B mother-to-child transmission guidance [C146-02]. **Disposition:** B supported; retain the explicit birth-dose **and** series distinction.

### Q19 — India's pentavalent combination (proposed key A)

- **A — supported:** The routine UIP pentavalent combination includes diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B and *Haemophilus influenzae* type b antigens. The five *disease targets* should not be confused with five separate injections.
- **B — incorrect:** It lists unrelated vaccine targets rather than the UIP DPT-HepB-Hib combination.
- **C — incorrect:** Pentavalent includes DPT **plus** HepB and Hib, not DPT alone.
- **D — incorrect:** Rabies, hepatitis A and influenza are not the specified components of this UIP combination.

**Evidence:** Government of India MoHFW, *Universal Immunization Programme* [C146-03]. **Caveat:** The question's phrase “India's primary pentavalent vaccine” is less precise than “the pentavalent vaccine used in India's UIP”; recommended future wording correction. **Disposition:** A supported.

### Q20 — DTP1→DTP3 dropout indicator (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** Dose-series dropout is not a laboratory assay of potency or product effectiveness.
- **B — supported with a statistical qualification:** The conventional administrative indicator is `(DTP1 − DTP3) / DTP1 × 100`, intended to describe incomplete continuation among starters; however, routinely aggregated first- and third-dose counts are **not** necessarily a true linked-child cohort, and migration, late doses, reporting errors or period mismatch can distort it.
- **C — incorrect:** Never-vaccinated children are not captured by the DTP1-starter denominator; zero-dose prevalence is a separate measure.
- **D — incorrect:** HBV infection prevalence is not a vaccination-series dropout indicator.

**Evidence:** WHO/UNICEF coverage definitions and 2025 WUENIC release [C146-04], with indicator arithmetic shown explicitly in Q21. **Disposition:** B acceptable as a programme proxy, **not** as proof that named individuals dropped out. This caveat should appear in teaching notes.

### Q21 — 8,000 DTP1 and 7,200 DTP3 (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** 5% would correspond to 400/8,000, not the observed 800 difference.
- **B — supported arithmetically:** `(8,000 − 7,200)/8,000 × 100 = 10%`. The question is a numerical exercise, not a validated longitudinal cohort analysis.
- **C — incorrect:** 20% would require 1,600 fewer third-dose records among 8,000 starters.
- **D — incorrect:** 90% is the *DTP3-to-DTP1 ratio*, not the dropout percentage; this is the only genuinely plausible distractor.

**Evidence:** Direct reproducible arithmetic; WHO/UNICEF indicator context [C146-04]. **Disposition:** B supported; A/C too weak, D useful.

### Q22 — WHO/UNICEF 15 July 2026 coverage release (proposed key C)

- **A — incorrect:** 25% is not the global 2025 DTP3 estimate in the specified release.
- **B — incorrect:** 50% is not the reported estimate.
- **C — directly supported:** The **15 July 2026** WHO/UNICEF WUENIC release reports **85% global DTP3 coverage for the 2025 reference year** (about 110 million infants), versus 90% for DTP1. These are global estimates, **not India or Telangana rates**.
- **D — incorrect:** 100% is contradicted by the reported coverage gap.

**Evidence:** WHO 2025 immunization coverage dashboard [C146-04] and UNICEF's original dated joint release [C146-05]. **Disposition:** C supported as a dated data-recall question; the publication year and reference year must remain distinct.

### Q23 — Adverse event following immunization (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** A post-vaccination event does not, solely by its timing, establish vaccine causality.
- **B — directly supported:** WHO defines AEFI as an untoward medical occurrence following immunization that **does not necessarily have a causal relationship** to the vaccine; investigation is needed.
- **C — incorrect:** An AEFI is not limited to allergy; it may include symptoms, diagnoses or laboratory abnormalities.
- **D — incorrect:** Manufacturing-quality failure is only one possible safety-investigation context, not the definition of AEFI.

**Evidence:** WHO Global Advisory Committee on Vaccine Safety, *Serious AEFI* [C146-06]. **Disposition:** B supported. **Quality:** C/D too easily eliminated.

### Q25 — Vaccine vial monitor (proposed key B)

- **A — incorrect:** A VVM does not replace temperature logs or cold-chain quality assurance.
- **B — directly supported:** WHO describes VVMs as colour-changing indicators of **cumulative heat exposure** for applicable vaccine vials, subject to specified discard thresholds.
- **C — incorrect:** VVMs are not sterility tests and cannot rule out microbial contamination.
- **D — incorrect:** VVMs do not measure the recipient's antibody titre or actual clinical immunity.

**Evidence:** WHO VVM technical document (21 June 2021) [C146-07]; WHO cold-chain guidance distinguishes freeze exposure from VVM heat monitoring [C146-08]. **Disposition:** B supported; add a teaching warning that freeze-sensitive vaccines may be damaged without a VVM threshold change.

### Q27 — Mission Indradhanush launch (proposed key A)

- **A — directly supported:** The Indian Ministry of Health and Family Welfare records the launch of Mission Indradhanush on **25 December 2014**, aimed at children and pregnant women missed by routine immunization.
- **B — incorrect:** Digital India is a different programme and not the immunization mission launched on that date.
- **C — incorrect:** The National Rabies Control Programme is distinct from Mission Indradhanush.
- **D — incorrect:** Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission is not the programme named in the MoHFW account.

**Evidence:** MoHFW, *Universal Immunization Programme* [C146-03]. **Disposition:** A supported, but B/C/D are low-discrimination distractors; replace with plausible immunization-programme dates in a controlled revision.

### Q29 — Herd effects and tetanus (proposed key C)

- **A — incorrect:** Not every vaccine prevents infection/transmission sufficiently to interrupt spread; direct disease protection and indirect herd effects are distinct.
- **B — incorrect:** Tetanus generally arises from environmental spores and is not passed person-to-person; classic transmission-interruption herd immunity does not apply.
- **C — supported:** Indirect protection depends on the pathogen's human-to-human transmission and the vaccine's effect on it. Tetanus is a clear counterexample to simplistic herd-protection claims.
- **D — incorrect:** Rabies is primarily zoonotic; ordinary sustained person-to-person transmission is not its usual epidemiology.

**Evidence:** WHO tetanus fact sheet [C146-09], WHO rabies recommendations [C146-01]. **Disposition:** C supported; B is the strongest distractor, D weak.

## 2. Standalone quick revision — the entire chapter in one pass

1. **Work-rate identity:** If a person finishes one job in *a* days, the person's constant rate is 1/*a* jobs per day. Joint rates add only when both are contributing to the same measurable job and their efficiencies are stable.
2. **Time is reciprocal to rate:** If the combined rate is 5/24 job/day, one full job requires 24/5 = 4.8 days, not 5/24 days. The dimensional unit is the quickest error detector.
3. **Efficiency:** For a fixed job, efficiency ratio A:B = 3:2 implies time ratio 2:3. It does not imply A completes three jobs in the time B completes two unless the same job unit is used.
4. **Variable crews:** Use work = sum(rate × active duration). When a crew changes after a shift, split the timeline; do not apply the final rate to the whole job.
5. **Pipes:** Inlets have positive rates, outlets negative rates. A negative net rate means the tank empties rather than fills.
6. **Distance:** distance = speed × time; speed = distance/time. 1 m/s = 3.6 km/h. State whether the distance is one-way, round-trip, or relative.
7. **Average speed:** total distance ÷ total time, **not** generally the arithmetic mean of speeds. Equal-distance legs at speeds u and v have average 2uv/(u+v).
8. **Relative speed:** same direction → absolute difference of speeds; opposite direction → sum. Train crossing a platform uses train length + platform length.
9. **Boat motion:** downstream speed = still-water speed + current; upstream speed = still-water speed − current, provided current is steady and still-water speed exceeds current.
10. **Simple interest:** SI = PRT/100 for annual R and T in years; amount = P + SI. For months use T = months/12, for days use an explicitly stated year convention.
11. **Analytical reasoning:** Translate 'all', 'some', 'only', 'unless' and 'if' into explicit logical forms. 'If P then Q' does not establish 'if Q then P'.
12. **Critical reasoning:** Separate a reported association from a causal mechanism; check selection, denominator, timing, alternatives and unintended consequences.

**Fast error audit:** (i) Is the unit correct? (ii) Are the workers or vehicles active during the stated interval? (iii) Is the rate constant? (iv) Does the conclusion follow in *all* cases compatible with the premises? (v) Are administrative numbers illustrative or verified?

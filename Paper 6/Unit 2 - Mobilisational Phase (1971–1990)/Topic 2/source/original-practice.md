## Original MCQ practice — 30 questions with explanations

**Q1. Which event supplied the name “Naxalite” to a family of radical movements?**
A. The 1997 Samatha decision
B. The 1967 Naxalbari uprising
C. The 1948 Hyderabad integration
D. The 1973 Six-Point Formula
**Answer: B.** The name comes from Naxalbari in West Bengal in 1967; the other events belong to different historical or legal processes.

**Q2. Which distinction is essential when writing about Telangana agrarian resistance?**
A. All peasant associations were underground armed parties
B. They had identical leadership throughout
C. The 1940s peasant struggle and post-1967 Naxalite currents were different conjunctures
D. Both were founded in 1978
**Answer: C.** Continuity of grievances and memory does not establish organisational identity.

**Q3. Why did landlord power extend beyond ownership of land?**
A. It always depended only on formal acreage
B. It was abolished everywhere in 1956
C. It excluded all influence over wages
D. It could combine credit, tenancy, employment and local influence
**Answer: D.** Control over multiple livelihood relationships could make individual resistance costly.

**Q4. What is the best analytical meaning of vetti in the relevant rural context?**
A. Coercive or unpaid service obligations
B. A statutory land-title certificate
C. A forest-rights committee
D. A form of district reorganisation
**Answer: A.** Vetti denotes exploitative service or labour obligations; it is not a synonym for every labour disagreement.

**Q5. Which year is associated with the Jagityala Jaitra Yatra?**
A. 1996
B. 1978
C. 1947
D. 1962
**Answer: B.** Retrospective scholarship places the major peasant mobilisation in September 1978; precise attendance remains source-dependent.

**Q6. What should an examiner expect in an account of the Jagityala–Siricilla struggle?**
A. Only a national chronology
B. A fixed crowd count without sources
C. Local demands, organisation, state response and consequences
D. Only a list of party acronyms
**Answer: C.** A causal local account is stronger than unverified numerical assertions.

**Q7. What does Rytu-Cooli Sangham bring together in its name?**
A. Judges and civil servants
B. Forest guards and mining companies
C. District collectors and legislators
D. Cultivators and wage workers
**Answer: D.** The label signals an alliance of rural cultivators and agricultural labourers, whose interests may sometimes differ.

**Q8. Which statement about Rytu-Cooli Sanghams is most defensible?**
A. Their local affiliations and methods require case-specific evidence
B. Every sangham had identical registration
C. Every member endorsed armed violence
D. They were constituted by the Forest Rights Act
**Answer: A.** Local history should not be flattened into one timeless organisational identity.

**Q9. Why is a registered deed insufficient to settle a Scheduled Area transfer dispute?**
A. Only the buyer may supply evidence
B. A prohibited transfer may remain invalid despite registration
C. Registration overrides all protective laws
D. The Fifth Schedule is irrelevant to transfers
**Answer: B.** The competent authority must examine the governing regulation and transaction facts.

**Q10. Which constitutional framework governs Scheduled Areas in most relevant states?**
A. Article 356 alone
B. The Finance Commission
C. The Fifth Schedule
D. The Tenth Schedule
**Answer: C.** The Fifth Schedule contains special Scheduled Area governance provisions.

**Q11. What does the Fifth Schedule NOT automatically establish?**
A. That Governors may make specified protective regulations
B. That tribal interests merit special protection
C. That advisory structures exist
D. That every tribal-populated village is a notified Scheduled Area
**Answer: D.** Scheduled Area status depends on notification, not demographic shorthand.

**Q12. Which statement correctly describes the 1959 Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation in Telangana after the Supreme Court’s 1995 *Venkata Ramanaiah* decision?**
A. The regime was extended to the Telangana Scheduled Areas effective 1 December 1963, and the 1959/1963/1970 regulations do not automatically invalidate completed pre-commencement transfers
B. Every land transfer anywhere in Telangana became retrospectively void from 4 March 1959
C. The 1970 amendment abolished all protections for Scheduled Tribe landholders
D. The 1996 PESA Act retrospectively validated all prohibited land transfers
**Answer: A.** The Supreme Court distinguished commencement dates and held the regulations prospective. The result does not validate fraud or transfers independently prohibited by another applicable law; see the 18 September 1995 Supreme Court judgment, pp. 6–15.

**Q13. Which Supreme Court case is a landmark for mining leases in Scheduled Areas?**
A. P. Sambamurthy v. Andhra Pradesh
B. Samatha v. State of Andhra Pradesh
C. Kesavananda Bharati v. Kerala
D. Minerva Mills v. Union of India
**Answer: B.** Samatha (1997) addressed the protective land-transfer regime and mining leases.

**Q14. In which year was the Samatha judgment delivered?**
A. 1978
B. 2014
C. 1997
D. 1967
**Answer: C.** The Supreme Court decided Samatha on 11 July 1997.

**Q15. PESA is primarily a framework for what?**
A. Recruitment under Article 371-D
B. Issuing individual forest titles automatically
C. Replacing every state law
D. Self-governance in Scheduled Areas
**Answer: D.** The 1996 Act adapts panchayat governance for Scheduled Areas.

**Q16. The Forest Rights Act was enacted in which year?**
A. 2006
B. 1970
C. 1985
D. 1996
**Answer: A.** The FRA was enacted in 2006 and commenced subsequently; it is distinct from PESA.

**Q17. Which institution initiates forest-rights claims under the FRA process?**
A. Regional Transport Authority
B. Gram Sabha with Forest Rights Committee assistance
C. State Election Commission
D. Lok Sabha Secretariat
**Answer: B.** The official FRA process begins at Gram Sabha level before higher scrutiny.

**Q18. What is a community forest resource right principally about?**
A. Unconditional commercial mining rights
B. Automatic exemption from conservation duties
C. Community management of traditionally used forest resources
D. Only a private residential plot
**Answer: C.** CFR rights recognise community governance and sustainable management responsibilities.

**Q19. Which example best illustrates land alienation?**
A. A lawful wage increase
B. Election of a panchayat
C. Publication of a district map
D. Loss of effective land control through a prohibited or coerced transfer
**Answer: D.** Alienation concerns loss of rights, possession or access; the legal remedy depends on facts.

**Q20. What is the most appropriate first step in a Scheduled Area land inquiry?**
A. Confirm notified status and locate the parcel
B. Immediately cancel every deed
C. Assume all buyers are guilty
D. Issue a press release instead of gathering records
**Answer: A.** Territorial applicability and parcel identification are threshold questions.

**Q21. What does Jal, Jungle, Zamin mean?**
A. Land, labour and tax
B. Water, forest and land
C. Justice, jobs and language
D. Judiciary, legislature and administration
**Answer: B.** The slogan highlights ecological livelihood relationships; it is not itself a statute.

**Q22. Which claim requires special care when reporting 1978 events?**
A. That land grievances existed
B. That September 1978 was significant
C. An exact crowd estimate lacking original attendance records
D. That rural workers organised
**Answer: C.** Retrospective crowd figures differ; avoid presenting one as an audited official total.

**Q23. Why are contemporary district boundaries risky in historical descriptions?**
A. They are identical to Hyderabad State borders
B. All districts were created in 1947
C. Historical maps are unnecessary
D. Taluk and district boundaries have changed
**Answer: D.** Use historical administrative units for period-specific events.

**Q24. Which evaluation dimension concerns access to grievance forums?**
A. Procedural outcomes
B. Only agricultural productivity
C. Only party ideology
D. Only rainfall
**Answer: A.** Procedural justice asks whether affected people can use institutions and obtain reasoned decisions.

**Q25. How should a historian use oral testimony about a movement?**
A. Replace records with political slogans
B. Triangulate it with other independent sources
C. Treat every recollection as a certified government statistic
D. Discard it entirely
**Answer: B.** Oral testimony recovers experience but memory and perspective require source criticism.

**Q26. Which policy response is constitutionally preferable to indiscriminate detention?**
A. Collective punishment
B. Suppressing all wage complaints
C. Evidence-based investigation with due process
D. Treating all rally participants as armed cadres
**Answer: C.** Public safety and civil liberties must both be protected.

**Q27. Why are women’s experiences important in analysing sanghams?**
A. Women were legally prohibited from organising
B. Gender never affected village politics
C. All benefits automatically reached women
D. Class mobilisation may coexist with gendered exclusions
**Answer: D.** Examine voice, leadership, safety and distribution of benefits.

**Q28. Which claim about the Naxalite movement and 2014 statehood is weakest?**
A. One 1978 march directly and solely caused statehood
B. Agrarian politics shaped regional discourse
C. Different groups pursued different objectives
D. Constitutional state formation followed separate processes
**Answer: A.** Avoid deterministic causal claims across decades without demonstrating mechanisms.

**Q29. Which is a meaningful measure of land restoration success?**
A. Only deeds photocopied
B. Physical possession after a valid order and follow-up
C. Number of press statements
D. Only cases opened
**Answer: B.** Orders and actual restoration are different administrative outputs.

**Q30. What is the strongest concluding lesson from this syllabus topic?**
A. Land law is irrelevant to mobilisation
B. Every historical allegation is an established fact
C. Pair agrarian and tribal justice with lawful democratic institutions and accountability
D. Violence is the only possible remedy
**Answer: C.** A balanced conclusion combines rights enforcement, due process and evidence-based history.

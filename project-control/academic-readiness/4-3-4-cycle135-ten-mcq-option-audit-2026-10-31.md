### Q1 — collection rate versus recovery rate; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** 90/100 tonnes collected measures pickup, not recovered or recycled material. No recycling-output mass is supplied.
- **B — correct:** 90 tonnes collected / 100 tonnes generated = **90% collection by mass**. The units and denominator match the stem.
- **C — incorrect:** Landfill diversion requires downstream destination and reject data, not only collection.
- **D — incorrect:** Treatment efficiency needs treatment input/output and performance criteria; no treatment is described.
- **Evidence:** Independently reproduced arithmetic; [CPCB SWM 2026 portal](https://swm.cpcb.gov.in/) separates generation, collection, processing and disposal reporting. **Limit:** the 100/90 tonnes are hypothetical, not a municipal statistic.

### Q2 — four-stream segregation; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** Paper, metal and glass are dry-material subtypes, not the four mandatory headline streams.
- **B — correct:** The four streams are **wet, dry, sanitary and special-care** waste.
- **C — incorrect:** Biodegradable/combustible/inert/industrial does not reproduce the four-stream generator duty; industrial waste can fall outside this regime.
- **D — incorrect:** Household/industrial/agricultural/hospital describes origins, not the four segregation streams; biomedical waste has separate requirements.
- **Evidence:** [MoEFCC-hosted 2026 SWM Rules, rule 5(1)(b)](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_69a16e3b04c107.91022257.pdf); [CPCB portal](https://swm.cpcb.gov.in/). **Limit:** legal text is verified for this question, not all duties or later amendments.

### Q3 — disjunctive bulk-generator criteria; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** 100 kg/day is **one alternative** threshold, not a universal prerequisite.
- **B — correct:** 21,000 m² exceeds the **20,000 m²** floor-area threshold even at 60 kg/day; a residential complex is a listed category.
- **C — incorrect:** Residential societies/RWAs are expressly listed by the CPCB portal.
- **D — incorrect:** 40,000 L/day is an alternative threshold, not an additional mandatory condition; the stem does not supply water use.
- **Evidence:** [CPCB centralised SWM portal, BWG eligibility and categories](https://swm.cpcb.gov.in/). **Limit:** qualification is not proof that this hypothetical complex has registered or complied.

### Q6 — biochemical oxygen demand versus dissolved oxygen; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** BOD concerns oxygen **consumed/demanded** during biological decomposition; DO is oxygen present.
- **B — correct:** The measures describe different aspects of water oxygen status: demand versus available concentration.
- **C — incorrect:** They are not identical tests or interchangeable numerical results.
- **D — incorrect:** High BOD alone does not diagnose a heavy-metal spill; organic and ammonia-related oxygen demand are other causes.
- **Evidence:** [US EPA oxygen/BOD technical primer](https://www3.epa.gov/npdes/pubs/primer.pdf); [EPA estuary-monitoring oxygen chapter](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2015-09/documents/2009_03_13_estuaries_monitor_chap9.pdf). **Limit:** technical correspondence, not a local Telangana sample.

### Q7 — material recovery facility; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** An open mixed-waste dump is not a facility that sorts and prepares materials for recovery.
- **B — correct:** An MRF separates recyclable material classes and prepares them for onward recovery; its outputs still need destination checks.
- **C — incorrect:** Biomedical sharps require a different controlled waste pathway, not the purpose of a routine dry-material MRF.
- **D — incorrect:** Sorting generates residual or unsuitable material; universal saleability is not guaranteed.
- **Evidence:** [US EPA materials recovery centre explainer](https://www3.epa.gov/recyclecity/recovery.htm); [MoEFCC SWM 2026 Rules](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_69a16e3b04c107.91022257.pdf). **Limit:** EPA is an explanatory model; it does not certify any Indian MRF performance.

### Q9 — anaerobic digestion; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** Continuous atmospheric oxygen is the defining condition for **aerobic** processing, not anaerobic digestion.
- **B — correct:** Microorganisms break down organic feedstocks **without oxygen**, producing methane-containing biogas and digestate.
- **C — incorrect:** Digestate and other residues remain; 'no residues' contradicts the process.
- **D — incorrect:** Batteries are not an appropriate organic feedstock and require separate management.
- **Evidence:** [US EPA basic anaerobic digestion explanation](https://www.epa.gov/anaerobic-digestion/basic-information-about-anaerobic-digestion). **Limit:** does not establish suitability or regulatory approval for a specific facility.

### Q11 — plant design capacity versus throughput; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** 2,000 tonnes/day is stated **input design capacity**, not useful output; input actually received is 900 tonnes/day.
- **B — correct:** 2,000 > 900, so design capacity exceeds observed throughput by **1,100 tonnes/day**; utilisation by received mass is **45%** (900/2,000).
- **C — incorrect:** Neither input quantity establishes stack emissions or permit compliance.
- **D — incorrect:** Energy recovery still leaves rejects and residues; landfill elimination is not demonstrated.
- **Evidence:** Reproduced hypothetical arithmetic and process logic; [MoEFCC SWM 2026 Rules on residual disposal](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_69a16e3b04c107.91022257.pdf). **Limit:** no real plant data, energy conversion efficiency or emissions measurements are given.

### Q13 — landfill leachate; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** Water contacting waste may mobilise pollutants and is not automatically clean rainwater.
- **B — correct:** Leachate is liquid passing through/contacting waste that can carry dissolved and suspended contamination.
- **C — incorrect:** Leachate is liquid; landfill gas is a distinct gaseous emission.
- **D — incorrect:** Migration to groundwater is possible depending on liners, geology, hydraulic pathways and management; 'cannot' is unsupportable.
- **Evidence:** [US EPA landfill-gas and leachate overview](https://www.epa.gov/lmop/basic-information-about-landfill-gas); [EPA-indexed Indian groundwater-leachate study](https://hero.epa.gov/reference/524114/). **Limit:** study is not a measurement of any Telangana site; site-specific exposure requires sampling.

### Q24 — methane in landfill gas; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** Landfill gas is not solely oxygen; decomposition commonly yields methane and carbon dioxide.
- **B — correct:** Anaerobic degradation of buried organics can generate methane-containing landfill gas.
- **C — incorrect:** Methane is flammable, so an absolute 'no fire risk' claim is false.
- **D — incorrect:** Landfill gas and leachate are different media requiring different collection and monitoring.
- **Evidence:** [US EPA basic landfill-gas information](https://www.epa.gov/lmop/basic-information-about-landfill-gas). **Limit:** typical composition is not a site-specific methane concentration.

### Q26 — C&D waste separate legal framework; keyed B
- **A — incorrect:** Construction debris is not an ordinary household wet-waste stream; SWM 2026 rule 5 directs separate storage/management.
- **B — correct:** The **Environment (Construction and Demolition) Waste Management Rules, 2025**, G.S.R. 219(E) dated **2 April 2025**, commenced **1 April 2026**.
- **C — incorrect:** A distinct notified C&D framework exists.
- **D — incorrect:** Construction debris is generally managed through segregation, reuse and recycling; mandatory universal incineration is not the rule.
- **Evidence:** [MoEFCC 2025–26 Annual Report, C&D rules notice](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/Nitin_Rev_4.pdf); [SWM 2026 rule 5(1)(d)](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/pdf-uploads/pdf_69a16e3b04c107.91022257.pdf). **Limit:** the annual report corroborates commencement; the full signed C&D Gazette was not separately downloaded and line-audited here.
